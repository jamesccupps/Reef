# Innate habitat, movement and behaviour of four added species × 4 modes (Hot Brook lakes and brook)

Compiled 2026-10-16. Species: redbreast sunfish, fallfish, banded killifish and blacknose dace. This note builds on `Hot Brook populations and water/fish_numbers_and_new_species.md` (Q4–Q6: life history, length–weight, growth, fecundity, FB4 surrogates) and `small_fish_numbers.md` (numbers), and does not repeat them. The tables use the format of `habitat3d.md` §1–§12.

**Conventions:** as `habitat3d.md` §0.
- **Zb** is the bottom depth (m) of the chosen column; **hab** is height above the bed; **dbs** is depth below the surface.
- The flags are the same: [inf] my inference; [sur] a surrogate species; [ag] an agency source; [ang] an angler source; [sec] ADW, Ontario LHD, efish, Wikipedia or a similar compilation; [snip] seen only in a search summary or abstract. Unflagged values are primary sources (or a primary source quoted in a report I read in full).
- Horizontal distances are **real metres**.
- **Region tags:** [N] = Maine, New England, NY, Atlantic Canada, Quebec or Ontario; [S] = southern (south of New Jersey), flagged because these species' best-studied populations are southern.
- **Hot Brook settings used below:**
  - **Lakes:** a sandy or rocky shelf at 0–1.3 m along much of the shore; weeds to about 2.5–3 m; mud basins below. August water is 22–25 °C, top to bottom.
  - **Brook:** about 150 m long and 8 m wide; pools about 1–1.2 m deep, riffles about 0.3 m, a run about 0.5 m.
  - **Owner's observations:** sunfish in the brook's 1–1.2 m pools, mostly redbreast (with pumpkinseed), adults about 18–20 cm; "lots" of fallfish.

## Q1. Redbreast sunfish (*Lepomis auritus*): habitat, movement and behaviour by mode

### Takeaway
Redbreast is a stream-and-lake-margin *Lepomis* tied to **hard structure and low current**.
- **Nests:** northern lake nests sit in about **0.4 m** of water on sand or fine gravel, usually within 1 m of a rock or log.
- **Spawning:** late June to early July, as water approaches **20 °C** (New Brunswick).
- **Below 5 °C:** fish gather in deeper, slow water. These winter aggregations form by day and break up at night.
- **Movement:** mostly sedentary. Two-thirds of tagged stream fish moved less than 100 m, but a few travel 4–9 km and come back.
- **Diet:** an insectivore that eats some crayfish and small fish but never becomes piscivorous.
- **Gaps:** no northern telemetry and no thermal preferendum exist. Day and night depths in lakes, group size and O₂ limits are therefore surrogate or inferred.

#### Sources and details
- **Habitat, nests and spawning in New Brunswick lakes; movement; overwintering; diet; predators.**

  - Overwintering: aggregates below 5 °C (Breder & Nigrelli 1935).

### Inferences
- **Hot Brook placement.**
  - Redbreast belongs **in the brook's pools and runs and on the lakes' rocky shelf near the brook mouths**, more than on the soft weedy margins where pumpkinseed live. The HSI ties it to hard structure and low-velocity stream habitat, the Oromocto nests sit beside rocks and logs, and the owner catches it in the pools.
  - Lake fish would hold within about 30 m (real) of shore over sand, gravel and rock with wood.
- **Pumpkinseed vs redbreast split [inf].**
  - Pumpkinseed: weeds, snails, soft bottom.
  - Redbreast: rock and wood, drift and benthic insects, current-tolerant to ~35 cm/s.
  - Both nest at about the same depths, but redbreast starts about 2–4 °C later (~20 °C against 13–17 °C).
- **Activity.** Day and night are roughly equal while feeding (the conflicting diel studies), so use moving day 0.6 / night 0.4. Winter aggregations dissolve at night, so night cohesion → 0.
- **Under ice.** The brook's 1.1-m pool is the obvious overwinter aggregation site for brook fish, if it stays oxygenated. Lake fish winter on the deeper littoral like pumpkinseed.

### Gaps
- No telemetry, home range in hectares, or lake depth distribution for redbreast anywhere in the north.
- No measured preferred temperature, CTmax or O₂ threshold (also noted in the existing note).
- No gape or body-depth equation for redbreast; the congener values stand in.
- Summer group size is unmeasured.
- The Breder & Nigrelli 1935 paper (aggregation sizes and temperatures) was seen only through Hasler 2007 and COSEWIC.
- The MDIFW redbreast page returned **403**; its text was seen only in a search summary.

## Q2. Fallfish (*Semotilus corporalis*): habitat, movement and behaviour by mode

### Takeaway
Fallfish is a pool-and-run stream cyprinid that also lives on northern lake margins.
- **Spawning run:** in Maine, fish **move from larger waters into streams to spawn**.
- **Mounds:** once water reaches **15 °C** (optimum 17 °C), one male builds a pebble mound in **≤ 0.5 m** of water near overhead cover. The mound is usually 30–50 cm across and 10–20 cm high, rarely up to 1.2–2 m. Three to five females and other males spawn on it communally. Nobody guards the eggs, which hatch in about 6 days at 17 °C.
- **Juveniles:** use faster water and smaller streams than adults.
- **Diet:** shifts toward fish, fish eggs and terrestrial insects above about 10 cm.
- **Thermal:** preferred 22.3 °C; seldom in water above 28 °C.
- **Gaps:** no telemetry, home range, lake depth use, winter behaviour or O₂ tolerance was found. Diel feeding is conflicting: one study shows a single evening peak, while ADW claims nocturnal feeding.

#### Sources and details
- **USFWS HSI** (Trial, Wade, Stanley & Nelson 1983, FWS/OBS-82/10.48; Maine Cooperative Fishery Research Unit; full text read, OCR). [N]
  - Adults are usually 155–255 mm (Richardson 1935). Most mature at age IV; males are more often precocious.
  - Diet: "aquatic insect larvae, terrestrial insects, crustaceans, and fish" (Kingsbury 1977); algae important (Reed 1971).
  - Spawning starts after the water reaches 15 °C and "may cease if water temperature drops below 15 °C" (Ross & Reed 1978). Fallfish spawn "in the quiet waters of streams and in the shallow margins of lakes" (Adams & Hankinson 1928).
  - **Maine:** fallfish "move from larger waters into streams to spawn", and "juveniles occur in smaller streams than adults".
  - Nests are built near overhead cover or pools. Mill River nests were midstream or at the edge at ≤ 0.5 m. A single male carries gravel and sand upstream by mouth into a mound and "holds a central position". Spawning "is always elicited by the dominant male, normally by dropping a stone on the nest". "Three to five females and nearby males rush onto the nest and spawn communally. Twenty or more fallfish may be associated with a single nest" (Ross in press).
  - Eggs are adhesive after fertilization. Incubation is usually at 16–18 °C, hatching in 138–144 h at 17 °C (Reed 1971).
  - "Young fish frequent rapid water more than adults". Large adults seek pools and deep runs, and are commonly near cascades and falls. Seldom in water over 28 °C.
  - The model's fry and juvenile depth band is 0.1–0.5 m. There are no adult depth, velocity or cover curves ("unknown"). — [Trial et al. 1983](https://www.arlis.org/docs/vol1/AK_Heritage/FWS/OBS/82/FWS-OBS-82-10-48.pdf)
- **ADW account** [sec]:
  - Habitat: pools at all ages; juveniles in faster currents, adults in slower pools; living depth 0.3–0.5 m (mean 0.35); northern populations use lakes.
  - Nest: 1–4 days of pebble gathering; 30–50 cm wide and 10–20 cm high, up to 2 m across; 1–2 satellite males, more often young males.
  - Spawning above 15 °C; hatch 139–144 h; yolk sac 5–7 days, gone by about 9 mm; mouth functional at 9–11 mm. No parental care; young live in faster currents.
  - Foraging "mainly nocturnal, about 2100–0500" (the same page calls the species diurnal).
  - "Primarily a solitary species". Predators: northern pike, osprey, herring gull (Mendall 1939, Maine), and cannibalism. — [ADW, *Semotilus corporalis*](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Semotilus_corporalis/)
- **Nest size**, agency and naturalist sources:
  - "Nests can be 4 feet wide and 1.5 feet tall" (1.2 × 0.46 m); "doesn't like muddy water"; eats insects, small fish, crayfish and some plants. — [NJ DFW fallfish sheet](https://www.nj.gov/dep/fgw/pdf/delriver/artdel_sp_fallfish.pdf) [ag]
  - "As large as 6 feet in diameter and 3 feet high" (1.8 × 0.9 m); stones average 5 cm; "keel-like crest" across or along the current; built in quiet water or riffles. — [Lake Champlain Committee note](https://lakechamplaincommittee.org/learn/news/item/spring-natural-history-note-fish-spawning) [sec]

### Inferences
- **Hot Brook placement:**
  - In the brook all year, at its densest in late May–June when lake fish run in to spawn.
  - On the lake margins near both brook mouths in summer. "Lots" (owner) fits a population that commutes between the lakes and the brook.
  - Adults use pools and runs; juveniles the riffles and run edges.
- **The spawning migration** is the clearest designed movement. At ≥ 15 °C, mature fish (age ≥ 3–4) from both lakes move into the brook. They spawn over 2–3 weeks, then return.
- **Thermal.** August lake water at 22–25 °C sits at or above the 22.3 °C preference. Adults should favour the brook's pools (shaded, moving water) and the deeper water off the mouths in late summer.
- **Diel.** Make it day-active with an evening feeding peak, the better-sourced primary pattern, and keep some night feeding.

## Q3. Banded killifish (*Fundulus diaphanus*): habitat, movement and behaviour by mode

### Takeaway
Banded killifish is a shallow-shelf schooling topminnow.
- **Habitat:** clear, still water over sand or mud with submerged plants (in Newfoundland, isoetid rosette plants), in **shoals of about 25–40** that sort by size. Some fish move at night into deeper rocky water.
- **Range:** home ranges are 0.3–2 ha (littoral minnows in Minnesota), yet individuals cross a lake in 24 h.
- **Cold:** fish are inactive and uncatchable below about 12 °C; they probably leave the shallows for deeper water in autumn.
- **Spawning:** males are territorial and spawn repeatedly (about 3 clutches) on plants at **19–23 °C** (July in the north). Eggs hang on threads and hatch in 11–12 days at 22–27 °C. There is no parental care.
- **Swimming:** weak.
- **Predators:** it disappears from net catches of lakes with introduced chain pickerel.

### Inferences

- **The bass–shelf interaction** is a good emergent target: killifish should retreat into the shallowest water when a bass or pickerel is near, and enlarge their groups under alarm.
- **Seasonal.**
  - Active only above about 12 °C: mid-May to early October on the Hot Brook calendar.
  - Off the shelf to 1.5–3 m in autumn.
  - Under ice they barely move and do not feed below about 3–4 °C [sur].

## Q4. Blacknose dace (*Rhinichthys atratulus*): habitat, movement and behaviour by mode

### Takeaway
Blacknose dace is a small, day-active, bottom-oriented minnow of clear, shaded, gravelly streams under 8 m wide.
- **Habitat:** it needs both pools and riffles (optimum 50–80 % pools). Adults are densest at surface velocities of **15–45 cm/s** over gravel and cobble, juveniles at 15–30 cm/s, and fry at < 15 cm/s over silt and sand on pool margins.
- **Spawning:** on gravel in riffles **< 25 cm deep at 20–45 cm/s**, at 15.6–22 °C (north: about 21 °C). In the eastern subspecies, males spawn en masse with no territories.
- **Winter:** overwinters in deeper water, moving downstream into bigger water.
- **Lakes:** only rarely.
- **Thermal:** UILT about 29 °C.

  - Thermal and winter: UILT 29.3 °C; overwinters in deep water and migrates into rivers.
  - Embryos at 7–60 cm/s, depth a few cm to 30.5 cm, 15–22 °C. Fry over silt and sand at < 15 cm/s, on "shoals and pool margins". Juveniles over sand and gravel at 15–30 cm/s.
  - HSI model 1: optimum streams **< 8 m wide** with a **1:1 pool–riffle ratio**, turbidity < 30 JTU, sand and gravel, maximum summer temperature 14–24 °C. The variable table assumes 50–80 % pools is optimal. — [Trial et al. 1983b](https://www.arlis.org/docs/vol1/AK_Heritage/FWS/OBS/82/FWS-OBS-82-10-41.pdf)
- **Swimming** (full text read): 32 dace, 41–44 mm SL, from five Maryland sites. ### Inferences
- **Hot Brook placement.** Hot Brook (8 m wide, pools and riffles alternating, gravel) is close to the HSI optimum. Dace belong only in the brook:
  - adults on the riffle–run gravel and the pool heads and tails, under overhanging banks;
  - fry and young on the slack silty margins;
  - in winter, the pools.
  Lake use is accidental: a few fish at the mouths.
- **Thermal.** August outflow from the lakes at 22–25 °C sits at the top of the 19–25 °C preference and within 4–7 °C of the UILT. In the hottest spells, shade and the pools matter.
- **Current.** The 15–45 cm/s figures are surface velocities. A 7-cm dace's 30-min speed is about 27 cm/s, so it holds station in the bed's boundary layer and behind stones, not in mid-current [inf].

## Q5. Encoding summary, speeds, diel transitions, seasonal moves and brook use, and angler realism (rows for `habitat3d.md` §9–§12)

### Takeaway
- **Swimming groups.** The DFO fatigue curves come in six groups. "All subcarangiform swimmers" are lumped as the **salmon–walleye group**, and the cyprinids and killifish are not in the other five groups. Fallfish, dace and killifish therefore take the salmon–walleye curve, and the labriform redbreast takes the catfish–sunfish curve. Killifish's documented weak swimming argues for its lower bound.
- **Behavioural contrasts.**
  - Redbreast: sedentary, structure-bound, slow-water.
  - Fallfish: a lake-to-brook spring spawner, a pool dweller and a partial piscivore.
  - Killifish: a surface-schooling shelf fish that is weak in current.
  - Dace: a day-active riffle forager of the brook only.

### Cited Findings
- **DFO groups:** after review, the database was split into six groups: catfish and sunfish, Clupeidae (herring), eel, pike, salmon and walleye, and sturgeon. "The updated database (Katopodis and Gervais 2016) groups all subcarangiform swimmers together and names them 'Salmon and Walleye Group'". — [DFO Science Advisory Report 2016/001](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/365359.pdf) (full text read). The meeting record that set up the high and low performance groups: [DFO Proceedings 2016/001](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/362441.pdf).
- **Coefficients** (`fish_movement_and_groups.md`, citing [Katopodis & Gervais 2016](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/csas-sccs/Publications/ResDocs-DocRech/2016/2016_002-eng.html)):
  - catfish–sunfish k = 2.176, b = −0.202;
  - salmon–walleye k = 4.004, b = −0.250.
  - Check: these reproduce the note's 15-cm sunfish value (9.2 BL/s at 3 s) and its 20-cm percid value (13.1 BL/s at 3 s).

#### Seasonal moves and brook use (append to `habitat3d.md` §11)

Hot Brook calendar: ice-out about Apr 23; 15 °C by late May; a 24 °C peak in late July; ice-on about Dec 1.

- **Ice-out to mid-May (< 12–15 °C):**
  - Dace are active in the brook; killifish are still inactive (below 12 °C), off the shelf.
  - Redbreast remain in deeper water until the shallows pass 16.7 °C.
- **Late May to mid-June (15–19 °C):**
  - **Fallfish run from both lakes into the brook** (the Maine observation in Trial 1983). Males build mounds on the run and riffle-head gravel ≤ 0.5 m near the pools and overhanging cover. Communal spawning continues while the water stays ≥ 15 °C (optimum 17 °C); a cold snap below 15 °C pauses it. Common shiners, if present, join the mounds.
  - Spent fallfish drift back to the pools and the lakes over 2–3 weeks [inf].
  - Killifish return to the shelf once it passes 12 °C.
  - Redbreast move into 0.15–0.46 m shallows above 16.7 °C.
- **Mid-June to July (≥ 20–21 °C):**
  - Redbreast nest late June to early July (as in NB) on sand and fine gravel by rocks and logs: the brook's slack edges and pool tails, and the lakes' rocky shelf near the mouths [inf].
  - Dace spawn on the riffles once the brook reaches about 21 °C.
  - Killifish spawn repeatedly in shelf plant beds at 19–23 °C through mid-August.
- **Late July to August (22–25 °C):**
  - Fallfish and dace are at or above their preferences: adults favour the shaded brook pools and the water off the mouths.
  - Redbreast and killifish are comfortable.
  - Young dace stay on the slack silty margins; young fallfish in the shallow fast water; young redbreast among the structure and plants of the shallows; young killifish in the shelf plants.
- **September to November:**
  - Killifish leave the shelf for 1.5–3 m as the water falls below about 12 °C.
  - Redbreast aggregate in the deep slow water below 5 °C (lake deep littoral; the brook's 1.1-m pool).
  - Dace and fallfish move from riffles into the pools; some fallfish go back to the lakes [inf].
- **Under ice:**
  - Redbreast hold daytime aggregations that disperse at night.
  - Killifish are quiescent and fast below about 3–4 °C [sur].
  - Dace hold in the brook pools and under banks; fallfish in the pools and the lake [inf].
  - **The brook as an O₂ refuge** (`habitat3d` §11) suits all four in late winter [inf].
- **Between the lakes.** The brook is a corridor that fallfish use deliberately (the spawning run). Redbreast cross only as rare long movers (a few fish travel 4–9 km). Killifish and dace almost never cross: killifish are blocked by current, and dace are brook residents.

#### Angler realism (append to `habitat3d.md` §12)

**Redbreast sunfish**
- The brook's 1–1.2 m pools, by rocks and wood, all summer (owner); the rocky shelf near the brook mouths [inf].
- Typical adults 13–18 cm in Maine (MDIFW [snip]); the owner reports 18–20 cm.
- In late June and early July, males on nests in 0.3–0.6 m beside rocks and logs.

**Fallfish**
- Plentiful in the brook: in the pools and runs all summer, and **concentrated in the brook during the late-May–June spawning run**; at the lake mouths.
- Takes insects and drifting food, including at the surface [inf from the diet].

**Banded killifish and blacknose dace**
- Not angler fish. Killifish are seen as surface schools over the shelf weeds in July–August.
- Dace are seen darting over the gravel of the brook's riffles by day.
