# In-stand structure of soft-water macrophyte beds (2026-10-18)

Companion to `weed_bed_structure.md` §3 (whose own §3 holds only stopgap material), `weed_bed_controls.md` and `macrophytes.md`. A
research helper's report, kept as delivered (condensed lightly). **Tags:** [primary] full text read; [abstract] abstract only (often
rebuilt from OpenAlex); [secondary] from a page or review citing the original; [snippet] search summary only; [inf] the helper's
arithmetic. Blocked and not bypassed: RCIN.pl (a "verifying browser" check), the NCSU repository (a bot check), Wiley/ASLO PDFs, PeerJ,
BHL, the RPI DSpace, limnology-journal.org, DTIC.

## 1. Emergents
**Pontederia cordata (pickerelweed)**
- Biomass, three Connecticut rivers: peak 1,212 g DW/m² (524 above, 688 below ground), 100–150 days after spring growth began;
  overwintered rhizomes and roots 497 g/m² in early June; one season's net production 1,049 g/m²; new below:above ratio 0 in spring →
  1.71 in autumn (Heisey & Damman 1982, *Am. J. Bot.* 69:855–864, doi:10.2307/2442978) [abstract].
- Stems: pickerelweed stands in 10 created and 10 natural Florida marshes (fifteen 0.5-m² quadrats each, 1993): cover 54 % and 55 %,
  157 and 164 stems/m² (Streever, Portier & Crisman 1996, *Wetlands* 16:416–428) [snippet; southern surrogate].
- Height ~1–1.5 m (extension and RHS sheets: 3–5 ft) [snippet]. No northeastern shoot counts or heights found.

**Schoenoplectus validus / tabernaemontani (softstem), S. acutus (hardstem)**
- Nicol, Gehrig & Frahn 2014 (SARDI Report 794, Lakes Alexandrina and Albert, S. Australia — "S. validus" there = S. tabernaemontani)
  [primary]: stems in 1×1-m quadrats in autumn — natural stands ~10, 63 and 105 stems/m² at three sites (read off Fig. 7, ±5); planted
  20–142, rising ~7 stems/m² a year of stand age; tallest stem a quadrat 168–226 cm planted, ~124–238 natural; mean stem 87–193 cm;
  natural stands to 95 cm of water and > 20 m wide; "does not form dense monospecific stands".
- Hardstem 1–4 m tall, softstem to 3 m, both in water to 1.5 m (Washington Ecology) [snippet]; Saskatchewan delta (Dabbs 1971, via a
  1983 Clear Lake, Iowa report): softstem in 43 cm of water on average, hardstem 109 cm; Clear Lake softstem 10–170 cm [snippet].
- Above-ground biomass best predicted by culm density × mean culm height (Daniels, Cade & Sartoris 2010, *Wetlands* 30:231) [abstract].
- No northeastern culm densities.

**Schoenoplectus subterminalis (swaying bulrush)** — Lawrence Lake, Michigan (hard-water marl: surrogate; Rich, Wetzel & Thuy 1971,
*Freshw. Biol.* 1:3–21) [abstract]: nearly pure stands at 1–6 m (occurs 1–7 m), up to 200 g ash-free DW/m² annual mean; green under
the ice (≤ 150 g/m² in winter); 79 % of the lake's macrophyte biomass; the 2-m population peaked in autumn, the 4-m in summer, lowest
in June. Form: submersed mats of thin flaccid leaves, only the flowering culm (~1 m) reaching the surface [snippet]. No stem density.

## 2. Floating-leaved plants
**Brasenia schreberi (watershield)** — Kunii 1999 (*Jpn. J. Limnol.* 60:281–289; a dense pure stand in a Japanese irrigation pond, a
50×50-cm permanent quadrat, three years) [primary]: maximum LAI 0.90–1.36; maximum leaf mass 52.5–80.5 g DW/m², peaking late August –
early September; annual leaf production 154.9–243.5 g/m²; leaves live 24.8–28.3 days, turn over 6.0–6.3×/yr; production:peak biomass
2.95–3.25/yr; new leaves late May – late October; A (cm²) = 0.767·L·W + 0.386; DW (mg) = 5.678·A + 8.425; insects took 10–30 % of
leaf area in early summer, < 10 % in August. Leaves a season 277, 247, 159 in the quadrat → ~640–1,110 leaves/m²/yr; mean leaf ~0.22 g
(~37 cm²) → **peak standing ~370 leaves/m²** [inf; consistent with LAI 1.36].
- Beihai Wetland, China: continuous stands 100 % cover at 0.5–2 m, sporadic deeper than 1.5 m, ~70 % elsewhere [primary, no counts].
  Petioles to 2 m; leaves 4–12 × 3–8 cm [snippet].
- Pond B, Savannah River (soft-water reservoir): three floating-leaved species 50–60 % of the plants' standing crop; leaf + petiole
  turnover ~2 %/day *Nymphaea odorata*, 3 % *Brasenia*, 5 % *Nymphoides cordata* (5.9, 7.4, 14.7×/yr) (Kelly 1988, OSTI) [abstract].

**Nymphaea odorata, Nuphar variegata / advena** — no North American counts of leaves or petioles per m², nor LAI. Mesocosms at 30, 60,
90 cm: shallow plants make more, smaller, shorter-lived leaves; deep ones fewer, larger, more biomass in leaves and roots (Richards et
al. 2011, *Aquat. Bot.*) [abstract]. *N. variegata* leaves at 35 cm used ~50 % less biomass per leaf than at 70 cm; floating leaves
~70 % of leaves at one depth vs 23–43 % at the other [abstract, garbled]. *N. lutea*'s submerged leaves persist all summer and are a
substantial share of biomass (Schoelynck et al. 2014) [abstract]. Floating-leaf LAI rarely exceeds ~2; leaves live 13–35 days
(Tsuchiya 1991, *Vegetatio* 97:149–160) [snippet]. *N. alba* above-ground 143–339 g DW/m² over nine years (Paillisson & Marion 2006)
[snippet]. Rough count: LAI 0.8–1.2 with 200–400-cm² pads → ~25–60 leaves/m² [inf].

**Potamogeton epihydrus** (Connecticut rivers): peak 94 g/m² (81 above, 14 below) in 45–85 days; rhizomes 11 g/m² in late May;
below:above 0.37 late spring, 0.20 midsummer, 0.41 autumn (Heisey & Damman 1982) [abstract]. *P. natans* not found. Comparator:
*Nymphoides peltata*, Lake Kasumigaura — peak 47.6 g/m² late September, leaves live 28 days, annual production 182 g/m² [abstract].

## 3. Broad-leaved pondweeds
**Potamogeton robbinsii**: "maximum densities can reach 1000 plants per square meter" in 5–7 m of water (Go Botany, Native Plant Trust,
no primary source) [secondary]; sterile stems 10–90 cm, flowering to 2 m [secondary]; plant 20–40 in, depths 2–20+ ft [snippet];
carpets soft substrates; shade tolerant (Madsen, Hartleb & Boylen 1991) [abstract]; persisted at reduced abundance under a dense milfoil
canopy in Lake George (species per quadrat 5.5 → 2.2, 1987–89; Madsen et al. 1991, *JAPM* 29:94–99) [primary]. Lake Opinicon, Ontario
(Crowder et al. 1977): mean organic biomass in 25×25-cm diver quadrats **248 g/m² (1–1,154)**, falling with depth [abstract; mixed beds,
harder-water mesotrophic lake]. Lake George's diver index spans 0.01–5,000 growing points/m² ≈ 0.01–200 g/m² (Sheldon & Boylen 1978)
[abstract]. No in-bed biomass or mat height for *P. robbinsii* itself.
**Potamogeton amplifolius**: no shoots/m², height or bed biomass found; transplants in Chautauqua Lake grew 0.1–0.9 cm/day [abstract].

## 4. Bladderworts
- *U. purpurea*, Mirror Lake, NH (Moeller 1980, *Freshw. Biol.* 10:391–400) [abstract]: 0–6.5 m; growing season ~8 weeks at 6 m vs
  17 at 2 m; 60–75 % less annual biomass at 6 m; peak mid-August; overwintering tissue 52 % of the maximum; production:biomass 1.0.
  Bladders 26 % of plant biomass (Richards 2001) [abstract].
- *U. australis* (Adamec & Kovářová 2006, *Folia Geobot.* 41:395–406; Czech dystrophic pools 15–39 cm deep) [primary]: **4–40 shoot
  apices/m²**, covering 10–25 % of microsites; shoots usually 0.4–1 m; field shoots 30–38 cm with 40–54 whorls, 0.12–0.16 g DW; 2.2–3.6
  whorls a day, biomass doubling in 9–33 days → in-stand ~0.6–6 g DW/m² [inf].
- *U. vulgaris* (Friday 1989): 1.4–2.8 new leaf groups a day April–September; leaves live < 50 days [abstract].
- *U. intermedia* / *stygia* dense stands (Třeboň; Adamec): total 2.4–97.0 g DW/m², traps 18–29 % of biomass [abstract].
- Everglades *Utricularia*–periphyton mat ~364 g/m² (subtropical, mixed) [snippet]. Mat thickness and shoot length per m² not found.

## 5. Isoetid rosette lawns
- *Isoetes echinospora*, Baciver pool, Pyrenees (Chappuis, Ballesteros & Gacia 2016, *Limnetica* 35:373–384) [primary]: very soft water
  (17–150 µeq/L), 50 cm deep; **1,242–2,663 plants/m² (mean 1,949)**; 16.6 leaves a plant a year, the largest 0.002 g DW; leaves turn
  over 1.5–1.6×/yr; leaf production 38.7–49.3 g DW/m²/yr, all leaves dead by November; *I. lacustris* at 0.8 m nearby 75 g/m²/yr,
  30–50 % below ground. Isoetids' annual production 5–425 g DW/m² (Madsen et al. 2002 review, as cited).
- Lake Baciver communities (Ballesteros, Gacia & Camarero 1989) [abstract]: *I. lacustris* 120–460 g DW/m²; *Sparganium angustifolium*
  160; lake mean 140; *Nitella gracilis* garbled (a snippet: 11 g/m²).
- **Polish soft-water lakes, clear vs humic** (Szmeja & Bociąg 2004, *Acta Soc. Bot. Pol.* 73:165–173) [abstract]: biomass per m² of
  population area, 12 clear → 13 humic lakes: *Lobelia* 8.4 → 6.2 g DW/m²; *Fontinalis antipyretica* 4.6 → 0.01; *Isoetes lacustris*
  85.4 → < 0.001; *Sphagnum denticulatum* 39.3 → 7.2; plants lost first from the deep middle of each population — **in humic water
  Isoetes collapses while Lobelia persists**.
- *Lobelia dortmanna*: 1–30 individuals/m² (Étang du Bel-Air, France, July 2001) [snippet]; "not very gregarious", separate rosettes
  (BSBI) [snippet]; experiments at 64, 256, 1,024 plants/m² [abstract]; 25 Polish lakes: 0.1–2.2 m (median 0.8), **4.8 % of the
  littoral** on average [abstract].
- *Eriocaulon* and *Lobelia*: leaf area : sediment area 1:0.4 – 1:3.8 (LAI ~0.26–2.5) in four soft-water lakes (Howell 1990, *Can. J.
  Bot.* 68:1903) [abstract]. *Eriocaulon*: dense turfs to 2.6 m; leaves 2–10 cm × 2–5 mm [snippet]. *Isoetes macrospora*, Weber Lake,
  WI: ~117 g/m² [snippet + inf]. No natural *Lobelia* lawn densities from northeastern North America.

## 6. Nitella and charophytes
Lawrence Lake (hard water): *Nitella* at 7 m July–October; *Chara* to 100 g/m² at shallow sheltered sites (Rich et al. 1971)
[abstract]. Low-P lakes (TP 9–22 µg/L): charophytes 0.1–21 g DW/m² (doi:10.1111/fwb.14026) [abstract]. Hard-water contrast: Spanish
karst lagoon meadows 220–2,270 g/m² [abstract]. *Nitella flexilis* biomass or height not found; Stross 1979 (Lake George *Nitella*
meadow) not accessed.

## 7. Stream mosses (Fontinalis)
Hubbard Brook, NH (Steele et al. 2024, *Freshwater Sci.* 43:439–452) [abstract]: moss cover 4–40 % across streams (2019), 2022 medians
0–41.4 %; mats 14–414 g organic matter per m² of stream (live moss + trapped particles). *Fontinalis neomexicana*, Colorado: 28.47
mg/cm² ≈ **285 g/m² inside the patch** (Rader & Belish 1997) [abstract + inf]. Pyrenees (Dawson 1973) [abstract]: *F. squamosa*
production ≥ 92 g/m²/yr; whole-stream moss 325 g/m² at the high-cover site, the tributary 7 % cover; patches to 682 (Fontinalis +
Fissidens), 1,960 (*Cratoneuron*). Fox Pond inlet, Plymouth NH: *F. novae-angliae* dominates submerged rocks (Glime 1969) [snippet].
