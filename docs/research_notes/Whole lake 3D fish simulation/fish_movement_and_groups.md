# Fish movement, space use and grouping in small north-temperate lakes (for a whole-lake 3-D agent simulation of the Hot Brook lakes)

Scope: horizontal movement, home ranges, homing, daily distances, swimming speeds, diel and seasonal horizontal shifts, lake–stream–lake movement, and group (school/shoal) structure for the 12 Hot Brook species. Seasonal **depth** and habitat use is already covered in `docs/research_notes/Seasonal fish habitat use/` (`perch_pickerel_smallmouth_pumpkinseed.md`, `whiteperch_shiner_sucker_bullhead.md`). Where those notes already hold a number (Opeongo smallmouth ranges, Lake Madison perch speeds, Turkey Lake sucker ranges, Anacostia bullhead ranges, perch school sizes), it is only cross-referenced here, not repeated in detail.

Target system for scale: Upper Hot Brook Lake 360 ha (mean 3.35 m, max 5.5 m), Lower Hot Brook Lake 277 ha (max 4.0 m), joined by a 154-m brook with pools; unstratified, weedy, tea-coloured, ice Dec–Apr.

Tags used below: [primary, full text read], [primary abstract], [via search summary] (a search-engine digest of the source; not read by me), [secondary], [agency], [derived] (my arithmetic). "Southern", "estuarine", "European" or "reservoir" flags mark data from outside the preferred region.

## 1. Home range, site fidelity, homing, daily distance and swimming speed

### Takeaway
Space use splits into three classes. (a) **Sedentary shore-anchored fish** — pumpkinseed, redbreast, killifish, dace — that live in areas of tens to hundreds of metres of shoreline (0.2–1 ha) and home after displacement, but with a mobile minority (≈ half of tagged redbreast moved ≥ 160 m; a Great Plains bluegill population in a 332-ha lake roamed ranges up to 172 ha). (b) **Mid-range benthic/schooling fish** — white sucker, brown bullhead, yellow perch, white perch — with 5–20 ha ranges and 1–3 km/day of travel. (c) **Large-range predators** — smallmouth bass (100–400 ha, 1–9 km/day in big lakes; strongly site-faithful to nests, wintering sites and summer ranges) and chain pickerel (whole-lake use in fall, contracted to a few island/shore areas in winter; pike surrogates return to their summer activity centre within 6 days of displacement). Net displacement badly underestimates actual swimming: an activity-telemetry smallmouth swam ~27 km/day while netting only 77 m/day.

### Cited Findings

#### Sunfishes (pumpkinseed, bluegill as surrogate, redbreast)
- **Pumpkinseed home range** is given as "around 0.23 to 1.12 hectares"; males nest "in colonies of 3 to 15 nest sites"; pumpkinseed are "active during the day and rest at night" [secondary; ADW gives no source for the range figure] — [ADW, *Lepomis gibbosus*](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lepomis_gibbosus/)
- **Pumpkinseed displacement, PIT mark–recapture** (Emerald Lake, a deep former chalk pit, NW Poland; introduced population; 359 PIT-tagged, recaptured 2–674 days later by 19 littoral electrofishing runs, May 2012–Sep 2014) [primary abstract; European flag]:
  - 43 fish recaptured (56 detections); **39% of detections were at the place of the previous detection**.
  - Mean displacement **43 m (max 156 m)**; males mean 60 m (17–119 m), females 44 m (0–148 m).
  - Relocations: autumn 49%, summer 36%, spring 15%.
  - Fish favoured muddy, shallow shelves overgrown with reed, bulrush and horsetail.
  — [Zięba et al. 2015, XV European Congress of Ichthyology abstract](https://www.frontiersin.org/10.3389/conf.fmars.2015.03.00123/2930/XV_European_Congress_of_Ichthyology/all_events/event_abstract)
- **Homing of displaced sunfish** (Clear Lake, Iowa, summer 1964; pumpkinseed, bluegill, green sunfish marked and moved) [primary abstract]:
  - Recapture rates were higher for fish returned to their home area than for displaced fish; homing did not correlate with displacement distance, which did not exceed **2,200 ft (≈ 670 m [derived])**.
  - Larger fish showed a greater homing tendency; green sunfish oriented and returned more precisely than pumpkinseed and bluegill.
  - Home ranges "apparently maintained only for relatively short periods".
  — [Kudrna 1965, Proc. Iowa Acad. Sci. 72:263–271](https://scholarworks.uni.edu/pias/vol72/iss1/40)
- **Bluegill radio telemetry in a 332-ha shallow lake** (the size and shallowness are close to Upper Hot Brook Lake) [primary abstract]:
  - 78 bluegill (200–273 mm TL) radio-tagged Mar/May 2000; 20 fish located every 2 h over one 24-h period per month Apr–Sep, plus daily fixes on six consecutive days each month.
  - **Home ranges up to 172 ha**; about **half the fish showed site fidelity in any month**; many roamed the whole lake.
  - Movement peaked in midsummer; **little diel difference** in movement; not strongly linked to temperature, pressure or wind.
  — [Paukert, Willis & Bouchard 2004, NAJFM 24:154–161 (USGS record)](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/movement-home-range-and-site-fidelity-bluegills-a-great-plains-lake)
  - A magazine account identifies the lake as Pelican Lake, Nebraska (~820 acres, average depth under 4 ft) and reports an average bluegill movement of **62 yards per hour (≈ 57 m/h [derived])**, varying greatly among fish [secondary; via search summary] — [In-Fisherman, "Tracking bluegill research"](https://www.in-fisherman.com/editorial/tracking-bluegill-research/156771)
- **Radio telemetry of four species in an Illinois lake** (home range, "primary home range" and swimming speeds of largemouth bass, bluegill, yellow perch, pumpkinseed): the thesis exists but its numbers could not be read (PDF blocked) [title only] — [Fish 1982, Loyola Univ. Chicago M.S. thesis](https://ecommons.luc.edu/luc_theses/3310); published as Fish & Savitz 1983, TAFS 112:147–153 [citation via search summary].
- **Redbreast sunfish tag–recapture, Little River, North Carolina** (stream 12 m wide, mean depth 0.9 m, pools to 3.4 m; 481 tagged, 10.0–21.5 cm TL, mean 16.2 cm) [primary, full text read; southern flag]:
  - 189 recaptures (39.3%) of 142 individuals; **about 51% (72) were recaptured at least once ≥ 0.16 km from the tagging site**; the authors class the species as "moderately mobile".
  - **20 of 21 fish tagged in April–May and recaptured within 60 days had moved upstream** (spring upstream drift).
  - Maximum distance **6.1 km** (three fish); two fish moved 2.6 and 2.9 km upstream to a dam.
  - Males and females moved about equally far.
  — [Hudson & Hester 1975, Proc. SE Assoc. Game & Fish Comm., PDF](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/HUDSON-325.pdf); [abstract page](https://d11.seafwa.org/journal/1975/movements-ot-redbreast-sunfish-little-river-near-raleigh-north-carolina)
- **Juvenile redbreast in a large Coastal Plain stream** (paint marks, 550-m reach): most recaptures within **33 m** of the capture site over 18 months; three *Lepomis* moved ≥ 100 m (max 200 m) [via search summary; southern flag] — [USGS record, "Movements by two small fishes in a large stream"](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/5223350)
- **Green sunfish (relative), Ohio stream**: 56% of control recaptures in the same or adjacent 20-m section; about 3.5% moved ≥ 1,000 m [via search summary] — [J. Freshwater Ecol. 2007 record](https://digitalcommons.owu.edu/zool_pubs/73)

#### Yellow perch, white perch
- Yellow perch movement rates, offshore distances and school sizes are already in the repo notes: Lake Madison telemetry **92.6 m/h by day vs 27.6 m/h at night**; autumn the most mobile season; summer mean 344 m offshore (12.6–874 m) — see `Seasonal fish habitat use/perch_pickerel_smallmouth_pumpkinseed.md` (sources: [Radabaugh thesis](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/etd/367/); [Bauer et al. 2009](https://academic.oup.com/najfm/article-abstract/29/1/64/7847055)).
- **White perch, estuary acoustic telemetry** (Carmans River / Great South Bay, NY; 40 tagged Aug 2010–2011, 15 gave ≥ 28 days of data): spatial use expanded in autumn and spring and contracted in summer and winter; some fish stayed put while others dispersed; activity peaked before winter and at spring spawning [via search summary; estuarine flag] — [AFS 2012 abstract](https://afs.confex.com/afs/2012/webprogram/Paper10211.html); [Stony Brook record](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/diel-behavior-in-white-perch-revealed-using-acoustic-telemetry/)
- White perch summer movements are "local and random", rarely more than 19 km (estuarine studies) — already in repo notes, from [Stanley & Danie 1983](https://njscuba.net/wp-content/uploads/biology/pdf/usfws_white_perch_0199.pdf).

#### White sucker, brown bullhead
- **White sucker in a 52-ha boreal lake** (Turkey Lake, ON; depth tags): 95% kernel home range 18.4 ha (spring) and 14.6 ha (fall); seasonal ranges overlapped 74–82%; at most ~35% of the lake used; horizontal travel **2.1–3.16 km/day (mean 2.6)** — already in repo notes, from [Richter, Smokorowski & Blanchfield 2024, Environ. Biol. Fish.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10641-024-01581-8)
- **White sucker homing / spawning-site fidelity**:
  - Homing of spawning white suckers was studied in Many Point Lake, Minnesota (Olson & Scidmore 1963, TAFS 92:13–16) and the homing mechanism in Wolf Lake, New York (Werner 1979); white suckers "are thought to have a strong tendency to home to a spawning stream" [via search summary of an MSU thesis citing them] — [MSU thesis](https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/25060/FULL_TEXT/view)
  - Acoustic telemetry (Sturgeon Bay and Green Bay tributaries, Lake Michigan; 60 + 29 adults tagged 2022): high spawning-site fidelity; fish that returned to the same creek were not all associated with each other outside the spawning season [agency/NGO project page; via search summary] — [Shedd Aquarium sucker project](https://www.sheddaquarium.org/care-and-conservation/shedd-research/investigating-great-lakes-sucker-migrations)
- **Brown bullhead**: summer 95% polygon 4.5 ha and linear range 0.50 km (stayed within 500 m of release); spring 19.7 ha / 2.1 km (moved ~1 km upstream as water warmed); fall–winter 15.9 ha / 2.1 km; 5.8 ha in an impoundment; resident all year — already in repo notes, from [Sakaris & Jesien 2005, TAFS 134:1262](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1577/T04-086.1). Adult site fidelity in Presque Isle Bay, Lake Erie (n = 49) — [USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70035937)

#### Smallmouth bass
- Large-lake ranges (Opeongo: 100–400 ha, 1–9 km/day, mean 483 m/h, max 1,120 m/h; 15 of 18 displaced fish returned home) are in the repo notes — see `perch_pickerel_smallmouth_pumpkinseed.md` (sources: [Suski & Ridgway 2009](https://www.harkness.ca/PDFs/Contributions%202000s/Suski%20and%20Ridgway%20J%20Zoology%202009.pdf); [Ridgway 2002](https://www.harkness.ca/PDFs/Contributions%202000s/Ridgway%202002%20Smallmouth%20Bass%20the%20Jouvenile%20Transition%20Hypothesis.pdf); [Rupnik 2018](https://queensu.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/91766d5f-8b10-4d78-8a8f-790cdb962fbe/content)).
- **Net displacement vs actual swimming, confined lentic site** (intake forebay on the north shore of Lake Erie, longest linear distance 675 m; summer 1998, ~18 °C; fish mean 312 mm TL) [primary, full text read]:
  - Mark–recapture: mean daily movement **47.5 ± 12.5 m** (median 17.9 m).
  - Fixed radio telemetry: mean gross movement 3,018.8 m over 36.8 days; mean daily movement **77.1 ± 10.6 m**.
  - EMG activity telemetry calibrated in a respirometer (n = 4, 15 days): estimated mean daily swimming distance **27,408 ± 4,085 m**, more than 100× the locational estimates. No correlation with length or weight.
  - The authors note the "restricted movement" notion from early mark–recapture work was partly a sampling artefact.
  — [Cooke, Bunt, Schreer & Wahl 2001, J. Fish Biol. 58:573–587](https://www.fecpl.ca/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/EMG_Comp_Move_JFB.pdf)
  - 27.4 km/day = **0.32 m/s mean swimming speed ≈ 1.0 body length/s** for a 31-cm fish [derived].
- **Nest-site fidelity** (Millers Lake, a 45-ha widening of the Mississippi River, ON): nearly half of males that nested in consecutive years nested **within 20 m of their previous nest**; most stayed loyal to either the river or the lake [via search summary] — [QUBS project page](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/divergent-life-histories-among-smallmouth-bass-inhabiting-a-connected-river-lake-system)
- **Nearshore Lake Michigan harbour**: smallmouth home ranges 142.6 ha vs 12.9 ha for largemouth (26 fish, radio and acoustic) [via search summary; Great Lakes flag] — [Illinois experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/movement-patterns-of-smallmouth-and-largemouth-bass-in-and-around/)
- **River contrast** (Upper Mississippi, MN): summer home ranges 0.4–19.0 ha, winter 0.2–20.2 ha [via search summary; river flag] — [MN DNR report PDF](https://files.dnr.state.mn.us/areas/fisheries/saukrapids/mississippiriversmbtelemetry.pdf)

#### Chain pickerel (and pike as a surrogate)
- No chain pickerel home-range area (ha) was found in any source (see Gaps). Horizontal patterns from the only fine-scale positioning study are in §5.
- **Pike in a 25-ha lake** (Kleiner Döllnsee, NE Germany; 12 female pike 450–733 mm; positions every 3 h through 24-h cycles weekly Jun–Dec 2005 and on the ice Jan–Feb 2006) [via search summary of the abstract]:
  - **All 8 fish translocated away from their summer activity centres returned within 6 days**; return time did not depend on length or translocation distance.
  - Winter activity centres were larger than summer ones but overlapped them considerably (attributed to collapse of vegetation structure or prey distribution).
  — [Kobler, Klefoth & Arlinghaus 2008, J. Fish Biol. 73:584–596 (author PDF link)](https://www.forland.hu-berlin.de/de/institut/departments/dntw/jp_bfm/publ_html/Kobler_Klefoth_Arlinghaus2008.pdf)
- Pike (Belgium, radio telemetry): pre-spawning winter movements were mostly between distinct holding areas 40–550 m apart, contrary to the "sedentary pike" view [via search summary; European flag] — [ORBi record](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/6137)

#### Small cyprinids and killifish
- **Blacknose dace** reintroduced to Mountain Run, Virginia (marked at three sites in a 1.45-km reach): dispersed **up to 680 m downstream within one month**; only 34 recaptures in 2009 [via search summary; southern, relocated fish] — [J. Appalachian Studies 21(1):49](https://scholarlypublishingcollective.org/uip/jas/article/21/1/49/225708/Dispersal-and-Reproduction-of-Relocated-Blacknose)
- **Golden shiner** are "known to travel widely throughout the lakes they inhabit (Scott and Crossman, 1973)" and to make regular daily migrations (Hall et al. 1979) [primary, full text read, citing them] — [Reebs 1996, Behav. Processes 36:253–262](https://www.umoncton.ca/sites/prof.prod.umoncton.ca/files/users/user109/time-place%20learning%20golden%20shiners.pdf)
- **Fallfish**: pools are the preferred habitat at all ages; young often in faster currents, older fish in slower pools; northern populations also live in lakes [secondary] — [ADW, *Semotilus corporalis*](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Semotilus_corporalis); adults in gravel/rubble pools and runs of small–medium rivers "and also lake margins" [secondary] — [FishBase](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/2953)

#### Swimming speeds (laboratory fatigue curves, by group)
- The DFO database fits fatigue curves in dimensionless form, **U\* = k·t\*^b**, with U\* = U/√(gL) and t\* = t/√(L/g) (U swim speed, L fish length, t time to fatigue). Group coefficients [primary, full text read] — [Katopodis & Gervais 2016, DFO CSAS Res. Doc. 2016/002](https://www.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/csas-sccs/Publications/ResDocs-DocRech/2016/2016_002-eng.html) ([PDF](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/Library/362248.pdf)):
  - Catfish and Sunfish Group (Centrarchidae incl. smallmouth; ictalurids): k = 2.176, b = −0.202 (n = 1,282; "limited data on burst range").
  - Salmon and Walleye Group (includes Percidae and Moronidae, i.e. yellow perch and white perch, and a "Walleye and White Sucker" subgroup): k = 4.004, b = −0.250 (n = 17,085).
  - Pike Group (derived): k = 3.811, b = −0.329 — "all points in low prolonged range"; burst end assumed equal to the Salmon/Walleye and Eel groups.
  - Speed classes used: sustained > 200 min, prolonged 20 s–200 min, burst ≤ 20 s.
- **Speeds computed from those equations** (mean fish; 50% of fish do better) [derived]:

  | Group, length | 3 s burst | 10 s | 60 s | 30 min | 200 min (≈ sustained) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Sunfish/catfish, 15 cm | 1.39 m/s (9.2 BL/s) | 1.09 (7.2) | 0.76 (5.0) | 0.38 (2.5) | 0.26 (1.7) |
  | Sunfish/catfish, 30 cm (bass) | 2.10 (7.0) | 1.65 (5.5) | 1.15 (3.8) | 0.58 (1.9) | 0.39 (1.3) |
  | Perch/white perch/sucker, 20 cm | 2.62 (13.1) | 1.94 (9.7) | 1.24 (6.2) | 0.53 (2.6) | 0.33 (1.6) |
  | Perch/white perch/sucker, 40 cm | 4.04 (10.1) | 2.99 (7.5) | 1.91 (4.8) | 0.82 (2.0) | 0.51 (1.3) |
  | Pike group, 45 cm (big pickerel) | 3.36 (7.5) | 2.26 (5.0) | 1.25 (2.8) | 0.41 (0.9) | 0.22 (0.5) |

- Voluntary raceway ascents by smallmouth reached speeds "considerably greater than Ucrit" that were held for at least 2 min (Peake & Farrell 2004, 2005), so Ucrit underestimates real burst/prolonged capacity [primary, full text read, citing them] — [Katopodis & Gervais 2016](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/Library/362248.pdf). A search digest gives the raceway steady-gait maximum as 53.6–97.3 cm/s, scaling with length [via search summary].

### Inferences
- **Scale against the Hot Brook lakes.** A 360-ha lake holds roughly 300–1,500 pumpkinseed-sized home ranges but only one to three smallmouth-sized ranges (Opeongo values). Bluegill in a lake of nearly the same area and shallowness (Pelican Lake) roamed up to 172 ha with only half the fish site-faithful per month, so a strict "0.2–1 ha" sunfish anchor would be too rigid for a shallow 300-ha lake; a two-type mix (resident ≈ 50–60%, roamer ≈ 40–50%) fits both the Polish/Iowa homing data and the Pelican Lake telemetry.
- **Proposed per-species movement parameters for agents** (all inferred from the findings above; tune in the sim):

  | Species (agent) | Activity space, open water | Net travel | Site fidelity / homing | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Pumpkinseed (individual or loose group) | home spot ± 40–60 m of shore (0.2–1 ha); 40% "roamers" with 10–100+ ha | ~50 m/h when roaming (bluegill) | returns from ≤ 670 m; bigger fish home better | displacement highest in autumn |
  | Redbreast (individual) | stream/brook pool ± 30 m; half of adults make ≥ 160 m moves | spring upstream drift | weak | treat brook pools as home |
  | Yellow perch (school agent) | 10s–100s of ha; summer ~350 m offshore by day | 93 m/h day, 28 m/h night | — | autumn most mobile |
  | White perch (school agent) | whole-basin roaming schools | (no lake value; use perch ×1–2) | low | |
  | White sucker (group or individual) | 15–20 ha; ≤ ~35% of a 52-ha lake | 2–3 km/day | high (spawning stream) | big fish commute between basins (§3) |
  | Brown bullhead (individual) | 4.5 ha summer; 15–20 ha spring/fall | — | resident | night-active |
  | Smallmouth (individual) | 100–400 ha in big lakes; in a 300-ha lake expect a large share of the shoreline | 1–9 km/day net; ~27 km/day swum | nest within 20 m; wintering site | |
  | Chain pickerel (individual) | whole lake in fall, a few island/shore patches in winter | — | pike: home within 6 days | |
  | Golden shiner (school agent) | whole lake ("travel widely") | — | none | daily littoral↔open-water commute |
  | Killifish, dace (school/aggregate) | littoral strip / riffle reach, 10s–100s of m | dace ≤ 680 m/month | — | |
- **Cruise vs burst.** A sim fish can cruise at about 0.5–1.5 BL/s (field EMG smallmouth ≈ 1 BL/s; DFO 200-min speeds 1.3–1.8 BL/s are the ceiling), sprint at 5–13 BL/s for ≤ 10 s, and should fatigue on the DFO curve between. Pickerel (pike group) have the steepest curve: the fastest short bursts relative to their sustained speed (b = −0.33), which suits ambush.
- **Net path ≪ swum path.** Locational studies underestimate swimming by ~100× (Cooke et al.), so a sim tuned to match net displacement must still spend most energy on local wandering, foraging turns and holding position.

### Gaps
- No chain pickerel, white perch (lake), golden shiner, fallfish, banded killifish or blacknose dace home-range area from a north-temperate lake was found. The George Lake (Kejimkujik) pickerel telemetry has no published numbers yet.
- The Fish (1982)/Fish & Savitz (1983) four-species telemetry values (home range, swimming speeds) could not be read; the thesis PDF returned 403.
- A grass pickerel tag–recapture study in Ontario (most fish within 500 m; 16% moved > 500 m; max 13.5 km; mobile fish larger) and a white perch study in York River tributaries, Virginia (16 fish; mean home range 0.11 km²; site fidelity; utilisation ≤ 10% of the range) appeared only in search digests; the papers could not be opened to confirm the attribution or numbers. Candidate records: [MDPI Fishes 4(3):43](https://www.mdpi.com/2410-3888/4/3/43) (grass pickerel, unconfirmed); [TAFS doi 10.1577/T08-176.1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1577/T08-176.1) (white perch, unconfirmed).
- The DFO group membership of cyprinids (golden shiner, fallfish, dace) and killifish was not determined from the table layout; swimming curves for those species are therefore not given.
- No fast-start (strike) velocity or acceleration for chain pickerel was retrieved.

## 2. Diel horizontal movements (littoral–pelagic, inshore–offshore)

### Takeaway
Most species have a documented day–night shift in *where* they are, mostly via light: visual schoolers (yellow perch, golden shiner, white perch) hold one zone by day and move at twilight; benthic and nocturnal fish (bullhead, sucker) come inshore or shallow at night. Directions differ by species: golden shiner are littoral by day and in open water at dawn and dusk; white perch and yellow perch are offshore/deeper by day and inshore at night; bluegill in a shallow plains lake showed little diel difference.

### Cited Findings
- **Golden shiner** (Hall et al. 1979, as summarised by Reebs): golden shiners "living in lakes feed in open waters at dawn, in the littoral zone during the day, and back in open waters at dusk"; Reebs concludes they may need to distinguish only two places, open water and littoral [primary, full text read, citing Hall et al.] — [Reebs 1996](https://www.umoncton.ca/sites/prof.prod.umoncton.ca/files/users/user109/time-place%20learning%20golden%20shiners.pdf)
  - A species account (citing Hall et al. 1979): schools gather in the shallows by day and break apart as they move to open water shortly after sunset [secondary] — [Fishes of Texas](https://fishesoftexas.org/taxa/notemigonus-crysoleucas)
  - Golden shiners learned in the lab to be at one feeding place in the morning and another in the afternoon (time–place learning via a circadian clock, re-entrained by 6 h in about 3 days) [primary, full text read] — [Reebs 1996](https://www.umoncton.ca/sites/prof.prod.umoncton.ca/files/users/user109/time-place%20learning%20golden%20shiners.pdf)
- **Juvenile bluegill** make diel littoral–pelagic movements in a small lake (Shoup, Boswell & Wahl 2014, TAFS 143:796–801; keywords: vertical migration, predation risk, largemouth bass) [title and keywords only; numbers not seen] — [FIU record](https://discovery.fiu.edu/display/pub120152)
- **Adult bluegill, 332-ha shallow lake**: movement was similar across the 24-h period [primary abstract] — [Paukert et al. 2004](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/movement-home-range-and-site-fidelity-bluegills-a-great-plains-lake)
- **Pumpkinseed** are "active during the day and rest at night", heaviest feeding in the afternoon [secondary] — [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lepomis_gibbosus/)
- **White perch, estuary acoustic telemetry**: adults moved upstream and downstream daily; the frequency of diel movement was 13.8% in winter and 49.4–57.5% in summer; nightly downstream movements preferred in winter [primary abstract; already in repo notes; estuarine flag] — [TAFS 143(5), 2014](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00028487.2014.938193)
- **White perch, lakes**: shallow water (0.9–1.2 m) at night, deeper water (4.0–9.0 m) in daylight [agency synthesis; already in repo notes] — [Stanley & Danie 1983](https://njscuba.net/wp-content/uploads/biology/pdf/usfws_white_perch_0199.pdf)
- **Yellow perch**: day schools offshore; at dusk "dense perch schools migrated inshore close to the bottom", dispersed in the littoral and rested on the bottom in darkness, reversing at dawn (Eurasian perch, Lake Constance) [already in repo notes] — [Imbrock et al. 1996](https://d-nb.info/110877895X/34)
- **White sucker, connected Adirondack lakes (PIT telemetry)**: a subset of the population regularly travelled back and forth each day through a short, shallow channel between two lakes; **direction of movement depended strongly on time of day**; **larger fish were far more likely** to make the repeated movements; the authors suggest such movement may be widespread but unobserved [primary abstract] — [Marcy-Quay & Kraft 2021, Northeastern Naturalist 28(4):430–443](https://www.eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-28-4/11-Marcy-Quay.shtml)
- **White sucker, general**: inshore in the evening and offshore by morning; more active at night [secondary; already in repo notes] — [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Catostomus_commersonii/)
- **Brown bullhead**: more active at night, using shallow water extensively, especially in winter (Lake Taupo, NZ, 12 fish with pressure-sensing transmitters) [primary abstract; already in repo notes; introduced population, ice-free lake] — [Dedual 2002, Hydrobiologia 483:129](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1021319310063)
- **Smallmouth**: rose shallower after sunset but moved little horizontally at night (Opeongo) — already in repo notes.

### Inferences
- **Diel rule set** for agents (inferred): 
  - Golden shiner school: littoral weed edge by day → open water at dawn and dusk (zooplankton feeding) → littoral; night: dispersed (schools break up).
  - Yellow perch and white perch schools: deeper/offshore by day → inshore at dusk → dispersed resting (perch) or shallow feeding (white perch) at night → back at dawn.
  - Pumpkinseed: day-active near home spot; rest in cover at night. Bluegill-type roamers show no diel pattern in net travel.
  - White sucker: inshore/shallow at night, offshore by morning; large suckers commute between connected basins on a daily schedule (through the Hot Brook brook, if passable).
  - Bullhead: night forays into shallows; day in cover.
- In an unstratified 3–5 m lake, "offshore/deeper" is mostly horizontal distance from the weed edge, not depth: an offshore day school over the basin floor is the analogue of the 4–9 m daytime perch habitat (consistent with the repo's depth notes).

### Gaps
- No numbers (distance, % of population, timing in minutes from sunset) were obtained for Hall et al. 1979 or Shoup et al. 2014.
- The channel length, crossing counts and hours of the Marcy-Quay & Kraft white sucker crossings were not available beyond the abstract.
- No diel horizontal telemetry for chain pickerel, redbreast, fallfish, dace or killifish in lakes.

## 3. Seasonal movements: spawning migrations, inlet/outlet streams, wintering aggregations, summer stream pools, and movement between connected lakes

### Takeaway
A lake's connecting stream is used by many lake species, not just the obvious spawners. A season-long two-way trap on a small Michigan inlet (about 400 m long, 2.7 m wide, 0.46 m deep — close to the Hot Brook brook's scale) caught 1,786 bluegill, 654 pumpkinseed, 492 bullheads, 113 white ("common") suckers and 40 largemouth bass moving up from the lake between mid-March and mid-June, and "many bluegills and sunfish" stayed in the creek's larger pools all summer — direct support for the owner's observation of sunfish crowding the brook's pools. Movement between lakes through connecting streams is real but small (≈ 1% of tagged fish in an Adirondack lake chain), with daily commuting by a subset of large white suckers. Winter brings contraction: smallmouth to a few traditional wintering sites where they barely move until late March; chain pickerel to a few island and near-shore patches.

### Cited Findings

#### Inlet/outlet stream use by lake fish
- **Portage Creek, the inlet to Big Portage Lake, Jackson County, Michigan** (two-way weir 150 yards above the lake, March 15–June 17, 1946; creek a quarter mile long between the lake and a pond, ~9 ft wide, 1.5 ft deep, summer flow ~2 cfs, spring-fed, marl/peat bottom with some gravel) [primary, full text read; 1940s report]:
  - The records "indicate a negligible movement of escaped trout in the stream, a wandering of centrarchids and bullheads, and apparent spawning migrations" of white sucker ("common sucker"), chub sucker, mud pickerel, spottail shiner, creek chub and golden shiner.
  - **Upstream totals from the lake**: bluegill 1,786; pumpkinseed 654; yellow bullhead 299; black bullhead 193; chub sucker 120; common (white) sucker 113; mud pickerel 49; largemouth bass 40; yellow perch 9; spottail shiner ~748 and golden shiner ~54 (estimates; small fish passed through the mesh).
  - **Sunfish**: bluegill and pumpkinseed were taken every week; bluegill rose sharply in late May–early June; most were 2–4.5 in (5–11 cm [derived]), a few 6–7 in; "the small size of the bluegills and sunfish and the type of habitat found in the creek suggest that their migration was not for the purpose of spawning". Many upstream centrarchids had not returned by June 17; the largest downstream movement came in the last weeks. **"Many bluegills and sunfish were observed in several of the larger pools of the creek throughout the summer."**
  - **Largemouth bass** (3–13 in, mostly ~9 in) began ascending when the water reached 50 °F (10 °C) on April 21 and kept coming occasionally.
  - **Bullheads**: most came in the last half of March (sizes 2–10 in yellow, 3–7 in black); judged not a spawning run.
  - **White sucker**: a definite spawning run, mid-April to the first week of May at 52–55 °F (11–13 °C [derived]); 97 of 113 mature (42 F, 25 M of 67 sexed); only 14 seen returning, all badly bruised.
  - **Mud pickerel** (an *Esox*): apparent spawning run at 45–50 °F (7–10 °C) as the weir went in; most returned to the lake soon after spawning; some stayed in the stream through midsummer.
  — [Lievense 1947, Michigan Inst. Fish. Res. Report 1106](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1101-RR1200/RR1106.pdf)
- **Redbreast spring upstream drift** (Little River, NC): 20 of 21 fish tagged in April–May and recaptured within 60 days moved upstream; activity (trap catch) peaked in May–June [primary, full text read; southern flag] — [Hudson & Hester 1975](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/HUDSON-325.pdf)
- **White perch**: spawning schools gather in tributary streams or over gravel shoals; schools in turbid shallow areas where they feed (Oneida Lake, NY) [agency education page; via search summary] — [NY Sea Grant, Oneida Lake](https://seagrant.sunysb.edu/oli/olei-whiteperch.htm)
- **White sucker run residence** (relatives, western USA; radio/PIT): Lost River suckers ran 3.7–5.5 km upstream and stayed up to 16.4 days [via search summary] — [Perkins et al. 1996 PDF](https://spfgtsn.krisweb.com/biblio/klamath_nbs_perkinsetal_1996.pdf); flannelmouth suckers stayed a median 30 days in one tributary and ~10 days in another, longer in wet years [via search summary] — [USGS record](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70247370)
- White sucker run onset (≥ 10 °C after ice-out), dusk entry peak and discharge effects are already in the repo notes (`whiteperch_shiner_sucker_bullhead.md`, Swanson et al. 2021).

#### Movement between connected lakes
- **Adirondack chain of five stream-linked lakes** (studied since 1995): tagged fish moved among lakes; species introduced into one lake were later caught in neighbouring lakes; lake assemblages had the same species richness and composition as their inlet and outlet streams; "few individuals migrated to new lakes"; the authors propose treating lake fishes as a watershed metapopulation [primary abstract] — [Daniels et al. 2008, Northeastern Naturalist 15(4):577–588](https://www.eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-15-4/18-Daniels.shtml)
  - A news summary of the same work: a small (about **1%**) but significant number of tagged fish (yellow perch, white sucker and brown bullhead among the tagged species) moved through streams from one lake to another, and these movements were not tied to habitual upstream feeding or spawning migrations [secondary; via search summary] — [Audubon, "Fish gotta swim"](https://www.audubon.org/news/fish-gotta-swim)
- **Daily white sucker commuting** between two connected Adirondack lakes through a short shallow channel, by a size-biased subset (large fish) — see §2 — [Marcy-Quay & Kraft 2021](https://www.eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-28-4/11-Marcy-Quay.shtml)
- **Chain pickerel, George Lake, Kejimkujik (NS)**: of 61 acoustic-tagged fish (31–62 cm TL), the 30 tracked for 12 months showed "extended summer migrations beyond George Lake", winter inactivity, and individual differences in habitat [primary abstract] — [Sauriol, Nilsen & Avery](https://merseytobeatic.ca/sites/merseytobeatic.ca/files/agendafiles/Sauriol_Felixe.pdf)

#### Wintering aggregations and seasonal contraction (horizontal)
- **Smallmouth, Milipsigate Lake, NS** (335.8 ha, max 17 m, island-studded): widespread across the array in Sep–Oct (partly a tagging/release effect); from November movements narrowed to specific aggregations; **by December–January all fish occupied distinct wintering sites — four sites, each with at least two individuals, one in each quadrant of the lake — with "extremely little horizontal movement", stationary until late March**; a marked dispersal in April–May across the lake; **by June 79% of positions were within 50 m of the east or west shore** [primary, full text read] — [Sergio 2026, Dalhousie M.Sc. thesis, ch. 3 (PDF)](https://dal.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/0d151e69-14e6-46e0-ab65-3fac80168436/download)
- **Chain pickerel, same lake**: in fall detected throughout the array (lake centre and both shores); **December: only the western reaches, 80% of positions within 30 m of shore; January: 91% in the south-western section, aggregated around one island; February: 97% around another island in the south-east; March: expanded to the whole array again**; spring–summer mostly in the north-eastern quadrant around an island; spring–summer detections were the fewest (monthly positions averaged 40 vs 165 in fall and 94 in winter) [primary, full text read] — [Sergio 2026](https://dal.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/0d151e69-14e6-46e0-ab65-3fac80168436/download)
  - Sergio notes pike show activity peaks in spring and fall and contracted movement in summer and winter [primary, full text read, citing Swindall 2025 and Rous 2023] — same source.
- **Pike, 25-ha lake**: winter activity centres larger than summer ones but overlapping [via search summary] — [Kobler et al. 2008](https://www.forland.hu-berlin.de/de/institut/departments/dntw/jp_bfm/publ_html/Kobler_Klefoth_Arlinghaus2008.pdf)
- **Pumpkinseed relocation** was highest in autumn (49% of relocations) and lowest in spring (15%) [primary abstract; European flag] — [Zięba et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/10.3389/conf.fmars.2015.03.00123/2930/XV_European_Congress_of_Ichthyology/all_events/event_abstract)
- **Bluegill** movement peaked in midsummer in a 332-ha shallow lake [primary abstract] — [Paukert et al. 2004](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/movement-home-range-and-site-fidelity-bluegills-a-great-plains-lake)
- Smallmouth fall gathering on a few traditional points/reefs and yellow perch under-ice schools are already in the repo notes.

### Inferences
- **Brook use rule (spring–summer)**: from ice-out, once brook water passes ~7–10 °C, a fraction of lake fish enter the brook — suckers and pickerel to spawn and leave (suckers mid-April–early May at 11–13 °C; pickerel first, at 7–10 °C), bullheads early and briefly, sunfish (pumpkinseed, redbreast; young and small adults especially) and some bass in late spring, with many sunfish **staying in the deeper pools through summer**. Sunfish return to the lake through late summer–fall. This matches the owner's field observation; the 1946 counts (≈ 2,400 sunfish through one small inlet in three months) give an order of magnitude for a weedy lake's inlet.
- **Inter-lake exchange**: a per-fish probability of ending up in the other lake of order 1% per year (Adirondack; study length unknown, so this is a rough figure), higher for a size-biased subset of white suckers that commute daily, and for chain pickerel in summer ("migrations beyond the lake"). In the sim the brook should therefore be a two-way corridor with low background dispersal plus species-specific purposeful use (spawning runs, summer pool residence, sucker commuting).
- **Winter rule**: smallmouth go to a few fixed, traditional wintering sites (≥ 2 fish each, about one site per lake quadrant in a 336-ha lake) and stop moving from December to late March; chain pickerel stay shallow and near shore (≤ 30 m) but shift as a group between a few island/shore patches month to month; they stay active (repo notes: anglers catch them through the ice).
- **Spring dispersal**: bass disperse widely in April–May, then settle near shore (≤ 50 m) for the summer — a usable "home shoreline" for bass agents.

### Gaps
- No study of fish use of a brook connecting two lakes (as opposed to an inlet) was found, nor any quantitative summer pool-occupancy count for sunfish in such a brook.
- Fallfish and blacknose dace stream–lake movements, and fallfish spawning migrations into tributaries, were not found (Reed 1971, TAFS, the main fallfish biology paper, was not retrieved).
- No white perch lake-to-tributary run sizes or residence times from northern lakes.
- The Adirondack "~1%" figure comes from a news summary; the paper's methods, durations and per-species rates were not seen.

## 4. Group structure: school/shoal sizes, density, stability, size sorting, solitary large fish

### Takeaway
Wild shoals of small littoral fish are small and fluid: banded killifish shoals captured whole held 2–24 fish (median 5), encountered other shoals about once a minute for a few seconds, and sorted by species and body length, with larger fish at the front and parasitised or hungry fish more often alone. Yellow perch form larger, size-sorted schools (50–200 fish, 20–30 m across) that need light and break up at dusk. Within schools, nearest-neighbour distance is about one body length. Adults of the sunfish, bass, pickerel and bullhead are solitary or in loose groups of a few; their young school.

### Cited Findings
- **Banded killifish, whole free-ranging shoals captured with a grid-net** (Morice Lake, New Brunswick; 80-m strip of littoral; 0.25-m² grid-net set in 15 cm of water, 2.5 m from shore) [primary, full text read]:
  - **21 complete shoals, 128 fish; shoal size 2–24, median 5 (quartiles 3 and 8)**; median body length 34 mm (quartiles 29 and 38 mm).
  - Fish in the front half of a shoal were larger than those in the rear; parasitised fish tended to be at the front and periphery; solitary fish outside shoals were more likely parasitised and more heavily so.
  - Killifish form two-dimensional shoals in the littoral zone; shoals with many parasitised fish were more phalanx-like, others elliptical.
  — [Ward et al. 2002, J. Anim. Ecol. 71:10–14 (accepted manuscript)](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/989/1/ms-gridnet2.pdf)
- **Mixed shoals of killifish, golden shiner and white sucker** (a temperate lake in New Brunswick) [primary abstract]:
  - Shoal encounters occurred about once per **1.1 min per shoal** and lasted **3.7 s** on average.
  - Encounter duration depended on shoal size, but not on body-length or species differences between shoals; whether an individual switched shoals depended on body-length and species differences, not on shoal size.
  - Ten entire shoals collected were **assorted by species and body length**.
  — [Krause et al. 2000, Proc. R. Soc. B (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1690773)
- **Group size depends on context** (banded killifish, lab, odour cues): groups were smaller with a food cue and larger with an alarm (predator) cue than in controls [via search summary] — [Hoare, Couzin, Godin & Krause 2004, Anim. Behav.](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/1c25e706-6963-43ae-a12f-80543e60628d/full)
- **Golden shiner interaction rules** (lab trajectories of 2- and 3-fish groups): speed regulation dominates interactions, and speed changes are transmitted to fish both ahead and behind; no evidence of explicit alignment of body orientation; three-body interactions contribute substantially [via search summary] — [Katz, Tunstrøm, Ioannou, Huepe & Couzin 2011, PNAS 108:18720](https://catalog.comses.net/publications/16091); a companion study found each fish responded mainly to its single nearest neighbour, speeding up if the neighbour was far ahead or right behind and slowing if it was far behind or too close ahead [secondary] — [Science News, "School rules"](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/school-rules)
- **Golden shiner schooling** in the wild: "travel in large shoals"; larvae of 10–30 mm school in vegetated littoral areas [secondary; already in repo notes] — see `whiteperch_shiner_sucker_bullhead.md`.
- **Yellow perch schools**: spindle-shaped, 50–200 fish, sorted by size and age; young-of-year school more than adults; large adults sometimes travel alone; sexes often in separate schools; schools 20–30 m in diameter and 2–4 m high (Eurasian perch); break up in the evening and reform in the morning; lone perch swim at about half the speed of schools — all already in repo notes (`perch_pickerel_smallmouth_pumpkinseed.md`; sources [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Perca_flavescens), [Imbrock et al. 1996](https://d-nb.info/110877895X/34), [Hergenrader & Hasler 1967](https://academic.oup.com/tafs/article-abstract/96/4/373/7893637)).
- **White perch** school "from youth through adulthood" [agency; already in repo notes] — [NJ F&W Digest 2012](https://dep.nj.gov/wp-content/uploads/njfw/digest-marine-2012-feisty-white-perch-by-maryellen-gordon.pdf); surface schooling with dorsal fins out of water is a recognised sight in Maine [agency; already in repo notes] — [MDIFW White Perch Plan](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/docs/strategic-management-plans/whiteperch.pdf)
- **Pumpkinseed**: adults in pairs or loose groups of 3–4, young in shoreline schools; nest colonies of 3–15 nests [secondary; repo notes and ADW] — [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lepomis_gibbosus/)
- **Smallmouth wintering groups**: each of four wintering sites held at least two tagged fish [primary, full text read] — [Sergio 2026](https://dal.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/0d151e69-14e6-46e0-ab65-3fac80168436/download)
- **White sucker**: larger individuals far more likely to make repeated daily inter-lake movements [primary abstract] — [Marcy-Quay & Kraft 2021](https://www.eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-28-4/11-Marcy-Quay.shtml); tagged suckers that shared a spawning creek were not all associated outside the spawning season [via search summary] — [Shedd Aquarium](https://www.sheddaquarium.org/care-and-conservation/shedd-research/investigating-great-lakes-sucker-migrations); young-of-year form schools of several hundred [secondary; repo notes] — [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Catostomus_commersonii/)
- **Brown bullhead**: juveniles stay in schools [secondary; repo notes] — [ADW](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Ameiurus_nebulosus/)
- **School geometry (general, mostly marine)**:
  - School volume ≈ N·b³, with b the mean nearest-neighbour distance, roughly one body length (Pitcher & Partridge 1979, saithe, herring, cod in a tank) [secondary] — [arXiv q-bio/0501035](https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-bio/0501035)
  - Wild anchovy school photographed in situ: mean density 114.8 fish/m³, mean nearest-neighbour distance **1.2 body lengths** (s = 0.3) [via search summary; marine flag] — [NOAA SWFSC report](https://swfsc-publications.fisheries.noaa.gov/publications/CR/1977/7718.PDF)
  - Field herring schools are about an order of magnitude less dense than tank schools (Misund 1993, via secondary) [secondary; marine flag] — [arXiv q-bio/0501035](https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-bio/0501035)
  - In a tank, nearest-neighbour distance rose as light fell and stabilised near 1.5 BL above a threshold illuminance [via search summary] — [arXiv 2301.09577](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.09577)

### Inferences
- **Agent granularity** (inferred): school-agents for golden shiner, yellow perch (one school ≈ 50–200 fish), white perch, killifish (≈ 5 fish per shoal, range 2–24) and blacknose dace; single-fish agents for adult smallmouth, chain pickerel, brown bullhead and (mostly) pumpkinseed and redbreast; white sucker either way (adults loosely associated, size-biased commuters).
- **Fission–fusion**: small-fish shoals should meet about once a minute and exchange members by species and size match, not by shoal size; shoals grow under predator threat and split when food is found. A school agent therefore needs a membership count that can merge and split, with a rule preferring same-species, similar-length partners, and larger members leading.
- **Spacing**: render school members ~1 BL apart (1–1.5 BL), looser in dim light; schools of visual species (perch, shiner, white perch) dissolve at dusk.
- **Size and solitude**: large perch, large suckers and (by inference) large bass are more often alone or in small groups; parasitised or hungry small fish are more often alone — a cue for predators.

### Gaps
- No wild school-size counts for golden shiner, white perch, fallfish or blacknose dace were found.
- No nearest-neighbour or packing measurements specific to any of the 12 species in the wild.
- No measure of how long a school keeps its identity (hours vs days) for any of these species.

## 5. Telemetry studies giving positions over time (2-D/3-D) in small lakes

### Takeaway
Fine-scale positioning data for these species in small northern lakes are few: one acoustic-positioning study of chain pickerel and smallmouth with depth tags in a 336-ha Nova Scotia lake (Sergio 2026), white sucker depth-tag telemetry in a 52-ha Ontario lake, radio telemetry of bluegill in a 332-ha shallow plains lake, PIT telemetry of white suckers commuting between Adirondack lakes, pike positioning in a 25-ha German lake, and yellow perch telemetry in South Dakota lakes. They give movement statistics and seasonal maps rather than downloadable 3-D tracks.

### Cited Findings
- **Milipsigate Lake, Nova Scotia** (335.8 ha, max 17 m, island-studded) — chain pickerel and smallmouth [primary, full text read] — [Sergio 2026 thesis record](https://dal.scholaris.ca//items/57c35737-f666-4cc9-8fb3-45eb5c0a0e46); [PDF](https://dal.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/0d151e69-14e6-46e0-ab65-3fac80168436/download):
  - 18 adult chain pickerel and 12 smallmouth tagged Sep–Oct 2018 with depth-sensing acoustic transmitters; positions by VPS, checked with YAPS.
  - Raw positions: 86,134 pickerel and 78,957 bass; after error filtering 76,944 and 48,447; after removing dead or departed fish 3,584 pickerel and 46,961 bass positions remained.
  - Three pickerel were never detected, one died, and **six left the array within two weeks of release and were never detected again**; five bass died after the winter.
  - Horizontal seasonal maps as in §3; depth use: pickerel 50% of detections at 0.76–1.82 m; bass 0–15 m in warm months, 2–6 m in winter; bass on average ~3× deeper than pickerel.
  - Strong individual variation in habitat use (significant random effect of fish ID).
- **Turkey Lake, Ontario** (52 ha), 9 white suckers with depth tags, 29 receivers, Dec 2021–Dec 2022: ranges and daily distances as in §1 [already in repo notes] — [Richter et al. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10641-024-01581-8)
- **Pelican Lake, Nebraska** (332 ha, very shallow), 78 radio-tagged bluegill, 2-h fixes over 24-h cycles monthly Apr–Sep [primary abstract] — [Paukert et al. 2004](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/movement-home-range-and-site-fidelity-bluegills-a-great-plains-lake)
- **Two connected Adirondack lakes**, PIT telemetry at a channel, white sucker [primary abstract] — [Marcy-Quay & Kraft 2021](https://www.eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-28-4/11-Marcy-Quay.shtml)
- **Kleiner Döllnsee, Germany** (25 ha), 12 pike, 3-h fixes, summer to under-ice [via search summary] — [Kobler et al. 2008](https://www.forland.hu-berlin.de/de/institut/departments/dntw/jp_bfm/publ_html/Kobler_Klefoth_Arlinghaus2008.pdf)
- **Lake Erie intake-forebay EMG study** of smallmouth (675-m enclosed basin): continuous activity, not positions [primary, full text read] — [Cooke et al. 2001](https://www.fecpl.ca/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/EMG_Comp_Move_JFB.pdf)
- **George Lake, Kejimkujik**: 61 chain pickerel acoustic-tagged; results so far only qualitative [primary abstract] — [Sauriol et al.](https://merseytobeatic.ca/sites/merseytobeatic.ca/files/agendafiles/Sauriol_Felixe.pdf)
- Yellow perch telemetry in Lakes Madison and Sinai (SD) and smallmouth in Opeongo are in the repo notes.

### Inferences
- Sergio's Milipsigate study is the closest analogue to Hot Brook for the two top predators (similar area, Maritime climate, islands, chain pickerel + smallmouth together). Its seasonal maps can be used directly as validation targets: bass at ≥ 4 fixed wintering sites Dec–Mar, near-shore (≤ 50 m) in June; pickerel near-shore (≤ 30 m) in December, clustered at islands Jan–Feb, re-expanding in March.
- The early loss of six pickerel from the array within two weeks, together with the George Lake "migrations beyond the lake", hints that pickerel readily leave a basin through connecting water — relevant to the brook (inference; Sergio does not attribute the losses).
- Strong individual variation in all telemetry studies supports giving each agent a persistent "movement personality" (range size, boldness) rather than identical rules.

### Gaps
- No fine-scale 3-D positioning study was found for golden shiner, white perch, brown bullhead (northern lake), pumpkinseed, redbreast, fallfish, killifish or dace in a small northern lake.
- None of the studies above publishes raw 3-D tracks in a form that could be replayed; only summaries and figures were available.
- Sergio's chain pickerel spring–summer coverage was thin (≈ 40 positions per month), so pickerel summer space use there is weakly resolved.
