# Movement between Upper and Lower Hot Brook Lake, and use of the connecting brook

Target system: Upper (369 ha, mean 3.3 m, ~3.4 km long) and Lower (274 ha, mean 2.7 m, ~2.5 km long) Hot Brook Lake, joined by a ~150 m × ~8 m rocky brook (1–1.2 m pool at the upper mouth, ~0.3 m riffles, ~0.5 m run). The owner reports that pickerel, bass and "everything" swim through. This note adds to `habitat3d.md` and `habitat3d_new_species.md`. It does not repeat their §6 "Range / moves" rows, apart from one correction (see Q1, Inferences).

Tags: **[ft]** full text read; **[abs]** abstract or summary page read; **[snip]** search-result snippet only, not verified against the paper; **[S]** southern or non-northern system; **[EU]** European; **[sur]** surrogate species; **[inf]** my inference.

## Q1. Movement BETWEEN connected lakes: fraction moving, returns, timing, size and sex

### Takeaway
Connected-lake studies do not give one rate. Each species moves in its own way:
- **Sunfish:** move between lakes at **3–36 % a year** (bluegill, 16-lake Minnesota chain).
- **Pike:** **10–20 %** of a lake's population runs through a 1-m-deep creek each spring, and **83–96 % return**.
- **Yellow perch:** shift between lakes in **autumn (Sept–Oct)** and partly return **before spawning (Mar–May)**.
- **Smallmouth:** split into lake-resident and stream-spawning fish, and males keep their type for life.
- **White suckers:** large fish **commute nightly** between lakes in June–July.
- **Adirondack lake chain (all species pooled):** only **~1 % of tagged fish** reached another lake. That is a whole-study figure, **not a per-year rate**.

### Cited Findings

**All species pooled (Adirondacks, the nearest analogue to Maine)**
- Daniels et al. 2008 (*Northeastern Naturalist* 15(4):577–588) [abs]:
  - **System and method:** five Adirondack lakes linked by streams, studied since 1995. Adult fish were netted, tagged and released.
  - **Movement:** about **1 % of tagged fish** moved through the streams to another lake. **No annual rate is given.**
  - **Fastest:** white suckers, reaching a new lake in as few as **21 days** after tagging.
  - **Cause:** the moves were not tied to the usual upstream feeding or spawning migrations.
  - **Species:** white sucker, brown bullhead and yellow perch, among others. Stocked bass were later caught in neighbouring lakes.
  - — [Audubon summary](https://www.audubon.org/news/fish-gotta-swim)
- The same paper [abs]:
  - Lake communities matched their inlet and outlet stream communities in richness and composition, though relative abundance varied.
  - "Even though few individuals migrated to new lakes", the authors propose treating lake fish as a watershed-wide metapopulation.
  - — [BioOne abstract](https://bioone.org/journals/northeastern-naturalist/volume-15/issue-4/1092-6194-15.4.577/Fish-Movement-Among-Lakes-Are-Lakes-Isolated/10.1656/1092-6194-15.4.577.full) [snip]

**White sucker: nightly commuting (Adirondacks)**
- Marcy-Quay & Kraft 2021 (*Northeastern Naturalist* 28(4):430–443):
  - **Method:** a PIT antenna array in a **short, shallow channel** between two Adirondack lakes.
  - **Behaviour:** a *subset* of suckers crossed **nightly in June and July**. They left one lake **at sunset** and returned **shortly before dawn**.
  - **Most active fish:** one made **29 round trips in 60 days**.
  - **Size and direction:** direction was strongly tied to time of day, and **larger individuals were far more likely** to commute.
  - n tagged, lake sizes and channel size are behind the paywall.
  - — [NENA abstract page](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-28-4/11-Marcy-Quay.shtml) [abs]; [NY AFS summary](https://newyork.fisheries.org/?p=1040) [snip]

**Bluegill and black crappie [sur for pumpkinseed and redbreast] (Minnesota lake chain)**
- Parsons & Reed 2005 (*NAJFM* 25:689–695):
  - **System:** a **chain of 16 lakes** in west-central Minnesota. Fish were T-bar anchor tagged and tracked by angler returns from lakes other than the tagging lake.
  - **Bluegill:** 3-year emigration was **9 %** (Le Homme Dieu Lake) and **14 %** (Victoria Lake). **Annual rates ran 3–36 %.**
  - **Black crappie** (4 lakes): 6–23 % over 3 years, and annual rates of **0–92 %**.
  - **No size effect and no upstream or downstream preference.**
  - — [abstract](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1577/M04-021.1) [snip; HTTP 403 on fetch]; citation confirmed in Chizinski et al. 2016 [ft]
- Tag sizes in the companion Minnesota DNR exploitation work: bluegill ≥ 150 mm and crappie ≥ 180 mm, trap-netted in Sept–Oct 1993–95. The angler reporting rate was 69 %. — [MN DNR 1998 report via LRL](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/pre2003/other/990117.pdf) [snip]
- In the same chain, larval crappie catch failed to predict year-class strength in two chain lakes, "likely due to supplemental recruitment caused by emigration from other lakes in the chain". Two isolated lakes behaved as expected. — [Parsons et al. 2004, MN DNR Investigational Report 514](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2005/other/050139.pdf) [ft]

**Northern pike [sur for chain pickerel]: spawning run through a small creek between lakes**
- Chizinski et al. 2016 (*NAJFM* 36:769–779) [ft]:
  - **Lakes:** Lake Susan (37.5 ha, 5.1 m max) → Rice Marsh Lake → Lake Riley (120 ha, 14.9 m max).
  - **Riley Creek** joins them. It is **5–15 m wide, about 1 m deep at most, and "often goes dry in the summer"**: a close physical analogue of Hot Brook.
  - **Method:** full-width temporary barrier traps, 2009–2011.
  - **Start:** pike began moving toward the marsh on **17 March (DOY 76)** from Susan and **25 March (DOY 84)** from Riley, when the stream was at **~2 °C** and stream depth was **58 cm**.
  - **Duration:** fish were caught "nearly every day for **3 weeks** through early April" while the water warmed **from 3 to 10 °C**. The run was "a few fish per day for a few weeks", not a pulse.
  - **Condition:** most were running with eggs or milt. From 10 April to mid-May the catch was dominated by **post-spawning fish returning**.
  - **Returns:** **96 %** of pike had moved back to Lake Susan and **93 %** to Lake Riley before carp began moving.
  - **Share of the population:** about **10–20 %** of Lake Riley's roughly 1,600 pike migrated each year ("less than 20 %"), which the authors read as partial migration.
  - **Effect of rain:** movement probability rose with precipitation.
  - — [Chizinski et al. 2016 PDF](https://rpbcwd.org/files/0c215a59d/chizinskiEtAl2016_differentMigratoryStrategies_Pike_Carp.pdf)
- **The same traps also counted other species moving through Riley Creek** (Table 2, counts per site-year, spring to early summer) [ft]:

  | Species | Count per site-year |
  |---|---|
  | Bluegill | 31–592 |
  | Black bullhead [sur for brown] | 219–563 at the Lake Susan outlet |
  | Black crappie | up to 408 |
  | Largemouth bass | up to 61 |
  | Yellow perch | up to 37 |
  | White sucker | up to 28 |
  | Pumpkinseed | 0–8 |
  | Small minnows | up to 1,409 |

  - The authors say the large numbers of bluegills "suggested that they were actively moving between lakes".
  - — [Chizinski et al. 2016](https://rpbcwd.org/files/0c215a59d/chizinskiEtAl2016_differentMigratoryStrategies_Pike_Carp.pdf)

**Yellow perch: autumn shift between lakes**
- Schmitt & Hubert 1983 (*Proc. Iowa Acad. Sci.* 90(3):103–106), East and West Okoboji Lakes, Iowa [abs]:
  - Perch moved from East into West Okoboji **in autumn** and returned to East **before spawning in spring**.
  - The drivers were "thermal gradients" from **seasonally reversing interlake flow** and the two lakes' **different warming and cooling rates**.
  - n tagged and recaptures are in the full text (HTTP 403).
  - — [UNI ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.uni.edu/pias/vol90/iss3/8)
- Tabor et al., Lake Union (via the Ship Canal) and Lake Washington, Seattle [ft of slides] **[non-northeastern; urban canal]**:
  - **Method:** **47 adult perch** acoustic-tagged at Gas Works Park in 2020–21, of which **30** gave seasonal data. They were followed for **460 days** on 14 receivers.
  - **Outbound:** **80 % moved to Lake Washington**, and **74 % of those moves came in September or October**.
  - **Return:** **half of the moves back came in spring (March–May)**, but **"most did not return"**.
  - **Distance:** the farthest move was **~10 miles (~16 km)**.
  - — [WRIA 8 presentation, May 2023](https://www.govlink.org/watersheds/8/committees/2305/WRIA8_predator_presentation_May-18-2023.pdf)
  - The peer-reviewed version is Tabor et al. 2024, *Northwest Science* 98(1). [Preprint link](https://northwestscience.org/web/default/files/resources/Journal/Volume%2098/98-1/pre-prints/tabor_et_al._2024_preprint.pdf) (HTTP 404).

**Smallmouth bass: lake residents vs stream migrants**
- Millers Lake and the Mississippi River, Ontario (QUBS) [abs]:
  - **Method:** **29 adults** radio-tagged (16 M, 13 F; 372 ± 49 mm TL, range 285–486), tracked for **3 years** from May 2000.
  - **System:** the lake is 45 ha, 1.75 km long and 25 m deep. The river section is 1.5 km with 8 pools (> 6 m deep, 0.49–2.14 ha).
  - **Two types:** "lake-resident fish remained in the lake throughout the year". **Potamodromous** fish migrated upriver to spawn in spring and "returned to the lake prior to the onset of winter".
  - **Fidelity:** "the vast majority of males" kept to **either river or lake** as spawning habitat **for life**. About half of males that nested in consecutive years nested within 20 m of their previous nest.
  - The fraction of migrants is not on the page.
  - — [QUBS project page](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/divergent-life-histories-among-smallmouth-bass-inhabiting-a-connected-river-lake-system)
- Lake Washington Ship Canal (Northwest Science 86(2), 2012) **[non-northeastern]** [snip]:
  - **Method:** 72 adults acoustic-tagged, 57 followed for seasonal moves.
  - **Outbound:** **82 %** left the canal for Lake Washington **between June and October**.
  - **Size effect:** bass **< 350 mm were more likely to overwinter in the canal**, and those that went left later, in Aug–Oct.
  - **Return:** they came back to the canal from **March to mid-April**.
  - **Genetics:** preliminary evidence that canal bass are reproductively isolated from year-round lake bass.
  - — [BioOne](https://complete.bioone.org/journals/Northwest-Science/volume-86/issue-2/046.086.0205/Seasonal-Movements-of-Smallmouth-Bass-in-the-Lake-Washington-Ship/10.3955/046.086.0205.full)

**Largemouth bass and northern pike [sur] (Rideau Canal, Ontario: lakes linked by locks)**
- **224 fish** (largemouth bass, northern pike, common carp) acoustic-tagged over 2019–2021 [abs]:
  - **35 passages by 23 native fish**, about 10 % of tagged fish in 3 years. **49 %** of passages went through locks.
  - **Most passages were downstream**, and high-lift locks were impassable upstream.
  - This connection is barrier-limited, unlike Hot Brook.
  - — [CJFAS 2023, doi 10.1139/cjfas-2023-0207](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/ecological-connectivity-of-invasive-and-native-fishes-in-a-historic-navigation-waterway)

**Largemouth bass [sur, S] (Lake Mattamuskeet, NC: lake ↔ canals and culverts)**
- **Method:** 42 tagged (30 in the lake, 12 in the canals), 32 retained after a probation period, Jul 2017 – Mar 2019. **90 % of all 609,875 detections were at the lake–canal interface** [ft].
- **Results** [ft]:
  - "with two notable exceptions … each individual fish … exhibited **frequent movement between the lake and canal**".
  - One canal fish was never detected in the lake in **203 days**. Another was detected 141,443 times in the canal and only twice in the lake over **441 days**.
  - **West-lake bass were never detected on the east side.** East-lake bass used both sides, including all 5 causeway culverts.
  - East-lake bass moved to the west lake "during a relative increase in water level".
- — [NCWRC 2021 report](https://ncwildlife.gov/media/3109/download)

**Lake ↔ stream winter partial migration [EU; roach and white bream are not in the Hot Brook set, but the piscivores are]**
- Skov et al. 2008 (*Ecol. Freshw. Fish* 17:406–415) [abs]:
  - **Method:** PIT telemetry between **a shallow lake and its 3 streams**, October to June.
  - **Migrants:** **40 % of roach** and **55 % of white bream** wintered in the streams. **Rudd < 6 %**, with smaller rudd migrating more.
  - **Piscivores** (pike and perch): **< 2 %**.
  - **Timing:** migration began in late October and ended in May, with synchronised bursts in **December, January and April**. Fish entered the streams in late afternoon or morning, depending on season.
  - — [DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/inter-and-size-specific-patterns-of-fish-seasonal-migration-betwe/); [Lund LUP](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/561161a1-6fdb-4e82-b31f-9f47492e523f)
- A 4-year study of the same system reported that pike and perch did not migrate. — [ESA 2007 abstract](https://eco.confex.com/eco/2007/techprogram/P5843.HTM) [snip]
- A follow-up (Skov et al. 2013, *Biology Letters*) found that roach staying in the lake were substantially more likely to be eaten by cormorants. — [ScienceNordic summary](https://www.sciencenordic.com/agriculture--fisheries-animals-biology/fish-migrate-to-avoid-predators/1383242) [snip]

**Pike in a large connected lake system [EU; a scale contrast]**
- Lake Siljan, Sweden. **Method:** 45 large adults, 28 receivers, 2 years [snip].
- **Ranges:** maximum ranges ran from **< 3 km to ~47 km**, with a mean of ~26 km. Ranges expanded in autumn and winter and contracted in spring.
- **Spawning fidelity:** fish returned within a ~7 °C window.
- **Avoidance:** **none entered the northern basin** despite open connection, which suggests "ecological avoidance rather than physical limitation".
- — [SLU thesis 2025](https://stud.epsilon.slu.se/21972/)

**Pumpkinseed: moves out of lakes down the outflow [EU, introduced]**
- **Study:** an English Marie Curie project (ALIENFISHMIGRATIONS, 2004–05) using coded-wire and PIT tags [abs].
- **Leaving lakes:** pumpkinseed "move from on-line lakes into adjacent streams", and **"movement intensity was influenced by the type of water outlet"**.
- **Distances:** the farthest move was **2.5 km downstream**, at a mean of **about 30 m per day**. Upstream moves were also recorded.
- **Age and season:** **age 3+ fish moved farthest** in both directions, **farthest in spring**. Mean age at maturity in streams was 3.3 years, suggesting maturity-related moves.
- **Recapture:** 43.5 % of PIT-tagged fish were recaptured.
- — [CORDIS project 501750 final report summary](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/501750/reporting/it)

**Brown bullhead**
- Presque Isle Bay, Lake Erie: **49 telemetered adults** did not leave the bay. They showed site fidelity, and genetics found one panmictic population (112 fish). — [USGS record](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/movements-brown-bullheads-presque-isle-bay-lake-erie-pennsylvania) [snip]. This is already summarised in `habitat3d.md` as "resident"; the new detail is the n.
- Bullheads were among the species moving between Adirondack lakes (Daniels 2008, above). Black bullheads passed the Lake Susan outlet trap in **hundreds per year** (Chizinski 2016, above).

**White perch [S / estuarine; no inland lake-to-lake study found]**
- McGrath & Austin 2009 (*TAFS* 138:966–974):
  - **Method:** 16 acoustic-tagged fish in two small tidal tributaries of the York River, Virginia.
  - **Site fidelity:** **most were site-faithful**, with mean home range **0.11 km² (11 ha)**. Core use (utilisation distribution) was ≤ 10 % of the home range.
  - **Tidal shuttle:** fish were on the flooded marsh at high tide and in the deeper channels at low tide.
  - — [TAFS abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1577/T08-176.1) [snip]
- Carmans River, Long Island:
  - **Method:** 40 tagged, 15 with ≥ 28 days of data.
  - **Two types:** "**both residential and dispersive individuals**".
  - **Season:** space use expanded in autumn and spring.
  - **Diel shuttle:** daily up- and downstream movement was seen on **13.8 %** of days in winter and **49.4–57.5 %** in summer, **upstream at night** in summer and fall.
  - — [AFS 2012 abstract](https://afs.confex.com/afs/2012/webprogram/Paper10211.html) [snip]
- Maine: dams that raised water over natural falls let white perch "swim over the inundated falls and invade previously unoccupied waters". — [MDIFW white perch plan](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/docs/strategic-management-plans/whiteperch.pdf) [snip; HTTP 403 on fetch]

### Inferences
- **[inf] Correction to `habitat3d.md` §11 ("about 1 % of fish a year cross").**
  - Daniels et al. give ~1 % of tagged fish over a multi-year study, not per year. Their lakes are joined by longer Adirondack streams.
  - A 150-m, 8-m-wide brook with a 1.1-m mouth pool is far more permeable. The Riley Creek counts (hundreds of bluegill and bullhead a year through a 5–15 m wide, ≤ 1 m deep, summer-drying creek) and the owner's report both point to much higher rates.
  - Replace the flat 1 % with species rules (next bullet). Keep ~1 %/yr only as the floor for the least mobile species.
- **[inf] Suggested annual chance that an adult ends up living in the other lake ("re-settle"), plus temporary trips:**

  | Species | Re-settle / yr | Temporary trips and timing | Basis |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Pumpkinseed, redbreast | **5–15 %**; no size or direction bias | trips mostly spring (age 3+) and drift in summer | bluegill 3–36 %/yr, median of the two lakes ≈ 10 %; pumpkinseed 2.5 km/30 m·d⁻¹ |
  | Yellow perch | **10–25 %** | an **autumn shift (Sept–Oct)** with a partial **spring return before spawning (Mar–May)**, by school, not singly | Okoboji; Seattle 74 % of moves Sep–Oct, "most did not return" |
  | Chain pickerel | **~5 %** | **10–20 % make a spring trip** at ice-out (2–10 °C) through the brook to flooded margins or the other lake; **~90 % return within 3–8 weeks** | pike, Chizinski 2016 |
  | Smallmouth bass | **~5 %** | give each fish a fixed **"stream-spawner" trait (~10–20 % of males)** that nests in or near the brook every year and returns to its home lake by autumn; the rest are lake-resident for life | Millers Lake |
  | White sucker | **5–10 %** | spring spawning run (habitat3d), plus **nightly commuting in June–July** by the **largest ~20 % of adults** (up to ~50 % of nights per fish, 29/60). Leave at sunset, return before dawn | Marcy-Quay |
  | Brown bullhead | **~5 %** | **corridor use at night, spring–summer** | Riley Creek counts |
  | White perch | **10–20 %** | **schools** cross; spring spawning runs into the brook at night and the autumn space expansion | resident/disperser split, Long Island |

  - Every figure in the table is a calibration starting point [inf], not a measured Hot Brook value.
  - Bluegill annual rates are highly variable, so add year-to-year noise.
  - For pickerel, the Siljan pike result (no fish entered a connected basin) says that avoidance, not distance, may hold most fish back.
  - For white perch the evidence is estuarine only.
- **[inf] Make crossing depend on distance from the brook mouth.** Mattamuskeet west-lake bass never reached the east side, and canal-tagged fish kept to the canal. Fish whose home range lies near Upper's north-basin outlet or Lower's inflow mouth should cross far more often than fish 2–3 km away.
- **[inf] Size and sex rules.**
  - Sucker commuting: large fish.
  - Smallmouth: small bass (< 350 mm) were less migratory in the Ship Canal study; the stream-spawner habit is held by males across their lives.
  - Sunfish and crappie: no size effect.
  - Pike: no sex ratio reported.
- **[inf] Timing triggers.**
  - Rain or flow pulses raise the chance of moving (pike, carp, alewife emigration).
  - A temperature difference between the lakes drives perch (Okoboji). Lower is shallower (2.7 m mean) and should warm and cool faster than Upper.

## Q2. Use of the connecting stream itself: predators at mouths, residence, season, passage limits

### Takeaway
A short connecting stream is used in four ways:
- **A corridor that fish pass in hours.** Suckers make overnight round trips.
- **A seasonal destination.** Pike spawning runs last ~3 weeks in, and fish are back within ~4–8 weeks. Stream-spawning bass stay spring to autumn. Cyprinids winter there for months as a predation refuge, while piscivores mostly do not follow (< 2 %).
- **A predator ambush point.** Pike gather at river–lake confluences when prey pass. Bass gather at culverts and deeper flowing water.
- **A channel that can close.** Riffles block first at low flow. A creek like Riley Creek "often goes dry in the summer", and fish can be trapped until spring water rises.

### Cited Findings
**Residence time and transit speed**
- **Hours:** suckers left one lake at sunset and were back before dawn, making 29 round trips in 60 days. — [NENA](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-28-4/11-Marcy-Quay.shtml) [abs]
- **Days to weeks (pike):** the run in lasted ~3 weeks, and spent fish returned from 10 April to mid-May, so individual stays were on the order of 2–8 weeks. **93–96 %** were back in their lakes by the time carp moved. — [Chizinski 2016](https://rpbcwd.org/files/0c215a59d/chizinskiEtAl2016_differentMigratoryStrategies_Pike_Carp.pdf) [ft]
- **Months (stream-spawning smallmouth):** "migrated … to upriver spawning habitat in the spring and then returned to the lake prior to the onset of winter". — [QUBS](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/divergent-life-histories-among-smallmouth-bass-inhabiting-a-connected-river-lake-system) [abs]
- **Months (wintering cyprinids) [EU]:** cyprinids sat in the streams from late October to May (roach 40 %, white bream 55 %), while pike and perch mostly did not (< 2 %). — [DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/inter-and-size-specific-patterns-of-fish-seasonal-migration-betwe/) [abs]
- **Slow drift (pumpkinseed):** a mean of ~30 m/day and up to 2.5 km downstream. Home ranges in streams were "mainly pools with overhanging trees", with non-random core sites. — [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/501750/reporting/it) [abs]
- **Canal residents (largemouth, S):** some canal bass stayed in the canal for 203 and 441 days with almost no lake visits. Most fish moved often between lake and canal. — [NCWRC 2021](https://ncwildlife.gov/media/3109/download) [ft]

**Predators at stream mouths and in the channel**
- **Pike at confluences [EU, salmon smolts in a large lake]:**
  - "Tagged pike aggregated at river‐to‐lake confluences during the peak of the smolt run".
  - "Heavy mortality occurred at the river‐to‐lake confluences, but was lower in the main body of the lake".
  - Smolts entered the lake "in the late evening after dusk, possibly as a predator‐avoidance behaviour".
  - — [J. Fish Biol. 2018, openpolar record](https://openpolar.no/Record/crwiley:10.1111%2Fjfb.13676) [snip; certificate error on fetch]
- **Bass at culverts [S]:** largemouth bass "were likely attracted to the causeway culverts due to relatively deeper water, variable water flow, forage fish availability, and proximity to rip-rap habitat". Angler harvest was suspected for tagged bass last heard near a culvert. — [NCWRC 2021](https://ncwildlife.gov/media/3109/download) [ft]
- **Bass at a bridge [non-NE]:** smallmouth in the Lake Washington Ship Canal "strongly selected the bridge as well as nearshore overwater structures". Milling salmon smolts at a bridge were exposed to structure edges that bass used. — [search summary of WSDOT/USFWS report](https://trid.trb.org/View/884617) [snip]
- **Piscivores rarely winter in the streams** where their prey shelters: < 2 % of pike and perch. — [DTU Orbit](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/inter-and-size-specific-patterns-of-fish-seasonal-migration-betwe/) [abs]

**Seasonal use**
- **Spring, at 2–10 °C:** pike moved through the creek to marsh spawning from mid-March. Many were running ripe, and there was little overlap with carp. — [Chizinski 2016](https://rpbcwd.org/files/0c215a59d/chizinskiEtAl2016_differentMigratoryStrategies_Pike_Carp.pdf) [ft]
- **Spring to early summer:** bluegill, bullhead, crappie, largemouth, perch, sucker, pumpkinseed and minnows were all trapped moving through the same creek (Table 2). — same source [ft]
- **Summer (June–July nights):** sucker commuting. — [NENA](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-28-4/11-Marcy-Quay.shtml) [abs]
- **Summer and fall nights:** white perch moved upriver nightly. — [AFS 2012](https://afs.confex.com/afs/2012/webprogram/Paper10211.html) [snip]
- **Autumn:** perch shifted between lakes (Okoboji; Seattle Sept–Oct). — [UNI](https://scholarworks.uni.edu/pias/vol90/iss3/8) [abs]; [WRIA 8](https://www.govlink.org/watersheds/8/committees/2305/WRIA8_predator_presentation_May-18-2023.pdf) [ft]
- **Winter refuge [EU]:** cyprinids moved into streams to escape predators (fish and cormorants). Migration bursts in December and January were tied to environmental cues, and a 2011 Oikos study links the migration to temperature. — [Lund LUP](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/561161a1-6fdb-4e82-b31f-9f47492e523f) [abs]
- **Winter O₂ at an inlet (Rice Lake, Wisconsin):**
  - **How the inlet helped:** surface agitation upstream aerated the water and gave "a steady trickle of dissolved oxygen". Flow **thinned the ice at the inlet from 19 to 10 cm**, leaving "barely enough water depth for small fish to move upstream".
  - **How little was left:** only **40–45 % of the circulating water area** kept any O₂, and fish were held to **< 15 % of their ice-free living area**.
  - — [UW digital collection](https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/ADU4BXBYGGPCCO8M/text/AKEFXKD6A3ASGD86); a second UW item, ["Drought reduced water depth in Rice Creek"](https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/AUDIZPPM673S2I82/text/ANZBA2VFX7O2LM8R), covers the same system [snip; I did not open either page]
  - The Cooper & Washburn inlet crowding is already in `habitat3d.md` §11.

**Passage limits (depth, velocity, low flow)**
- **Depth:**
  - Pike began their run when Riley Creek was **58 cm** deep at ~2 °C. The creek is ≤ ~1 m deep and **"often goes dry in the summer"**. — [Chizinski 2016](https://rpbcwd.org/files/0c215a59d/chizinskiEtAl2016_differentMigratoryStrategies_Pike_Carp.pdf) [ft]
  - Riffles "are the shallowest habitats, and thus the most likely to impede fish passage at low flow levels". (This is salmonid work in California streams.) — [Grantham 2013](https://nature.berkeley.edu/granthamlab/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Grantham_2013_RRA.pdf) or [CA SWAMP habitat-connectivity talk](https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/water_issues/programs/swamp/docs/cabw2014/twelve_holmes_habitat_connect.pdf) [snip; the search summary did not say which of the two]
- **Stranding (Rideau, Ontario):** a tagged fish "just couldn't get out until the water levels came up … By mid-April, it was able to scoot out". Most fish wintered in deeper water and are "pretty good at sensing changes in water level". — [Capital Current](https://capitalcurrent.ca/fish-rideau-canal/) [abs]
- **Velocity, pumpkinseed:** fish crossed "long shallow rapids (up to 0.7 m/s)" moving upstream. — [CORDIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/501750/reporting/it) [abs]
- **Velocity, pumpkinseed relative to others:** pumpkinseed had the **weakest swimming performance of eight freshwater species** tested. — [Univ. Girona study record](https://www.recercat.cat/handle/10256/18707) [snip]

  - — [UConn EEB article](https://digitalcommons.lib.uconn.edu/eeb_articles/23) [snip]; [Frontiers Mar. Sci. 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1721762/full) [snip]

### Inferences
- **[inf] Three movement modes for the brook.** The sim should use:
  - (a) **transit:** a fish swims the 150 m in minutes and leaves the other mouth;
  - (b) **sojourn:** it stays days to months in the mouth pool, the run or the margins;
  - (c) **commute:** a nightly return trip.
  - At an ordinary cruise of ~1 body length per second, a 30-cm fish crosses 150 m in about 8 minutes. Transit should therefore be short, and the time spent in the brook should come from sojourns.
- **[inf] Starting values for the time spent in the brook:**

  | Who | Behaviour in the brook | Length of stay |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pickerel at spawning (2–10 °C) | hold in the edges and the mouth pool | 1–4 weeks |
  | Pickerel, summer | ambush at the **Upper mouth pool and the Lower outfall**, timed to prey traffic | |
  | Stream-spawning smallmouth males | nest in or near the run and pool tail | late May through fall [Millers Lake] |
  | Other smallmouth | visit the mouth pool | hours to days |
  | Sunfish | drift in during spring and stay in the pool all summer (`habitat3d.md` §11, Lievense) | |
  | Suckers | | overnight |
  | Bullhead | at night | hours to days |

- **[inf] Predator hotspots.**
  - The 1–1.2 m mouth pool is the deepest, flowing, structured water near the brook, the same cues that drew Mattamuskeet bass to culverts.
  - Raise pickerel and bass ambush there in the hours when prey traffic peaks: dusk and night for suckers, and the spring pulses of young sunfish.
  - Because transit prey enter after dusk, predation at the mouth should be highest at dusk.
- **[inf] Passage gating by flow.**
  - The brook's riffles (~0.3 m) gate passage. A drop in flow should first stop large deep-bodied fish (bass, bullhead, white perch, big suckers) at the riffles, then everything.
  - The pool keeps fish but traps them until flow returns, as at Rideau and in the alewife drought year.
  - Suggested rules:
    - no passage of a riffle when depth < ~1.5× the fish's body depth;
    - passage by burst only when riffle velocity exceeds the size-class prolonged speed (~0.4–0.6 m/s);
    - pumpkinseed cannot cross above ~0.7 m/s.
  - These are rule-of-thumb inferences, not measured thresholds for these species.
- **[inf] Winter.**
  - Expect few piscivores in the brook in winter (< 2 % in the European analogue).
  - In late-winter hypoxia the riffle-aerated outflow of Upper (`habitat3d.md` §11) should draw all species. The inlet example shows how small the refuge is: thinner ice and barely enough depth.

### Gaps
- No study of these species reports **how long a fish stays** in a short (~150 m) connecting stream between two lakes, measured in hours.
- No North American study quantifies **predator aggregation at a lake–stream mouth** for pickerel or smallmouth. The pike–smolt study is European, and the abstract could not be fetched (certificate error).
- **Ucrit or burst speeds** for smallmouth, yellow perch, white sucker, pumpkinseed, white perch, pickerel and bullhead were not retrieved:
  - the DFO swimming-performance database (Katopodis & Gervais 2016) is ~550 pages and was not read;
  - the FishXing species tables returned HTTP 404.
- No minimum passable **depth** for these species was found. The 1.5× body-depth rule above is an inference.
- No Hot Brook discharge or low-flow riffle depths are available. The dry-year brook depth is the owner's observation only.

## Q3. Home range and dispersal: how often a fish re-settles, and the resident vs roamer split (beyond `habitat3d` §6)

### Takeaway
Across many species, movement is **leptokurtic**:
- **Roughly two-thirds** of a population is **stationary** and **one-third mobile** (160 datasets, 62 river species).
- **Home ranges grow with water-body size** as well as fish size (71 studies, 66 species).
- **Individuals switch modes:** 28 of 51 sculpin switched between stationary and mobile.
- **Re-settlement is commonest in autumn and spring.**

For Hot Brook this means a resident majority with a minority of roamers. Bigger fish and the bigger lake (Upper) get larger ranges, and roamers do most of the inter-lake crossing.

### Cited Findings
- **Radinger & Wolter 2014** (*Fish and Fisheries*) [rivers]:
  - Refitted leptokurtic dispersal kernels for **160 datasets from 71 studies on 62 riverine species**. "A share of one third and two thirds emerged as a general pattern of the mobile and stationary component."
  - Dispersal distance rises with **fish length, caudal-fin aspect ratio, river size and time**.
  - The FIDIMO model uses a stationary share of **0.67** as its default.
  - — [HU Berlin dissertation](https://edoc.hu-berlin.de/18452/17719) [snip]; [GRASS r.fidimo manual](https://grass.osgeo.org/grass64/manuals/addons/r.fidimo.py.html) [snip]
- **Example two-group kernel (brown trout, river)** [sur]: **71.1–87.5 %** of fish were stationary, with mean moves of **20.7–45.4 m** against **229.4–540.5 m** for the mobile group. — [PeerJ 5730](https://peerj.com/articles/5730.pdf) [snip; this comes from a search summary and I am not certain it is the right source; verify before use]
- **Banded sculpin:** **28 of 51 fish switched** between stationary and mobile behaviour, so the split is not a fixed trait for every fish. — [Wells et al. (in press)](https://sites.tntech.edu/jperkin/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2015/07/Wells_et_al_In_Press.pdf) [snip]
- **Woolnough, Downing & Newton 2009** (*Ecol. Freshw. Fish* 18:83–91): **71 studies, 66 species, 5 continents**. Home range **increased with water-body size regardless of shape**. "Body size and water body size" together predicted range better than body size alone. — [USGS UMESC abstract](https://umesc.usgs.gov/documents/publications/2009/newton_a_2009.html) [snip]
- **Lake-chain re-settlement rates:**
  - Bluegill **3–36 % a year**, black crappie **0–92 % a year**, with no size effect. — [Parsons & Reed 2005](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1577/M04-021.1) [snip]
  - Seattle perch: **80 %** moved lakes and **most did not return**. — [WRIA 8](https://www.govlink.org/watersheds/8/committees/2305/WRIA8_predator_presentation_May-18-2023.pdf) [ft]
- **Resident vs dispersive types in one population:**
  - white perch, Long Island ("both residential and dispersive individuals") — [AFS 2012](https://afs.confex.com/afs/2012/webprogram/Paper10211.html) [snip];
  - smallmouth, Millers Lake (lake residents vs stream migrants, with lifetime fidelity of males) — [QUBS](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/divergent-life-histories-among-smallmouth-bass-inhabiting-a-connected-river-lake-system) [abs];
  - pike, Riley Creek (< 20 % migrate) — [Chizinski 2016](https://rpbcwd.org/files/0c215a59d/chizinskiEtAl2016_differentMigratoryStrategies_Pike_Carp.pdf) [ft].
- **Fidelity to the habitat of capture (largemouth, S):** lake-tagged bass mostly used the lake, and canal-tagged bass the canal. One basin's fish never used the other basin over ~20 months. **Mean linear swim distance was 203.7 km per fish** (range 0.8–1,081.6 km) over the study, so the fish were highly mobile within the habitats they used. — [NCWRC 2021](https://ncwildlife.gov/media/3109/download) [ft]
- **White perch home range (tidal, S):** **0.11 km²** for site-faithful fish, with core use ≤ 10 % of the range. — [TAFS 2009](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1577/T08-176.1) [snip]
- **Muskellunge [sur, NB river]:** 691 angler-tagged fish, 9.3 % recaptured. "Most fish … settle into discrete summer home ranges with little or no movement", while some moved long distances in both directions. — [AFS muskellunge management volume](https://fisheries.org/?p=65735) [snip]

### Inferences
- **[inf] Default split for species without data:**
  - **67 % resident** (home range as in `habitat3d` §6);
  - **33 % roamer** (range 3–10× larger; does most of the crossings);
  - a **small chance of switching mode** each season, highest in **autumn and spring**. That timing follows the autumn space expansion (perch, white perch, pike) and the spring spawning moves.
- **[inf] Scale ranges by water-body size (Woolnough 2009).**
  - Upper (369 ha) should allow somewhat larger ranges than Lower (274 ha).
  - The brook (~0.12 ha) holds only small sojourn ranges: the pumpkinseed "pools with overhanging trees" pattern.
- **[inf] Species where the split is documented:**
  - smallmouth (stream-spawner vs lake-resident, fixed for life in males);
  - pike as the pickerel surrogate (10–20 % spring migrants);
  - white perch (residents vs dispersers);
  - white sucker (large-fish commuters).
  - Use a heritable or fixed individual trait for smallmouth males. For the others use a per-season draw.

### Gaps
- No **site-fidelity or resident-share figures from small northern lakes** were found for chain pickerel, white perch, brown bullhead or yellow perch. The lake-scale figures above are bluegill and crappie (Minnesota), estuarine white perch, southern largemouth and European cyprinids.
- **Not retrieved:**
  - the exact Radinger & Wolter kernel parameters and the Woolnough 2009 regression coefficients (paywalled; the USGS publication page returned HTTP 500);
  - Minns (1995), the home-range allometry for lake and river fishes.
- No **annual displacement distribution** (distances moved per year) was found for any of these species in a lake setting.
