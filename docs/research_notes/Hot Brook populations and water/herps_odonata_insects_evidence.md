# Herps, Odonata, aquatic and fall-in insects of the Hot Brook lakes (Danforth/Weston), 2026-10-21

A research sub-search's report, recorded by the coordinator (the sub-search wrote no file). It sits alongside
`full_biota_and_processes.md` and `plankton_microbes_plants_evidence.md`.

## Method and limits

- **Blocked sources.** maine.gov returned 403 to every request: MDIFW species pages, MARAP township maps, SGCN, and MDDS (now
  redirecting to maine.gov). None of these was bypassed; MDIFW statements here come from search snippets.
- **Search budget.** It ran out before these were reached:
  - Burian & Gibbs, "Mayflies of Maine";
  - the UMaine black-fly bulletins;
  - Maine DEP biomonitoring of Baskahegan Stream;
  - Hunter et al. 1999 (the book and its MARAP maps).

  So the absence calls below are strong data evidence, not atlas-confirmed.
- **Data used:**
  - the **iNaturalist API** (verifiable records only, radius from 45.62 N, 67.92 W; Washington County place 127, Aroostook 1646, Maine 17);
  - the **GBIF API**, including the New Brunswick Museum Odonata vouchers from the MDDS era, with exact localities at both lakes.
- **Caution.** iNaturalist counts show presence and observer effort, not abundance. No Maine herp densities were found.
- **Links:**
  - iNaturalist herps within 40 km:
    https://api.inaturalist.org/v1/observations/species_counts?lat=45.62&lng=-67.92&radius=40&iconic_taxa=Amphibia,Reptilia&verifiable=true
  - GBIF Odonata box:
    https://api.gbif.org/v1/occurrence/search?decimalLatitude=45.5,45.75&decimalLongitude=-68.1,-67.79&orderKey=789&country=US

## 1. Herps

Counts are iNaturalist records within 40 km / in Washington County / in Aroostook County.

### Frogs and toads

- **American bullfrog: 27 / 81 / 37, common.**
  - Breeds in permanent lakes; tadpoles overwinter 1–3 years in the north (gk).
  - The top frog predator: insects, crayfish, frogs (the mink frog's main predator), small fish, ducklings.
  - Eaten by snapping turtles, herons, otters and mink; bass and pickerel take the tadpoles (gk).
- **Green frog: 30 / 177 / 132, abundant.** Lake margins; tadpoles usually overwinter once (gk).
- **Mink frog: 2 / 3 / 60.** A northern species (263 records north of 45.3 N).
  - Highly aquatic, in lily-pad coves; prefers cold, oxygenated water; tadpoles ~1 year.
  - Adults eat invertebrates; tadpoles eat algae and detritus.
  - Predators: bullfrogs mainly, plus herons, raccoons and garter snakes.
- **Northern leopard frog: 35 / 41 / 90** (the most-recorded herp within 40 km). Breeds in shallow marsh and lake fringes, feeds in wet
  meadows, overwinters underwater in lakes (gk).
- **Pickerel frog: 22 / 89 / 11.** Cool, clear water and brooks; toxic skin (gk).
- **Wood frog (26 / 87 / 85) and spring peeper (17 / 188 / 58).** Breed in fishless vernal pools and beaver-pond edges, not open lake;
  explosive early breeders after ice-out (gk).
- **Gray treefrog: 8 / 61 / 16.** Not at its range edge here: it calls north to North Lake, Carleton County, NB (45.82 N, 67.72 W), and the
  NB range is expanding (McAlpine, Pratt & Terhune 2009, *Can. Field-Nat.* 123:309). Choruses at beaver ponds.
- **American toad: 26 / 122 / 115.** Breeds on shallow lake margins (gk).

### Salamanders

- **Eastern newt: 7 / 61 / 22** (one within 9 km).
  - Aquatic adults in vegetated ponds, lake coves and slow streams.
  - Tetrodotoxin deters most fish (sunfish excepted).
  - Eats insects, small crustaceans, molluscs, and amphibian eggs and young; its larvae are the most heavily eaten stage.
- **Spotted (18 / 126 / 62) and blue-spotted (7 / 23 / 35) salamanders,** and unisexual *Ambystoma*. Vernal-pool breeders; their larvae are
  largely excluded from lakes with fish (gk).
- **Four-toed salamander:** thinly recorded (1 in Washington County, 1 within 80 km); nests in sphagnum margins (gk).
- **Northern two-lined salamander: 5 / 7 / 29** (one within 9 km). Brook margins; larvae 2–3 years in the stream (gk). **The brook
  salamander for Hot Brook.**
- **Northern dusky salamander:** 21 within 80 km, none within 40 km; seeps; unconfirmed.
- **Spring salamander:** only 3 records within 80 km; needs cold headwater springs, so **probably absent**.
- **Eastern red-backed salamander: 10 / 60 / 36,** terrestrial.

### Turtles

- **Painted turtle: 21 / 96 / 17.**
- **Snapping turtle: 21 / 114 / 30.**
- **Wood turtle: 2 / 4 / 24.**
  - State Special Concern.
  - Uses slow streams with sand or silt beds, pools and woody debris; nests on sandbars; radio-tracked turtles moved a median ~2.3 miles
    along streams.
  - Northern Maine populations "robust" (UMaine Presque Isle).
  - Plausible on the Baskahegan and Mattawamkeag systems; possible but unconfirmed at Hot Brook.
- **Absent:** Blanding's (southern York and Cumberland only), spotted (north only to about Farmington and Bangor), eastern musk ("southern
  and central"). None has an iNaturalist record north of 45.3 N in the east.

### Snakes

- **Common watersnake: probably absent** (the human's belief holds at the level of the data reached).
  - No iNaturalist records in eastern Maine north of 45.35 N.
  - The northernmost eastern records are Big Lake Twp (45.18 N), Wesley (45.15 N) and Calais (45.15 N), so the lakes lie ~50 km past the
    known edge.
  - None in Aroostook or New Brunswick.
  - Confirm on the MARAP map when maine.gov is reachable.
- **Absent:** eastern ribbonsnake. **Probably absent:** milksnake, DeKay's brownsnake.
- **Common garter snake: 25 / 178 / 144.** Eats frogs, toads and fish.
- **Terrestrial:** red-bellied snake (10 / 78 / 5), smooth green snake (4 / 37 / 3), ring-necked snake (5 / 28 / 0).

## 3. Aquatic insects and spiders (iNaturalist, within 60 km unless stated)

- ***Hexagenia limbata*: 7 within 60 km** (Maine 337, incl. Washington and Aroostook counties).
  - Maine records span weeks 22–37, **peaking in weeks 26–30 (late June – late July)**.
  - Nymphs dig U-shaped burrows in mud and eat detritus; ~2-year cycle in cold northern lakes.
  - Adults don't feed; emerging subimagos are eaten by fish, dragonflies and birds.
- **Other mayflies:** *Ephemera*, *Callibaetis*, *Leptophlebia*, *Paraleptophlebia*, *Siphlonurus*, *Ephemerella*, *Stenacron
  interpunctatum*, *Rhithrogena* (*Caenis* likely, too small to be recorded). Maine records run weeks 13–49, bulk 18–36.
- **Caddisflies:**
  - lentic and plant-associated: *Ptilostomis*, *Banksiola dossuaria*, *Platycentropus*, *Nemotaulius*, *Limnephilus*, *Mystacides*,
    *Ceraclea*, *Leptocerus*, *Triaenodes*;
  - riffle and net-spinning: *Hydropsyche*, *Cheumatopsyche*, *Macrostemum zebratum*, *Chimarra*, *Neureclipsis*, *Rhyacophila*,
    *Helicopsyche*;
  - cold-season limnephilids: *Glyphopsyche*, *Psychoglypha*, *Pycnopsyche*, *Frenesia*;
  - peak weeks 22–33, an autumn limnephilid bump ~week 37.
- **Stoneflies (brook riffles):** *Acroneuria abnormis*, *Paragnetina media*, *Pteronarcys biloba*, *Taeniopteryx*, Capniidae,
  Isoperlinae, Perlodinae. Winter stoneflies weeks 7–16 (Feb–April), summer species weeks 20–31.
- **Megaloptera:** *Nigronia serricornis* 9, *Chauliodes pectinicornis* 6, *C. rastricornis* 3, *Corydalus cornutus* 5, *Sialis* 4.
- **Aquatic true bugs:**
  - water striders: *Aquarius remigis* 11, *Metrobates*, *Trepobates*, *Limnoporus dissortis*, *Gerris*;
  - *Mesovelia*;
  - giant water bugs: *Lethocerus americanus* 4, *Belostoma flumineum*;
  - *Ranatra fusca*, *Notonecta*;
  - Corixidae likely but unrecorded.
- **Water beetles:**
  - diving beetles: *Dytiscus harrisii*, *D. verticalis*, *Acilius semisulcatus*, *Ilybius*, *Neoscutopterus*;
  - *Dineutus* (whirligigs);
  - water scavengers: *Hydrophilus ovatus*, *Hydrobius*.
- **Blackflies:** *Simulium* (1 nearby). Maine peak weeks 16–23 (late April – early June). Densest larvae below lake outlets (Richardson &
  Mackay 1991; not retrieved — gk).
- ***Chaoborus*:** likely but unrecorded locally (Maine: *C. punctipennis* 37, *C. albatus* 3).
  - Eats copepods and cladocerans; fish are its main predator and push the larvae deeper.
  - In a mixed 5.5-m lake the sediment is its day refuge (gk).
- **Sponges and their followers** (in Maine, not recorded locally):
  - sponges: *Spongilla lacustris* 34, *Ephydatia muelleri* 1;
  - spongillaflies: *Sisyra nigra* 5, *S. vicaria* 1, *Climacia areolaris* 3;
  - the aquatic moth *Acentria ephemerella* 2.
- **Fishing spiders:**
  - ***Dolomedes tenebrosus* within 9 km of the lakes (2 records)**; *D. triton* at Butterfield Landing Road, Orient (~10 km north,
    2021); *Pisaurina mira* 7.
  - *D. triton* hunts lake edges and docks, taking aquatic insects, fallen insects, water striders, tadpoles and small fish.

## 4. Land insects that fall into the water

- **Ants (within 80 km):** *Camponotus novaeboracensis* 40, *C. pennsylvanicus* 29, *C. herculeanus* 9; invasive *Myrmica rubra* 13; many
  *Formica* and *Lasius*.
- **Cicadas:** the dog-day cicada *Neotibicen canicularis* 31 (July–August); *Okanagana noveboracensis* 3, *O. rimosa* 1. No periodical
  cicadas in Maine (gk).
- **Caterpillars and moths (60 km):** hickory tussock 68, fall webworm 26, eastern tent caterpillar 17, hemlock looper 12, spongy moth 9,
  forest tent caterpillar 7. May beetles (*Phyllophaga*) at the lakes.
- **Japanese beetle:** overwhelmingly southern Maine; none local (nearest: the Washington County coast; a few 2026 records in Aroostook).
- **Spruce budworm:** elevated in winter 2025–26 on ~83,000 acres; ~70,000 acres treated 3–8 June 2026, mostly northern Aroostook; no
  outbreak noted near Danforth.
