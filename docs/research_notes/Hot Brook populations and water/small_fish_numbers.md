# How many small fish? Absolute numbers of small-bodied and small-sized fish in small, shallow, northern lakes and their streams (for the Hot Brook true-scale census)

Purpose: the existing design census (`fish_numbers_and_new_species.md`) counts only fish ≥ 7.5 cm (≈ 2,500/ha, ≈ 100 kg/ha). This note looks for numbers *below* that size and in small-bodied species. It does not repeat what is already there: Cassidy Lake, Moe Pond, South Twin, Schneider's perch lakes, Oneida model, Phillips et al. 2007 life history, EMAP counts, and Keast & Harker's "80–90% of biomass at ≤ 2.5 m".

Conventions:
- fish/acre × 2.471 = fish/ha; lb/acre × 1.121 = kg/ha; 1 fish/m² = 10,000 fish/ha.
- Every conversion, mean weight and ratio I computed is marked **(derived)**.
- "Via search summary" = the number came from a search engine's digest of a page I could not open myself. Everything else I read in the primary text (PDFs were text-extracted locally with `pdftotext`).
- Region tags: [ME] Maine; [NE] New England/New York; [ON] [QC] [NB] [NL] [MB] Canadian provinces; [WI] [MI] [MN] [IA] upper Midwest; [S] southern US; [EU] Europe (outside the requested region; used only where nothing northern was found).
- Hot Brook figures used in derived examples: Upper 369 ha + Lower 274 ha = **643 ha**; the connecting brook ≈ 150 m × 8 m = **≈ 1,200 m²** (derived).

## Q1. Banded killifish in lake shallows: density, school size, share of catches, whole-population estimates

### Takeaway
I found **no published density per m² for banded killifish anywhere**. The only absolute count is a Schnabel mark–recapture estimate of **≈ 20,600 killifish (95% CI 12,500–40,200)** in the occupied shallows of two small ponds in Newfoundland's Indian Bay watershed in August. The same sites gave only 1,209 in July, when the water was cooler and the fish less active. Shoals of **25–40 fish** are typical, and killifish make up **74–93% of shallow (≤ 1.2 m) seine catches** where they are common. Treat them as a dense, patchy, margin-only population whose size has to be set from habitat area × an assumed density.

### Cited Findings
- **Indian Bay watershed, Newfoundland [NL]** (Chippett 2004, summarized by COSEWIC). — [COSEWIC 2014 status report](https://wildlife-species.canada.ca/species-risk-registry/virtual_sara/files/cosewic/sr_Banded%20Killifish_2014_e.pdf)
  - Snorkelling and/or fyke netting at 22 sites; killifish found in three lakes (four sites): Third Pond, Backup Pond, and a single fish in Indian Bay Big Pond.
  - **Schnabel estimate in August (water at its seasonal maximum): 20,569 individuals (95% CI 12,529–40,201).**
  - **An earlier July estimate at the same sites: 1,209 (95% CI 619–3,423).**
  - This is "the only quantitative estimate of population size" for Newfoundland. The occupied areas "are typically shallow and have extensive vegetation"; fyke catches suggest some nocturnal movement into deeper rocky habitat.
  - The area of the occupied habitat is not given in the COSEWIC text, so no density can be derived.
  - Fyke-net catch per ~24-h set (1999): **Indian Bay 42, Loch Leven 29, Freshwater Pond 23** fish per set.
  - "Schools of approximately **25–40 fish** could easily be observed in Loch Leven."
- **Movement, Minnesota [MN]** (Valley et al. 2010, via COSEWIC): mean displacement of marked-and-recaptured killifish was **228 m and 355 m** between May and August in two years; some crossed the whole lake within a season. In Indian Bay they stayed in parts of two ponds despite no barriers. — [COSEWIC 2014](https://wildlife-species.canada.ca/species-risk-registry/virtual_sara/files/cosewic/sr_Banded%20Killifish_2014_e.pdf)
- **Shoal behaviour in a New Brunswick lake [NB]** (banded killifish, golden shiner and white sucker in mixed and single-species shoals):
  - shoals met another shoal about **every 1.1 min**, and an encounter lasted **3.7 s** on average;
  - shoal size affected encounter duration; body length and species (not shoal size) decided whether a fish switched shoals;
  - **ten entire shoals collected were assorted by species and by body length.** — [Krause et al. 2000, *Proc. R. Soc. B* 267:2011–2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1690773)
- **Shoal size rises with density** in killifish, both in the lab and the field: more fish → larger and more numerous shoals. — [Couzin et al. 2005 record](https://catalog.comses.net/publications/135027) (via search summary)
- **Share of seine catches:**
  - Presque Isle Bay, Lake Erie [PA, Great Lakes]: killifish were 86% of all fish seined at four sites ≤ 1.2 m deep (by site **86, 92, 93 and 74%**). Gear: a 1.2 × 9.1 m beach seine, three hauls per site per period, June–October 2003–04. No catch per haul or swept area was reported, and the authors say the results "do not reflect their overall abundance" in the bay. — [Phillips, Ewert & Speares 2007](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-14-2/18-Phillips.shtml)
  - A river setting (Haskell & Tiffin 2011): killifish were **7–43%** of fish caught in seining surveys 2004–08. — [USFWS Ecological Risk Screening Summary](https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Ecological-Risk-Screening-Summary-Banded-Killifish.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Newfoundland distribution update** (2013–18 surveys with fyke nets, pole seines, electrofishing): more locations than previously known, but no abundance or density figures. — [Sargent, Dalley & Osborne 2020, *Can. Field-Nat.* 134:307–315](https://canadianfieldnaturalist.ca/cfn/index.php/cfn/article/download/2373/2583)

### Inferences
- **The July-vs-August contrast (1,209 vs 20,569) is a warning, not a population change.** A 17-fold difference in a month is far more than reproduction could add to catchable sizes. It most likely reflects catchability (killifish are inactive below ~12 °C and hard to catch) plus mark–recapture bias. A census should use the warm-season figure.
- **A bounded guess for Hot Brook (assumption-driven, not measured).**
  - Suitable habitat is sand or mud with submerged plants at < 1 m depth. Suppose that is 1–5% of each lake's area: 3.7–18 ha in Upper and 2.7–14 ha in Lower (derived).
  - Suppose 0.3–1 killifish/m² inside that habitat. This is my assumption: it puts killifish at or slightly above the all-species littoral densities in Q3 (≈ 0.2–1 fish/m²), consistent with their 74–93% share of shallow seine catches.
  - That gives **≈ 20,000–300,000 killifish across the two lakes** (derived). The low end matches the one real estimate (≈ 20,000 in two small Newfoundland ponds).
  - EMAP all-ages nets put killifish at a median 1.7% of eastern-Maine lake catches (existing note). Nets fish poorly in water < 0.5 m, so that share is a floor.
- **For the sim:** killifish should be aggregate/school records, not individual agents.
  - Adults are 59–96 mm; only age-2+ fish cross 7.5 cm.
  - Shoals of 25–40 that sort by size and species, meet other shoals every minute or so, and grow larger with density are a good behavioural target.

### Gaps
- No killifish density per m² or per 100 m of shoreline was found for any lake, northern or southern.
- The habitat area behind the Indian Bay estimate is in Chippett's 2004 MUN thesis. The PDF exceeds the fetch tool's 10 MB limit and was not read.
- No killifish numbers from Lake Opinicon, Nova Scotia (Fritz & Garside 1974), Quebec or Maine were found.
- The Hoare et al. free-ranging shoal papers (*Oikos* 2000) and Godin (1985) were seen only as citations; field shoal-size distributions were not retrieved.

## Q2. Blacknose dace and other small stream fish: numbers per m² in 5–10 m streams

### Takeaway
In small northeastern streams, electrofishing depletion counts run **about 1–7 fish/m² of all species**. Blacknose dace alone averaged **≈ 1.1 fish/m²** in Catskill streams of ≈ 4–20 m width. At those densities Hot Brook's ≈ 1,200 m² brook would hold **~1,000–9,000 fish** (derived). No Maine, Maritime or Quebec blacknose dace density, and no juvenile fallfish or white sucker stream density, was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Catskill Mountains, New York [NE]** (Batavia Kill, East Kill, Broadstreet Hollow Brook; 1999–2004). — [Baldigo, Warren, Ernst & Mulvihill 2008, *NAJFM* 28:954–969](https://www.nemi.gov/methods/method_pdf/12959/)
  - **Method:** reaches about 20 mean stream widths long; 3-pass (sometimes 4-pass) backpack electrofishing depletion; surveys in **July or early August**. Mean channel widths before restoration were **11.1, 19.5, 7.8 and 3.6 m**.
  - **Pre-restoration mean indices** (my alignment of a scrambled table, checked against the percent changes quoted in the text):

    | Index | Value |
    |---|---|
    | Richness | 4.7 species |
    | **Total density** | **2.24 fish/m²** |
    | Total biomass | 7.71 g/m² |
    | Sculpin + dace group | 2.15 fish/m² |
    | **Eastern blacknose dace** | **1.08 fish/m²** |
    | Slimy sculpin | 0.94 fish/m² |
    | Longnose dace | 0.12 fish/m² |
    | Salmonids | 0.06 fish/m² |
    | Other species | 0.03 fish/m² |

  - After restoration, blacknose dace fell by a net 0.35 fish/m² (−32%). Community density changed by +1.0 fish/m² at one reach and −0.4 to −1.1 at the others.
- **Upper Speed River, Ontario [ON]** (pre-impoundment survey; quantitative removal sites). **11,126–74,765 fish/ha, i.e. 1.1–7.5 fish/m² (derived)**, all species. Blacknose dace was among the dominant species. Production was 15, 19 and 54 kg/ha/yr at three intensive sites. — [Mahon, Balon & Noakes 1979, *Env. Biol. Fish.* 4:219–244](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/BF00005480) (via search summary; the attribution of the abstract to this paper was inferred by the search engine)
- **Shenandoah National Park, Virginia [S]:** blacknose dace in pools, spring 1993, **23.2 and 22 fish/100 m² (0.22/m²)** in a low- and a high-ANC stream. — [Virginia Tech record](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/503118b9-52e7-46a1-841d-a8a9add5432f) (via search summary; the link-to-number attribution is uncertain)
- **Catamaran Brook, New Brunswick [NB]** (a salmon stream). Blacknose dace, lake chub, white sucker, creek chub, northern redbelly and finescale dace, common and golden shiner and threespine stickleback are all present. The report's density tables are scanned and salmonid-focused. Minnow traps in two pools per reach (27 May–25 Aug 1992) caught 86 blacknose dace and 124 lake chub, with no area basis. — [Cunjak et al. 1993, *Can. Tech. Rep. Fish. Aquat. Sci.* 1914](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/144358.pdf)
- **Snow Brook, Maine [ME]** (MDIFW): single-pass counts that "undoubtedly underestimate" numbers; no densities. — [MEFWCO Snow Brook letter](https://resiliencedata.nfwf.org/dataset/e7e746a1-62e4-4fd8-84aa-04c6fcb9f5b7/resource/2434e4a7-71f9-4ec1-9e5f-693357cc228a/download/mefwco-snow-brook-efish-7-3-18.doc.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Sedgeunkedunk Stream, Maine [ME]** (Penobscot tributary, dam removal): density, biomass and diversity rose at all upstream sites after the 2009 removal. The abstracts give no densities. — [Zydlewski et al. 2011, *River Res. Appl.*](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/distribution-and-abundance-stream-fishes-relation-barriers-implications-monitoring)

### Inferences
- **Brook census (derived), at 1,200 m²:**
  - at the Catskill community mean of 2.24 fish/m²: **≈ 2,700 fish**, of which ≈ 1,300 blacknose dace at 1.08/m²;
  - at the Speed River range of 1.1–7.5 fish/m²: **≈ 1,300–9,000 fish**.
- **Hot Brook's brook is not a Catskill trout stream.** It is the warm outlet of a 5.5-m lake. Expect:
  - **no sculpin**, which made up ~40% of the Catskill numbers;
  - more **juvenile fallfish, white sucker, yellow and white perch, and pumpkinseed in the 1.1-m pool and the run**;
  - dace mainly in the riffles.
  
  A design value of **1.5–3 fish/m² in summer (≈ 2,000–3,500 fish)** is defensible. Perhaps 30–60% of them would be blacknose dace, with the rest juvenile fallfish, sucker and lake fish using the pool. These shares are my judgement.
- **Almost all of these fish are < 7.5 cm.** Blacknose dace adults are 5.6–8.3 cm (existing note). Brook fish should therefore be school/aggregate records, with only adult fallfish, suckers, bass and pickerel as individual agents.

### Gaps
- No electrofishing depletion density for blacknose dace, juvenile fallfish or juvenile white sucker was found for Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, the Maritimes or Quebec. The Mahon (1979) species table and the Sedgeunkedunk species-level data (Coghlan's UMaine thesis is campus-only) were not reached.
- No density was found for a *lake-connecting* (outlet) stream specifically.

## Q3. Total littoral fish density in northern lakes (all species, all sizes), and how much is age-0 or < 7.5 cm

### Takeaway
Littoral fish standing stocks in northern lakes run **≈ 60–270 kg/ha** (southern Quebec, seine with efficiency correction; mean 135 kg/ha). That is above the whole-lake ~100 kg/ha because fish crowd the margins in summer.
- By number, the best efficiency-corrected littoral count averaged **≈ 10,000 fish/ha (≈ 1 fish/m²)** over four years. That is Spirit Lake, Iowa, a large eutrophic unstratified lake. It is dominated by age-0 perch and bluegill and is 4–5× lower in spring than in late summer.
- Stony boreal shores (Finland) hold **0.2–0.4 fish/m²** by electrofishing.
- Most fish in littoral counts are age-0 or small-bodied, and so **< 7.5 cm**.

### Cited Findings
- **10 southern Quebec lakes [QC]** (quantitative, replicated seining with efficiency correction):
  - **mean total littoral biomass 13.5 g/m² = 135 kg/ha; lake means 6.1–26.9 g/m² (61–269 kg/ha)**;
  - more biomass in late summer than early summer;
  - yellow perch, golden shiner and pumpkinseed occurred in every lake and averaged 57% of biomass.
  - Sources: [Pierce, Rasmussen & Leggett 1994, CJFAS 51:1128–1138](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/f94-112) (via search summary; cdnsciencepub returned 403). The 61–269 kg/ha, mean 135 kg/ha, figure is confirmed in the primary text of [Pierce et al. 2001](https://www.cfwru.iastate.edu/files/inline-files/pierce2001_20.pdf).
  - The companion paper derived seine-efficiency corrections from 26 littoral stations in the same lakes. — [Pierce et al. 1990, CJFAS 47:1004–1010](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/f90-115) (via search summary)
- **Spirit Lake, Iowa [IA]**. — [Pierce, Sexton, Pelham, Liao & Larscheid 2001, *NAJFM* 21:884–896](https://www.cfwru.iastate.edu/files/inline-files/pierce2001_20.pdf)
  - **Lake:** 2,229 ha; mean 5 m, max 7 m; eutrophic; no thermal stratification; summer peak 24–26 °C. Littoral defined as 0–3 m, ≈ 14% of the lake.
  - **Method:** night lampara beach seines of 6-mm bar mesh, swept areas 0.28–1.76 ha. Capture efficiency measured by mark–recapture averaged **48.1% ± 5.9%**, so all catches were doubled. Sampling July/Sept 1995, May/July/Sept 1996–97, monthly June–Sept 1998.
  - **Total density averaged 10,024 fish/ha (≈ 1.0 fish/m², derived) and 276 kg/ha.** Annual means were 14,389 (1995), 13,381 (1996), 12,300 (1997) and 4,154 fish/ha (1998).
  - **Yellow perch 6,356/ha = 63% of numbers** (15% of biomass). The next most numerous species ran 1,268, 834, 556 and 525/ha. Species-to-value matching in the scrambled table is uncertain beyond perch.
  - **Seasonal pattern:** "May and June total densities were less than those in subsequent months". The July–September rise "correspond[s] primarily to the appearance of the age-0 yellow perch and bluegill". **Bluegill densities in September averaged two to three orders of magnitude higher than in early months.** Perch density was lowest in spring and peaked in late summer or fall "when age-0 perch were abundant and large enough to be sampled".
- **Finnish boreal lakes [EU]:**
  - Stony littoral, electrofishing: total density **19.3 vs 32.7 fish/100 m² (0.19 vs 0.33/m²)** in regulated vs reference lakes. — [Sutela & Vehanen 2008, *Hydrobiologia*](https://wicri-demo.istex.fr/Wicri/Eau/explor/LotaV3/Site/fr/Main/Exploration/bibRecord.php?hk=000730) (via search summary)
  - Central Lake Päijänne, 15 stony sites (4,096 m²): 1,681 fish, **0.41 fish/m² and 3.5 g/m²**. — [Springer record BF00024472](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00024472) (via search summary; the search engine's mapping of this number to this record is uncertain)
- **Lake Opinicon, Ontario [ON]** (strip counts):
  - weedbeds had the highest biomass and the most species and year classes;
  - each habitat kept a fairly constant species and year-class composition through the season;
  - **fish biomass dropped in late summer as fish moved offshore**;
  - black crappie and brown bullhead made large diel moves between habitats.
  
  No densities appear on the summary page. Its listed year (2004) conflicts with volume 3 of *Env. Biol. Fish.*, which is 1978. — [Keast, Harker & Turnbull, *Env. Biol. Fish.* 3:173–184, QUBS summary](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/nearshore-fish-habitat-utilization-and-species-associations-in-lake-opinicon-ontario-canada)
- **A small Michigan marl lake [MI]** (divers' transect censuses covering nearly the whole littoral, May–Oct 1976):
  - **small bluegills (< 80 mm) stayed in vegetation year-round**, apparently to avoid predation;
  - fish overwintered in two patches of tall perennial vegetation in the deep littoral and dispersed when water reached about 20 °C.
  
  The abstract gives no densities. — [Hall & Werner 1977, *TAFS* 106:545–555](https://academic.oup.com/tafs/article/106/6/545/7895053)
- **A 6.2-ha Precambrian Shield lake, whole-lake piscicide census [ON, probably]:** **75% of fish production was in the first year of life.** A pre-treatment perch mark–recapture **underestimated density ≈ 9-fold** (production ≈ 8-fold), with the largest errors in the youngest age groups. Species: yellow perch (71% of production), fathead minnow, pearl dace, creek chub, golden shiner, brook trout. — [Chadwick 1976, *Env. Biol. Fish.* 1:13–60](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00761728). The abstract page does not name the province; the author was at the University of Guelph, Ontario.

### Inferences
- **A reasonable summer littoral density for Hot Brook is ≈ 0.5–2 fish/m² (5,000–20,000/ha) over the weedy and sandy margins, and ≈ 0.2–0.4/m² over open rock.**
  - Biomass would be ≈ 60–270 kg/ha of littoral. With mean masses of 5–15 g, the Quebec biomass converts to ≈ 0.4–5 fish/m² (derived, assumption-dependent).
- **Hot Brook is so shallow that "littoral" is much of the lake.** If its basins resemble Spirit Lake's 0–3 m band, the Spirit Lake number (~10,000 fish/ha averaged over seasons) is a usable order-of-magnitude anchor for most of Lower Hot Brook and the shallower half of Upper. The tea colour (Secchi 3.5–4.5 m) still allows weeds to ~3 m.
- **Counts are minima.** Seines catch about half (Spirit Lake 48%). Mark–recapture under-counts young fish up to ~9× (Chadwick). Whole-lake piscicide counts (Q4) are the most complete.
- **Composition by size:**
  - in late summer, littoral counts are dominated by age-0 fish of the year's strongest spawners (perch, sunfish);
  - in spring, by age-1 fish and small-bodied species.
  
  Almost all are < 7.5 cm. Hall & Werner's < 80 mm bluegills living permanently in weeds is the behaviour to give small fish.

### Gaps
- No per-m² littoral density from a New England, Adirondack, Lake Memphremagog, or Experimental Lakes Area lake was retrievable. The Pierce et al. 1994 tables (cdnsciencepub 403) and Keast's Opinicon strip-count densities (*Env. Biol. Fish.* 2:235–240 and 3:173–184, paywalled) were not reached.
- Weaver, Magnuson & Clayton (1997, CJFAS 54:2277–2289; northern Wisconsin macrophyte fish) and Lyons (1987, *Env. Biol. Fish.* 18:93–107; Sparkling Lake small littoral fishes) were found only as citations.
- No study split littoral counts at 7.5 cm.

## Q4. Whole-lake numbers that include small fish (piscicide/rotenone censuses, hydroacoustics, ELA minnow lakes)

### Takeaway
The one complete, all-species, all-sizes census of a soft-water northern lake found is Nebish Lake, Wisconsin (38 ha, alkalinity 15–19 ppm, rotenone in early October). It held **≈ 92,000 fish/ha and ≈ 236 kg/ha**:
- **≈ 83,500 small minnows/ha** (bluntnose minnow + mimic shiner, mean ≈ 1.8 g), which were 64% of biomass;
- **≈ 7,500 age-0 yellow perch/ha**;
- **≈ 590 age-0 smallmouth/ha**;
- under ~1,600/ha older fish of all other species.

Fish ≥ 7.5 cm were therefore only ≈ 0.7–1.8% of the fish present (derived). Pelagic hydroacoustics of boreal lakes (Norway, fish > 4 cm) give **34–4,720 fish/ha**, rising with phosphorus. No Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont or Adirondack small-fish census and no ELA absolute minnow count was retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- **Nebish Lake, Vilas County, Wisconsin [WI]**. — [Kempinger & Christenson 1978, WDNR Research Report 96](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/EIQCHSPXF35AK8O/E/file-19978.pdf)
  - **The lake:** 94 acres (38 ha); max depth 50 ft (15 m); narrow littoral; seepage water; infertile; alkalinity 15–19 ppm; a mixed warm-water community not managed as a "problem lake".
  - **Method:** rotenone on 3 October 1966, judged a complete kill.
    - Game and panfish were estimated by mark–recapture: fish marked in September, recaptures counted among the dead.
    - **Minnows were estimated from the minnow : age-0 perch ratio in pre-treatment seine hauls (11 : 1)** applied to the age-0 perch estimate. The post-kill ratio was 18 : 1 but was rejected. The authors call the minnow figure "open to question" but are sure the population was "very high".
  - **Standing crop, Table 7** (numbers include 89,500 minnows, 4,500 perch and 150 bass removed just before treatment):

    | Component | Number/acre | Fish/ha (derived) | lb/acre | kg/ha (derived) | Mean g (derived) |

    | **Minnows** (bluntnose minnow + mimic shiner) | **33,801** | **≈ 83,500** | 135.2 | 151.6 | ≈ 1.8 |
    | **Yellow perch age 0** | **3,034.5** | **≈ 7,500** | 24.0 | 26.9 | ≈ 3.6 |
    | Yellow perch age I+ | 93.2 | ≈ 230 | 5.0 | 5.6 | ≈ 24 |
    | **Smallmouth bass age 0** | **238** | **≈ 590** | 3.1 | 3.5 | ≈ 5.9 |
    | Smallmouth 4.3–7.2 / 7.3–8.9 / ≥ 9 in | 10.9 / 2.7 / 2.6 (text) | ≈ 27 / 7 / 6 | | | |
    | Rock bass age I+ | 56.5 | ≈ 140 | 5.9 | 6.6 | |
    | Walleye, all ages | 16.0 | ≈ 40 | 10.8 | 12.1 | |
    | Northern pike | 6.8 | ≈ 17 | 6.5 | 7.3 | |
    | White sucker | 0.9 | ≈ 2 | 13.1 | 14.7 | |
    | All non-minnows | 3,933.3 | ≈ 9,700 | 75.2 | 84.3 | |
    | **Grand total** | **37,334** | **≈ 92,250** | **210.4** | **≈ 236** | |

    Some rows of the OCR'd table are garbled. The totals, minnow, perch, bass, rock bass, walleye and pike rows match the report's text.
  - **Estimate precision:** the age-0 perch estimate rested on 16 recaptures of 2,250 marked (0.8%), and the age-0 bass estimate on a recovery of 2.3%. Both are imprecise.
  - **Minnow benchmarks cited in the report** (culture ponds, flagged): 100,000 bluntnose + fathead minnows/acre (50 lb) from a very infertile Michigan gravel-pit pond; up to 200,000 fathead/acre (328 lb) in Michigan rearing ponds; 21,000–40,000 bluntnose and 65,000 fathead/acre in Wisconsin ponds.
  - Only 10 of 126 Wisconsin complete reclamations from 1941 to 1968 had standing-crop estimates. "Assessment of the minnow population in other chemically treated Wisconsin waters was virtually ignored."
- **Long Lake, northern Wisconsin [WI]** (27.1 acres; derris-root rotenone; carp removal from a bass lake): **59,072 fish = 2,180/acre (≈ 5,390/ha, derived); 3,657 lb = 135 lb/acre (≈ 151 kg/ha)**. The size range counted is unknown. — [O'Donnell 1943, *TAFS*](https://arch.neicon.ru/xmlui/handle/123456789/3523395) (via search summary)
- **6.2-ha Precambrian Shield lake [ON]** (whole-lake piscicide): production 30.8 kg/ha/yr; perch 71% of production; 75% of production in the first year of life; mark–recapture underestimated perch density ~9×. Total numbers are not in the abstract. — [Chadwick 1976](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00761728)
- **Five small (< 8 ha), shallow (< 7 m) dystrophic bog lakes [WI]** (central mudminnow, yellow perch, golden shiner; mark–recapture plus CPUE, six censuses May–Oct over 3 years):
  - total **biomass 25–86 kg/ha** by census and lake group;
  - after a winterkill, mudminnows rose to 61 kg/ha; when perch recovered from 35 to 65 kg/ha, mudminnows fell to 7 kg/ha;
  - minimum sizes counted were ≈ 40 mm (mudminnow), 55 mm (perch) and 80 mm (golden shiner).
  
  Numbers per ha are not in the extracted text. — [Tonn 1985, *Ecology* 66:415–429](https://ualberta.scholaris.ca/handle/123456789/15889)
- **17 Norwegian lakes [EU]**. — [*Effects of Lake Productivity on Density and Size Structure of Pelagic Fish Estimated by Means of Echosounding in 17 Lakes in Southeast Norway*, PMC8152495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8152495/)
  - **Lakes and method:** 0.96–141 km², mostly oligotrophic, surveyed 1985–2018. 70/200 kHz echosounding of the pelagic, from 2 m down; **fish ≳ 4 cm detected**.
  - **Densities:** 34–4,720 fish/ha (1.0–232 kg/ha). Groups: 8 lakes 34–186/ha; 5 lakes 269–610/ha; 3 lakes 1,445–1,841/ha; mesotrophic Hemnessjøen 4,720/ha (smelt and cyprinids, modal length 7 cm).
  - **Productivity:** density vs total phosphorus r² = 0.72. Lakes over 1,200 fish/ha were dominated by fish < 20 cm.
  - **Sølensjøen, by size and season:** **26 fish > 8 cm/ha in June rising to 89 in September, and ≈ 600 fish < ~6 cm/ha in June.**
  - **Storsjøen Rendalen:** a threefold night-time rise from June to August 1985.
- **ELA acidified lakes 223, 302N and 302S [ON]:** small-bodied fish were followed only as trap-net CPUE.
  - Fathead minnow vanished from Lake 223 in 1980 at pH 5.6. Finescale dace vanished from Lake 223 in 1980 and from Lake 302S in 1986.
  - Pearl dace increased with acidification.
  
  No absolute numbers. — [MacIsaac 2024, Lakehead thesis](https://knowledgecommons.lakeheadu.ca/items/0fc6c9df-6a64-4398-b846-37c2bf7e5d2c)
- **Maine [ME] reclamations:**
  - Rupp & DeRoche (1965) is the only Maine census of numbers, weights and species in three small lakes (rotenone plus shoreline seining, random shallow sampling and SCUBA transect counts). It remains unread: the archive scan exceeds the 10 MB fetch limit. — [Behnke archive scan](https://arc.lib.montana.edu/robert-behnke/objects/2491-36-10.pdf)
  - Maine's 1959 rotenone reclamation of Prong Pond and the ≈ 13-acre Little Mud Pond (to remove illegally introduced yellow perch) failed; perch became established. No counts are given. — [MDIFW Little Mud Pond sheet](https://www.maine.gov/IFW/docs/lake-survey-maps/piscataquis/little_mud_pond.pdf) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Nebish is the best analogue of a whole soft-water lake counted down to minnow size.** Its lesson: **if a small-bodied, schooling minnow is present, it dominates the census by number (≈ 90%) and can dominate biomass.**
- **Hot Brook has no golden, common, mimic or bluntnose shiner on MDIFW's list.** Its small-bodied fish are banded killifish (margins), blacknose dace (brook) and young fallfish. So the Nebish minnow figure should not be copied. The **age-0 perch (≈ 7,500/ha in early October) and age-0 smallmouth (≈ 590/ha)** figures are directly usable as order-of-magnitude fall YOY values.
- **The ratio of all fish to fish ≥ 7.5 cm at Nebish in October is ≈ 55–145 : 1 (derived).** The denominator is either the age-I+ non-minnow rows summed (≈ 257/acre) or everything except minnows and age-0 perch and bass (≈ 660/acre).
  - Without the minnows, the ratio is still ≈ 6–15 : 1, driven by age-0 perch and bass.
- **Norwegian pelagic acoustics** give an independent "small vs large" ratio: in Sølensjøen in June, ≈ 600 fish < 6 cm/ha against 26 > 8 cm/ha, i.e. ≈ 23 : 1. That is pelagic only and in Europe.

### Gaps
- No hydroacoustic survey of a small Quebec, Ontario, Adirondack or New England lake with per-ha densities by size class was retrievable. Boisclair's Lac Croche papers (Comeau & Boisclair 1998; Gaudreau & Boisclair 2000) and the CJFAS "acoustic measures of lake community size spectra" (2015; 403) were seen only as citations.
- No ELA absolute minnow population estimates were found. Mills et al. 2000 (Lake 223) and Kidd et al. 2007 (Lake 260) abstracts were not retrievable.
- No Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont or Adirondack pond-reclamation count of minnows or YOY was found online.

## Q5. Fish size spectra: estimating the count of 3–7.5 cm fish from the count ≥ 7.5 cm

### Takeaway
Hydroacoustic size spectra of a boreal Manitoba system give, for fish 4–50 cm, number density per cm of length **n(L) ∝ L^−2.6 to −2.8**: about **3× fewer fish per halving of length**. Extended below 7.5 cm, this predicts **≈ 3.3–4.2 fish of 3–7.5 cm for every fish ≥ 7.5 cm**. That is the floor. Real lakes add age-0 cohorts and small-bodied species on top, so in late summer and autumn the measured ratio is far higher: ≈ 6–15 : 1 at Nebish without minnows, ≈ 55–145 : 1 with them, and ≈ 23 : 1 for pelagic fish in a Norwegian lake in June. Single-species spectra are not power laws: Cassidy perch fell 180-fold from 7.6 to 17.8 cm.

### Cited Findings
- **Lac du Bonnet reservoir, Manitoba [MB]** (84 km²; basins max 26 m and ~10–20 m; mesotrophic–eutrophic; summer ~22 °C; Secchi ~1.2 m). — [Pollom & Rose 2015, *PLoS ONE* 10:e0124799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4398392)
  - **Method:** 200 kHz split-beam, 11 daytime surveys in July–August 2011–12, tracked single targets binned in log₂ length classes (4–7.9, 8–15.9, 16–31.9, 32–63.9, > 64 cm).
  - **Result:** smaller fish were consistently more abundant than larger by a factor of **≈ 3 per halving of length**. Initial counts by class:
    - 2011: ≈ 1,679 / 460 / 162 / 52 / 14, so successive ratios of 3.65, 2.84, 3.12, 3.71 (derived);
    - 2012: ≈ 752 / 287 / 74 / 23 / 5, so ratios of 2.62, 3.88, 3.22, 4.6 (derived).
  - **Summer losses:** −0.067/day (smallest class) to −0.016/day (largest). Mean summer survival was 33%. For fish > 16 cm, winter loss was about −0.001/day (≈ 71% fall-to-summer survival).
  - **Recruitment** into the 4-cm class was minimal in both years. YOY were mostly below the detection floor.
- **Same system plus Nopiming lakes [MB]**. — [Wheeland 2014, MSc thesis, Memorial University](https://memorial.scholaris.ca/items/6ee6203e-39f7-4b29-8e02-33c05250925a)
  - **Method:** fish < 5 cm were excluded (indistinguishable from dense plankton). 5-cm bins from 5 to 50 cm; ln(count) regressed on ln(bin midpoint).
  - **Slopes:** **−2.66 ± 0.35 (2011) and −2.73 ± 0.29 (2012)**. Slopes were steeper where transects crossed a shallow bay with a large littoral zone (**−2.83 ± 0.21**) than where they did not (**−2.31 ± 0.19**). All R² were 0.87–0.99.
  - **Seasonal change:** spectrum height (abundance) declined over each summer. In one basin the slope also flattened, which means small fish declined more.
- **Theory and whole-community lake spectra:**
  - Multi-trophic lake food webs (bacteria to fish) have normalized biomass-spectrum slopes of **−1.14 to −1.19**, steeper than the theoretical −1, with transfer efficiencies of 1.0–3.6%. — [USU record](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/wats_facpub/1009) (via search summary; authorship and attribution uncertain)
  - Lake Superior's combined phytoplankton–zooplankton–prey-fish spectrum slope is **−1.113**. — [USGS, *A new look at the Lake Superior biomass size spectrum*](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/a-new-look-lake-superior-biomass-size-spectrum) (via search summary)
- **78 German lowland lakes [EU]** (multimesh gillnets): length-spectrum slopes were flatter (more big fish) in deep, less nutrient-rich lakes with more predators. Shallow, nutrient-rich lakes with few predators had more medium-sized fish and a shorter maximum length. — [Emmrich, Brucet, Ritterbusch & Mehner 2011, *Freshw. Biol.* 56:2316–2333](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC60622) (via search summary)
- **Single-species check [MI]:** Cassidy Lake perch ≥ 7.6 cm ≈ 1,830/ha vs ≥ 17.8 cm ≈ 10/ha. — existing note, citing [Schneider 1973, RR1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Arithmetic (derived).** If n(L) ∝ L^−b over 3 cm to the largest fish, then

  N(3–7.5 cm) / N(≥ 7.5 cm) = (7.5/3)^(b−1) − 1

  | b | Ratio |
  |---|---|
  | 2.58 (×3 per halving) | 3.25 |
  | 2.6 | 3.33 |
  | 2.7 | 3.75 |
  | 2.8 | 4.20 |
  | 3.0 | 5.25 |
  | 4.0 (the theoretical "−1" NBSS) | 14.6 |

  The Manitoba fish-only spectra (b ≈ 2.6–2.8) give **≈ 3.3–4.2**. A shallow, littoral-rich lake sits at the steep end (b ≈ 2.8 in Hay Bay), which argues for **≈ 4** in Hot Brook.
- **This is a floor, not the answer.** The acoustic spectra were measured in mid-summer above 4–5 cm and missed the YOY. Pulses of age-0 fish (Q6) and small-bodied species (Q4) sit on top of the power law. The empirical ratios are:
  - Nebish in October: ≈ 6–15 : 1 without minnows, ≈ 55–145 : 1 with them;
  - Sølensjøen pelagic in June: ≈ 23 : 1 (< 6 vs > 8 cm);
  - Schneider's perch lakes in fall (existing note): 2,471–5,328 YOY/acre against 14–22 adults ≥ 7 in per acre.
- **For Hot Brook (design values; derived from the existing 2,500 fish ≥ 7.5 cm per ha):**

  | Season | Fish 3–7.5 cm per ha | Ratio to ≥ 7.5 cm | Whole two lakes (643 ha) | Basis |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Spring, before hatch (May) | ≈ 2,500–10,000 | ≈ 1–4 | ≈ 1.6–6.4 million | Spectrum floor (≈ 4) as the upper bound. The lower bound allows for the pre-hatch trough, when only age-1 fish, killifish and dace fill this class. |
  | Late summer–autumn (Aug–Oct) | ≈ 7,500–35,000 | ≈ 3–14 | ≈ 5–22 million | Spectrum plus YOY. Nebish age-0 perch 7,500/ha and bass 590/ha; Schneider fall YOY perch 6,100–13,200/ha; Spirit Lake littoral mean 10,000/ha. |

  Add fish **< 3 cm** in June–July: perch, sucker, bullhead and sunfish larvae and early juveniles, which reach **15,000–60,000/ha** for perch alone at 18 mm (Oneida model, existing note). These are best held as larval/fry biomass, not counted fish.
- **What this means for agents:** at ≥ 7.5 cm the sim would hold **≈ 1.6 million individuals** across both lakes. That is ≈ 20–50% of the fish present in spring and only ≈ 7–25% in late summer by number, though ≈ 70–90% of the biomass. Holding everything < 7.5 cm as school/super-individual records is therefore essential.

### Gaps
- No size spectrum measured *below* 4–5 cm in a northern lake, and none from a littoral seine or whole-lake piscicide census broken into length classes, was found.
- The CJFAS 2015 "acoustic measures of lake community size spectra" paper (Sprules group; cdnsciencepub 403) and Boudreau & Dickie's (1992) fish biomass spectra were not reached.
- Emmrich et al. (2011) slope values were not obtained, only the qualitative result.

## Q6. Seasonal swing: from spring (pre-hatch) to late summer (with young-of-year)

### Takeaway
By number, northern lakes swing several-fold within a season.
- Littoral fish counts in May–June are a fraction of July–September counts: bluegill rose two to three orders of magnitude at Spirit Lake. Single-species perch lakes went from ~120 to > 6,000 perch/ha (existing note).
- Fall YOY densities in soft-water northern lakes: **≈ 7,500 age-0 perch and ≈ 590 age-0 smallmouth per ha** at Nebish (early October).
- Over the summer, acoustic abundance of fish > 4 cm *declines* at 1.6–6.7% per day, faster for smaller fish.
- So the census peaks just after the YOY become countable (July–August) and falls through autumn and winter.

### Cited Findings
- **Spirit Lake, Iowa [IA]** (littoral, efficiency-corrected night seines):
  - May–June densities were lower than in later months;
  - the July–September increase was "primarily" age-0 yellow perch and bluegill;
  - **September bluegill densities averaged 2–3 orders of magnitude higher than in early months**;
  - total density peaked in September in 1995 and 1998 and in July in 1996–97;
  - annual means varied 3.5-fold (4,154–14,389 fish/ha) as perch year-class strength changed.
  - Prey biomass available to the largest piscivores was **17–132 kg/ha in July and 74–180 kg/ha in September** across years. — [Pierce et al. 2001](https://www.cfwru.iastate.edu/files/inline-files/pierce2001_20.pdf)
- **Nebish Lake [WI]**, 3 October 1966 (rotenone; soft water). Age-0 per ha (derived from per-acre):
  - **yellow perch ≈ 7,500/ha (≈ 3.6 g)**;
  - **smallmouth bass ≈ 590/ha (≈ 5.9 g)**;
  - walleye ≈ 6/ha;
  - minnows ≈ 83,500/ha (all ages).
  
  Age-0 smallmouth were 94% of the smallmouth population and age-0 perch 97% of the perch population. — [Kempinger & Christenson 1978](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/EIQCHSPXF35AK8O/E/file-19978.pdf)
- **Lac du Bonnet [MB]** (acoustic, fish > 4 cm):
  - counts in every size class fell linearly through July–August, with loss rates of −0.067/day for 4–8 cm fish down to −0.016/day for the largest;
  - mean summer survival was 33% (derived: 30 days at −0.067/day leaves ≈ 13%);
  - recruitment into the 4-cm class within the survey window was minimal. — [Pollom & Rose 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4398392)
  - Spectrum height fell over each summer in 2011 and 2012. — [Wheeland 2014](https://memorial.scholaris.ca/items/6ee6203e-39f7-4b29-8e02-33c05250925a)
- **Sølensjøen, Norway [EU]** (pelagic acoustics): fish > 8 cm rose from 26/ha in June to 89/ha in September. That is redistribution plus growth into the size class, not reproduction. ≈ 600 fish < ~6 cm/ha were present in June. — [PMC8152495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8152495/)
- **Lake Opeongo, Ontario [ON]** (smallmouth bass, long-term study):
  - juvenile (age 1–4) abundance ranged **≈ 5,000–48,000** fish over 1937–1990;
  - **age-0 bass moved a mean of only 87.6 m (SD 60.7) from their nest by fall**;
  - juvenile growth was strongly density-dependent. — [Ridgway, Shuter, Middel & Gross 2002, AFS Symp. 31:47–60](https://www.harkness.ca/PDFs/Contributions%202000s/Ridgway%202002%20Smallmouth%20Bass%20the%20Jouvenile%20Transition%20Hypothesis.pdf)
- **Sparkling Lake, Wisconsin [WI]** (what removes small littoral fish):
  - juvenile walleye predation caused **≈ 100% (1982) and 80% (1983) of adult darter mortality**, and **75% and 35% of adult minnow mortality**;
  - predation on darters and minnows fell when age-0 yellow perch were abundant (1983), consistent with YOY perch buffering other small fish. — [Lyons & Magnuson 1987, *TAFS* 116:29–39](https://academic.oup.com/tafs/article/116/1/29/7892065)
- **Banded killifish [NL]:** 1,209 (July) vs 20,569 (August) in the same Newfoundland shallows. This is mainly a catchability/temperature effect (Q1). — [COSEWIC 2014](https://wildlife-species.canada.ca/species-risk-registry/virtual_sara/files/cosewic/sr_Banded%20Killifish_2014_e.pdf)
- **Lake Opinicon [ON]:** littoral biomass drops in late summer as fish move offshore. — [Keast et al., QUBS summary](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/nearshore-fish-habitat-utilization-and-species-associations-in-lake-opinicon-ontario-canada)
- **Lake Bemidji, Minnesota [MN]** (an undergraduate study; late Aug–Sept 2013):
  - the age-0 perch whole-lake estimate rose from 1,464,864 (littoral seines only) to **3,851,254** when cast nets sampled deeper water, a 163% increase;
  - age-0 perch were not confined to the littoral. Lake area was not given in the text read, so there is no per-ha value. — [Vaske 2014](https://faculty.bemidjistate.edu/ahafs/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2024/10/2014-Vaske-M.-Use-of-cast-nets-and-seine-hauls-to-estimate-abundance-of-age-0-yellow-perch.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Seasonal arc for the Hot Brook census (by number; my synthesis):**
  1. **Ice-out to late May:** the yearly minimum. Fish ≥ 7.5 cm ≈ 2,500/ha. Fish 3–7.5 cm ≈ 2,500–10,000/ha (age-1 perch, white perch and sunfish; killifish; dace in the brook).
  2. **June–early July:** eggs and larvae. Perch, white perch and sucker larvae reach tens of thousands per ha but are < 3 cm. Hold them as fry biomass.
  3. **Late July–September: the peak of countable fish.** Age-0 perch reach 4–7 cm, sunfish 2–4 cm, smallmouth 5–9 cm, pickerel 10–13 cm. Age-0 densities of ~5,000–25,000/ha are plausible: perch ≈ 2,500–7,500, white perch similar or more, plus pumpkinseed, sucker and bullhead schools, and smallmouth ≈ 300–600. Littoral totals approach 1 fish/m².
  4. **October–ice:** losses of ~2–7%/day in summer slow in the cold. By the next spring a strong year class is ~10–30% of its September number. That fits Schneider's requirement that perch "fall to 15 lb/acre by spring" and Oneida's 0.4–1.6% survival from 18 mm to age 2 (existing note).
- **Species to emphasize for Hot Brook YOY by number:** yellow perch and white perch (both abundant, both school), then pumpkinseed (Upper only), white sucker (YOY schools inshore, EMAP Upper Middle Branch: 114 YOY), brown bullhead (fry balls guarded by parents), smallmouth (≤ 100 m from the nest, rocky shore), chain pickerel (few, but they reach agent size in the first year).
- **Counts are minima and bunch strongly.** Chadwick's 9× mark–recapture underestimate for young perch, seine efficiency ≈ 50%, and the 163% Bemidji correction all say published YOY densities understate the true numbers. Age-0 fish are also concentrated, in weeds or near nests, not spread evenly.

### Gaps

- No spring-vs-late-summer whole-lake count pair was found for any New England or Maritime lake.
- Hall & Werner (1977) seasonal density data and Keast's Opinicon year-class densities exist but are paywalled.
