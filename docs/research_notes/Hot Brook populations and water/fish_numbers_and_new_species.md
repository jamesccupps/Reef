# Fish numbers per species and size in Hot Brook-type lakes, and the life histories of four new species

Conventions: fish/acre × 2.471 = fish/ha; lb/acre × 1.121 = kg/ha. Every conversion and every mean weight (biomass ÷ number) is mine and is marked "derived". "Via search summary" means the number came from a search-engine digest of the page, not from my own reading of the primary text. The sim's lake slice is 2048 px = 700 m² = **0.07 ha** (CLAUDE.md `SLICE_M2`), so fish/ha × 0.07 = fish in the lake slice.

Region flags: **[NE]** New England/New York; **[ME]** Maine; **[ON/QC/NB/NS/NL]** Canadian provinces; **[MI]**, **[WI]** Great Lakes states; **[S]** outside the requested region (southern or plains), included only where nothing northern exists.

## Q1. Per-species population densities (numbers/ha and kg/ha, with the size range counted)

### Takeaway
No per-hectare population estimate exists for any species in an eastern Maine warm-water lake. The best direct numbers are northern Michigan and Wisconsin mark–recapture and rotenone censuses, plus one New York pond.
- Age-1+ fish (≥ ~7.5 cm) in small weedy lakes run to **thousands per hectare**: Cassidy Lake, Michigan, held about **5,500/ha (163 kg/ha)**.
- Yellow perch alone ran from **50–570/ha** (≥ 15 cm) to more than **2,300/ha** (stunted, all ages).
- Pumpkinseed ran **25–440/ha**, brown bullhead **50–260/ha**, golden shiner about **690/ha**, smallmouth adults **1–20/ha**, and white perch **~520 to ~1,500–1,900/ha** (southern and plains reservoirs only).
- No chain pickerel, redbreast, fallfish, killifish or dace density was found. The closest proxy is the small grass pickerel, at 22–275/ha.

### Cited Findings

**Yellow perch**
- **South Twin Lake, Michigan [MI]** (4.3 acres = 1.74 ha; 12.7 m deep; perch the only fish besides a few stocked trout; rotenone in 1934). At least **4,118 perch were counted = 955 per acre (≈ 2,360/ha, derived)**, weighing about **29 lb/acre (≈ 32.5 kg/ha)**. The author judged the real population "probably well in excess of 1000 fish per acre". The fish were stunted, in poor condition and slow-growing; three-quarters were from one year class; only 7% (about 60/acre ≈ 150/ha) were of legal length. Mean weight ≈ 14 g (derived). — [Eschmeyer 1936, IFR Report 334](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR0301-RR0400/RR0334.pdf)
- **Cassidy Lake, Michigan [MI]** (46 acres = 18.6 ha; maximum depth 11 ft = 3.4 m; weedy, hard water at 134 ppm alkalinity; mixed warm-water community; mark–recapture in May 1964, then rotenone). Perch ≥ 3.0 in (76 mm) by age:
  - age I **15,297**, age II **18,011**, age III **678**, age IV **6**, age V **4**;
  - ≈ **34,000 fish = ≈ 1,830/ha** (derived), **23.5 lb/acre (26 kg/ha)**, mean ≈ 14 g (derived).
  - Total annual mortality was **80–96%**. There were "only about 4 perch per acre [≈ 10/ha] larger than 7.0 inches" in both Cassidy and Mill lakes.
  - Ages are read from scrambled OCR columns and checked against the age ranges in the report's Table 1. — [Schneider 1973, Fisheries Research Report 1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)
- **Single-species perch lakes, Michigan [MI]** (Cassidy, and Jewett Lake: 12.9 acres, 17 ft deep, alkalinity 34 ppm):
  - Perch rose "from 50 fish per acre in the spring of 1965 to over 2,500 fish per acre in the fall" (≈ 120 → 6,200/ha, derived). Peak standing crop was 57.5 lb/acre (64 kg/ha), 92% of it one year class.
  - After one growing season Jewett held **5,328 YOY/acre at 2.6 in** and Cassidy **2,471/acre at 3.2 in**: 34 and 30 lb/acre.
  - "The lakes could produce, by fall, 3,000 young of the year per acre, 3.2 inches long, weighing 30 pounds" (≈ 7,400/ha, 34 kg/ha). The population had to fall to about 15 lb/acre by spring for growth to continue. Perch ≥ 6.5 in grew only below about 10 lb/acre.
  - Perch ≥ 7.0 in held at **14–22 per acre** (35–54/ha). — [Schneider 1972, Research Report 1791](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1791.pdf)
- **Escanaba Lake, northern Wisconsin [WI]** (293 acres = 119 ha; maximum depth 25 ft; alkalinity 20 mg/L; spring estimates 1956–69).
  - Perch ≥ 6.0 in: **20–232 per acre (≈ 49–573/ha)** and **7–66 lb/acre (≈ 8–74 kg/ha)**. The maximum population was 68,000 fish.
  - Perch were 34–64% of the sport-fish crop by number in 1956–63, then declined.
  - Total sport-fish spring standing crop: 19–117 lb/acre. — [Kempinger, Churchill & Priegel 1975, Wisconsin DNR Tech. Bull. 84](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/X77C7WRVNYWX38Z/E/file-3e801.pdf)
- **Michigan perch-only lakes [MI]** averaged **46 lb/acre (≈ 52 kg/ha)** of perch. Section Four Lake held 47 lb/acre and South Twin 58 lb/acre (adjusted rotenone totals). Schneider assumed that only **4% of perch biomass exceeds 7.0 in under poor growth** (30% under average growth). — [Schneider 1973, Research Report 1794](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1794.pdf)
- **Oneida Lake, New York [NE]** (yellow perch–walleye model calibrated to field data). Model averages: **103–112 adult perch/ha**, 29–44 spawners/ha and 205–221 age-2 recruits/ha, from about 15,000–60,000 18-mm juveniles/ha. — [Rose et al. 2001, Table 1](https://aquaticecology.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2012/07/Rose_etal_FishFisheries.pdf) (as extracted in this repo's `Maine lakes ponds and streams/recruitment_compensation_maturity.md`)
- **Mirror Lake, New Hampshire [NE]** (2018 fyke-net mark–recapture): "large populations of yellow perch and pumpkinseed sunfish, with very few fish reaching quality sizes". No numbers were obtainable (thesis behind a 403). — [Winant 2019, Plymouth State thesis](https://summit.plymouth.edu/items/32f1a602-aed0-46e5-9548-e38ffe47ef83) (via search summary)

**Pumpkinseed**
- **Cassidy Lake [MI]**, ≥ 3.0 in, May:
  - by age: II 4,051, III 3,181, IV 463, V 134, VI 46;
  - ≈ **7,900 fish ≈ 420/ha** (derived), **14.5 lb/acre (16 kg/ha)**, mean ≈ 38 g (derived);
  - large pumpkinseed (≥ ~6 in) at 15/acre (37/ha); total annual mortality 68%.
  - Nearby **Mill Lake** held 12 lb/acre and 21 large/acre; **Sugarloaf** held 3 lb/acre and 4 large/acre. — [Schneider 1973, RR1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)
- **Escanaba Lake [WI]**, ≥ 4.5 in: **11–177 per acre (≈ 27–437/ha)** and **6–29 lb/acre (≈ 7–33 kg/ha)** over 1956–65. The maximum, 52,000 fish, came in 1959. The population later fell "too few to estimate". — [Kempinger et al. 1975](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/X77C7WRVNYWX38Z/E/file-3e801.pdf)
- **Michigan [MI]**, assumption used for adjustments: "50% of the biomass of pumpkinseeds exceeds 6.0 inches when growth is average". — [Schneider 1973, RR1794](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1794.pdf)
- **Ontario [ON]**: 27 pumpkinseed populations were studied for growth versus density with funnel traps (Fox 1994, *Ecology* 75:1157–1171). The summary gives no per-ha values. — [QUBS summary of Fox 1994](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/growth-density-and-interspecific-influences-on-pumpkinseed-sunfish-life-histories)

**Brown bullhead**
- **Moe Pond, Otsego County, New York [NE]** (15.6 ha; mean depth 1.8 m; only brown bullhead and golden shiner; unexploited):
  - age-1+ bullhead **4,057 fish (Schnabel) = 260/ha and 13 kg/ha** (mean ≈ 50 g, derived);
  - annual survival of ages 2–5 was **48%**. — [McCoy, Madenjian, Adams & Harman 2001, *J. Freshwater Ecol.* 16:389–394](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1000834)
- **Cassidy Lake [MI]**: 900 brown + black bullheads (≈ 48/ha) at 12.5 lb/acre (14 kg/ha), mean ≈ 290 g (derived). Also 1,730 yellow bullheads (≈ 93/ha) at 5.7 lb/acre. Bullheads of all species were 5, 5 and 18 lb/acre in Sugarloaf, Mill and Cassidy. — [Schneider 1973, RR1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)
- **Michigan [MI]** adjustment rule: "78% of the bullhead standing crop is larger than 6.0 inches in typical populations". — [Schneider 1973, RR1794](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1794.pdf)

**Golden shiner**
- **Moe Pond, New York [NE]**: **7,154 shiners = 686/ha and 5 kg/ha** (beach-seine replicate estimate), mean ≈ 7 g (derived). The length-frequency was unimodal, with a mode of **80 mm** and a maximum of **115 mm TL**. — [McCoy et al. 2001](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1000834)

**White sucker**
- **Marsh Lake, Gogebic County, Michigan [MI]** (soft water, about 6 ppm): smallmouth bass and white suckers only, **52 lb/acre total, 47 of it sucker (≈ 53 kg/ha)**. **Devoe Lake** (Ogemaw County, "predominantly white suckers") held 57 lb/acre, about 50 of it "other" fish. — [Schneider 1973, RR1794 Table 4](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1794.pdf)
- **Ontario [ON]**: 23 lakes, populations estimated by Petersen mark–recapture or CPUE. Suckers grew faster at low density and with more littoral chironomids, and sucker population size was negatively correlated with benthos. The abstract gives no per-ha numbers. — [Chen & Harvey 1995, TAFS 124:262–271](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/growth-abundance-and-food-supply-of-white-sucker/)
- **ELA Lake 223, Ontario [ON]** (26.4 ha): during pH recovery the white sucker population rose to "almost 10 times" its pre-experiment number, then fell. The abstract gives no absolute count. — [Recovery of fish populations in Lake 223, CJFAS 1999](https://dx.doi.org/10.1139/f99-186) (via search summary)
- **Escanaba Lake [WI]**: "white suckers were very numerous from 1958 through 1962 when the panfish populations were abundant". The suckers declined just before the panfish did and were never quantified. — [Kempinger et al. 1975](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/X77C7WRVNYWX38Z/E/file-3e801.pdf)

**Smallmouth bass**
- **Mildred Lake, Wisconsin [WI]**: adult estimate **8.2 per acre (≈ 20/ha)**. — [Wisconsin DNR 2005](https://apps.dnr.wi.gov/doclink/lakes_fish-plant_reports/oneidamildredlake2005.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Cosgrove Lake, Wisconsin [WI]**: about 48 adults, **0.53 per acre (≈ 1.3/ha)**. — [Cosgrove Lake 2015 summary](https://www.exploreflorencecounty.com/i/f/Cosgrove%20Lake%202015%20Summary.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Katherine Lake, Upper Peninsula, Michigan [MI]** (soft water, smallmouth only): **10 lb/acre (≈ 11 kg/ha)**. **Marsh Lake** held 5 lb/acre (≈ 5.6 kg/ha) of smallmouth alongside 47 lb/acre of sucker. — [Schneider 1973, RR1794](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1794.pdf)
- **Little Moose Lake, Adirondacks, New York [NE]** (271 ha, oligotrophic):
  - **47,682 smallmouth were removed over 6 years** (≈ 176/ha cumulative, derived; all sizes);
  - snorkel counts fell more than 90% within two years, yet the population "produc[ed] strong year-classes throughout the experiment";
  - six native littoral species rose 4–90× in relative abundance. — [Weidel, Josephson & Kraft 2007, TAFS 136:778–789](https://doi.org/10.1577/T06-091.1) (via search summary)
- **Congener for scale, largemouth bass in Cassidy Lake [MI]**: age I+ ≈ 720 fish (≈ 39/ha, derived), 9.9 lb/acre, mean ≈ 285 g (derived). Legal fish (> 10 in) numbered **3, 9 and 9 per acre** (7–22/ha) in Sugarloaf, Mill and Cassidy. — [Schneider 1973, RR1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)

**Chain pickerel** (no density estimate found; Maine facts and a congener proxy)
- **Maine [ME]**:
  - "Pickerel attain sexual maturity at age 3-4, generally at lengths from 13 to 16 inches". Young of the year reach "4 to 5 inches" by September–October and turn "strongly piscivorous" at 7–9 inches. Annual growth is about 2.5–3.5 in.
  - "Many pickerel ponds support good numbers of 15-19 inch fish".
  - The sex ratio favours females about 1.3:1.
  - The statewide harvest objective was "approximately 0.35 pounds per acre" a year. Angled fish averaged 16.2 in and 1.1 lb.
  - Pickerel "maintain moderate to high density populations" wherever there is shallow, weedy habitat. — [MDIFW Chain Pickerel Assessment](https://www1.maine.gov/ifw/docs/strategic-management-plans/pickerel.pdf)
- **Grass pickerel (*Esox americanus*, a much smaller congener) as the only esocid proxy found:**
  - **Pleasant Lake, Wisconsin [WI]** (138 acres): fall Schnabel estimates of **5,419; 3,121; 1,300; 1,240** fish (1962–65), or **39.3 → 9.0 per acre (≈ 97 → 22/ha)** and **3.5 → 1.1–1.4 lb/acre**. Only 10% exceeded 10 in.
  - Earlier Michigan figures: **101 and 111 per acre (≈ 250–275/ha) in two ponds** (Eschmeyer & Clark 1939), and 24.9/acre (≈ 62/ha) recovered by rotenone in a 14.8-acre lake, **81.2% of them young of the year** (Carbine & Applegate 1948). — [Kleinert & Mraz 1966, Wisconsin Tech. Bull. 37](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/JPQYSFG3QCFOM87/E/file-789f9.pdf)
  - **Cassidy Lake [MI]**: 356 grass pickerel ≈ 19/ha, 0.8 lb/acre. — [Schneider 1973, RR1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)
- **Nova Scotia [NS]**: pickerel-invaded lakes had lower total fish abundance and truncated size distributions. Sampling effort differed two orders of magnitude between lake types, so no density can be derived. — [Mitchell et al., NS Fisheries](https://novascotia.ca/fish/documents/special-management-areas-reports/impact-chain-pickerel.pdf) (via search summary)

**White perch** (no northern per-ha estimate found)
- **Lake Anna, Virginia [S]** (5,600-ha reservoir; cove rotenone): white perch rose from **2.73 kg/ha (1979) to 24.2 (1982) and 21.0 kg/ha (1983)**, averaging 12.7 kg/ha. Numbers "averaged **520/ha**" over 1981–83, a mean of about 40–45 g in those years (derived). — [NRC docket ML050670520 (Vepco data)](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0506/ML050670520.pdf)
- **Branched Oak Lake, Nebraska [S]** (stunted population): an estimated **1.1–1.4 million white perch**. — [Stewart 2015, UNL thesis](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/natresdiss/108). The lake covers about 1,800 acres (≈ 730 ha) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branched_Oak_State_Recreation_Area) — so this is **≈ 1,500–1,900/ha** (derived; the size range counted is unknown). Predation there removed the largest, oldest perch and may have reinforced the stunting. — [Gosch, Pierce & Pope 2010](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70037671)
- **Maine [ME]**: white perch "form very large populations that occasionally dominate". Stunting can be "so extreme that a 6-year-old perch may be only 4 inches long". — [MDIFW White Perch Plan](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/docs/strategic-management-plans/whiteperch.pdf) (quoted in this repo's `docs/reports/Maine lakes ponds and streams.md`). **Upper Hot Brook** carries a special 25-fish white perch bag limit. — [MDIFW lake map](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/docs/lake-survey-maps/washington/hot_brook_lake_upper.pdf)
- **New Hampshire [NE]**: Bow, Harvey, Massabesic, Pawtuckaway and the Suncook lakes have populations described as stunted, but no numbers are given. — [Manchester Ink Link](https://manchesterinklink.com/?p=17795) (via search summary)

**Redbreast sunfish, fallfish, banded killifish, blacknose dace**
- No per-area density was found for any of the four in the Northeast.
  - Banded killifish made up **86% of all fish seined** at four shallow (≤ 1.2 m) sites in Presque Isle Bay, Lake Erie [PA, Great Lakes]. — [Phillips, Ewert & Speares 2007](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-14-2/18-Phillips.shtml)
  - The Indian Bay watershed, Newfoundland [NL], holds "over 20 000" killifish (no area basis). — [COSEWIC 2014](https://wildlife-species.canada.ca/species-risk-registry/virtual_sara/files/cosewic/sr_Banded%20Killifish_2014_e.pdf) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Size threshold matters more than species.** Most published densities count fish ≥ 3–6 in (7.5–15 cm), which is the sim's "individual" threshold. At Cassidy, perch ≥ 7.6 cm numbered ≈ 1,830/ha, but those ≥ 17.8 cm numbered only ≈ 10/ha: a 180-fold drop.
- **Mean weights** of the age-1+ fish counted (derived): stunted perch 14 g, golden shiner 7 g, Cassidy pumpkinseed 38 g, Moe Pond bullhead 50 g, white perch about 25–45 g, grass pickerel about 40 g. A sim that conserves kg/ha with realistic mean weights will hold thousands of fish per hectare, mostly small.
- **Smallmouth bass.** 1–20 adults/ha across northern lakes. Hot Brook is managed for "larger-than-average bass", which argues for the middle of that range (5–15 adults/ha) rather than the crowded top.
- **Chain pickerel** (no direct estimate). Take 4–8 kg/ha (the existing report's 4–8% share of ~100 kg/ha) at a typical Maine angled weight of about 0.5 kg (1.1 lb). That gives **≈ 8–16 pickerel ≥ 33 cm per ha**, plus perhaps as many sub-adults of 15–33 cm. Grass pickerel at 22–97/ha, at a tenth of the body mass, is consistent with that.
- **White perch.** Maine's descriptions match the Branched Oak style (crowded, small fish) more than Lake Anna. A plausible Hot Brook range is **400–1,500 age-1+ fish/ha and 15–30 kg/ha**, with most fish 12–20 cm. This is an extrapolation from [S] systems and Maine's qualitative statements.
- **White sucker.** Biomass is well bounded (≈ 50 kg/ha in sucker-dominated Michigan soft-water lakes; 12–20% of a ~100 kg/ha total in the design). Adults of 0.5–1 kg then imply **≈ 15–40 adults/ha** (derived).
- **Lake-slice numbers.** At 0.07 ha, Cassidy's 5,470 age-1+ fish/ha would be ≈ 380 individuals in the lake slice. Moe Pond's 946 fish/ha (bullhead + shiner) would be ≈ 66.

### Gaps
- No mark–recapture, depletion or rotenone estimate was found for chain pickerel, white perch (in any northern lake), redbreast sunfish, fallfish, banded killifish or blacknose dace.
- No numbers per ha were found for white sucker. The Trippel & Harvey (1987) and Chen & Harvey (1995) full texts probably contain them but were not accessible.
- The Mirror Lake NH thesis (Winant 2019) and Mazsa (1973, Cornell) likely hold New Hampshire yellow perch, pumpkinseed and smallmouth estimates but could not be retrieved (HTTP 403).
- No hydroacoustic small-lake densities for Quebec, Ontario or New England were found in this pass.

## Q2. Whole-community totals (rotenone, draw-down, whole-lake census) and the species split

### Takeaway
No northeastern or Maine whole-community census was retrievable. Rupp & DeRoche (1965) again failed (the scan exceeds 10 MB). The best complete censuses are three small, shallow southern-Michigan lakes and a two-species New York pond.
- Cassidy Lake had **≈ 5,470 fish ≥ 3 in per ha at 163 kg/ha** (mean ≈ 30 g).
- Michigan "normal" lakes averaged ≈ 99 kg/ha, and soft-water lakes 11–70 kg/ha.
- In centrarchid–perch lakes, small panfish make up about 80–90% of the numbers and 60–80% of the biomass. Piscivores are about 1% of the numbers but 7–15% of the biomass.

- **Cassidy Lake, Michigan [MI]** (18.6 ha, ≤ 3.4 m, weedy, hard water; May 1964 mark–recapture checked against a full rotenone recovery). **Total 145 lb/acre (≈ 163 kg/ha).** By species (fish ≥ 3.0 in):

  | Yellow perch | ≈ 34,000 | ≈ 1,830 | 23.5 | 26 |
  | Pumpkinseed | ≈ 7,900 | ≈ 420 | 14.5 | 16 |
  | Green sunfish | 3,400 | ≈ 183 | 2.9 | 3.3 |
  | Yellow bullhead | 1,730 | ≈ 93 | 5.7 | 6.4 |
  | Brown + black bullhead | 900 | ≈ 48 | 12.5 | 14.0 |
  | Largemouth bass | ≈ 720 | ≈ 39 | 9.9 | 11.1 |
  | Lake chubsucker | 450 | ≈ 24 | 2.5 | 2.8 |
  | Grass pickerel | 356 | ≈ 19 | 0.8 | 0.9 |

  Numbers are summed from the report's age and species estimates; per-ha values and totals are derived. — [Schneider 1973, RR1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)
- The same report puts the total standing crop of fish of all sizes at **about 95 lb/acre in Sugarloaf Lake and 116 in Mill Lake** (both shallow, hard water). — [Schneider 1973, RR1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)
- **64 Michigan lakes [MI]**:
  - standing crop 10–360 lb/acre; "normal" lakes averaged 88 lb/acre and perch-only lakes 46 lb/acre;
  - lakes dominated by slow-growing bluegills averaged 182 lb/acre;
  - Katherine Lake (soft water, smallmouth only) held 10 lb/acre;
  - most rotenoned "trout-potential" lakes "were dominated by slow-growing warmwater fish such as yellow perch, pumpkinseed, white sucker, and various species of minnows". — [Schneider 1973, RR1794](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1794.pdf)
- **Escanaba Lake, Wisconsin [WI]** (119 ha; alkalinity 20 mg/L):
  - spring standing crop of sport fishes "varied annually from **19 to 117 lb/acre**";
  - in 1956–62 that was 243–438 sport fish per acre (≈ 600–1,080/ha, derived), dominated by perch and pumpkinseed;
  - white suckers and minnows were not estimated. — [Kempinger et al. 1975](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/X77C7WRVNYWX38Z/E/file-3e801.pdf)
- **Moe Pond, New York [NE]** (two species, unexploited): 260 bullhead + 686 shiner per ha = **946 fish/ha and 18 kg/ha** (derived sum). — [McCoy et al. 2001](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1000834)
- **Maine [ME]**: Rupp & DeRoche (1965) covers three small Maine trout lakes and remains the only Maine rotenone census known. It was not extractable this pass either: the archive file exceeds the fetch limit. — [Behnke archive scan](https://arc.lib.montana.edu/robert-behnke/objects/2491-36-10.pdf)

### Inferences
- **A design census for Hot Brook** (age-1+, ≥ ~7.5 cm, late summer; my synthesis). It is built to sum to about 100 kg/ha, the existing report's target, using the densities and mean weights above.

  | Species | Fish/ha (range) | kg/ha (range) | Lake slice (0.07 ha) at midpoint | Basis |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | White perch | 400–1,500 | 15–30 | ≈ 55 | Lake Anna / Branched Oak; Maine "very large populations" |
  | Yellow perch | 300–1,800 | 10–26 | ≈ 60 | Cassidy, Escanaba, South Twin |
  | Pumpkinseed | 50–420 | 2–16 | ≈ 15 | Cassidy, Escanaba; not on the Hot Brook list, so low |
  | Redbreast sunfish | 20–150 | 1–5 | ≈ 5 | none; pumpkinseed analogue, stream pools |
  | Golden shiner | 200–700 | 1.5–5 | ≈ 30 | Moe Pond |
  | Fallfish | 10–50 | 2–8 | ≈ 2 | none; stream/inlet species |
  | White sucker (adults) | 15–40 | 12–25 | ≈ 2 | Michigan biomass ÷ 0.5–1 kg |
  | Brown bullhead | 50–260 | 8–14 | ≈ 10 | Moe Pond, Cassidy |
  | Smallmouth bass (age-1+) | 15–60 (5–15 adults) | 4–11 | ≈ 2–3 (≈ 0.7 adults) | Wisconsin, Michigan |
  | Chain pickerel (age-1+) | 10–30 | 3–8 | ≈ 1–2 | Maine size data; grass-pickerel proxy |
  | **Total** | **≈ 1,100–5,000 (mid ≈ 2,600)** | **≈ 60–150 (mid ≈ 100)** | **≈ 180** | |

  The midpoint (≈ 2,600 fish/ha, mean ≈ 38 g) sits about halfway to Cassidy's 5,470/ha, which fits Hot Brook's soft, tea-coloured water against Cassidy's hard water.
- **Fish too small to be individuals.** Banded killifish (adults 59–96 mm) and blacknose dace (adults 56–83 mm) mostly fall below a 7.5 cm threshold. They are better held as school/aggregate records, like `world.fry`. Only age-2+ killifish of 79–93 mm would cross it.

### Gaps
- No whole-lake census was found for any New England, New York, Quebec or Maritime warm-water lake with this species set (white perch + pickerel + smallmouth + sucker).
- The Massachusetts and Connecticut 1930s–50s pond rotenone surveys and Maine IF&W reclamation records were not found online.

## Q3. Size structure: young-of-year versus age-1+, and summer length-frequency

### Takeaway
By number, late-summer populations are dominated by young of the year. In a strong perch year class, fall YOY outnumber the older fish by 20–50×. Grass pickerel recovered by rotenone were 81% YOY. By biomass, age-1+ fish dominate. Among age-1+ fish, annual mortality of 50–96% concentrates the numbers in ages 1–3.

### Cited Findings
- **Perch [MI]:** "from 50 fish per acre in the spring … to over 2,500 fish per acre in the fall". Fall YOY were 2,471–5,328/acre at 2.6–3.2 in; adults ≥ 7 in were 14–22/acre. — [Schneider 1972, RR1791](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1791.pdf)
- **Cassidy age structure in May [MI]**, i.e. before that year's hatch:
  - perch: ages I–II were 98% of ≥ 3-in perch (15,297 + 18,011 of ≈ 34,000);
  - pumpkinseed: ages II–III were 92%;
  - bluegill: age II alone was 65%;
  - annual mortality was 63% bluegill, 68% pumpkinseed, 80–96% perch, and 26% (age II) to 73% (age VIII) in largemouth bass. — [Schneider 1973, RR1792](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1701-RR1800/RR1792.pdf)
- **South Twin stunted perch [MI]**: "over three-fourths were of one age group"; all fish over four winters old were females; 7% were legal size. — [Eschmeyer 1936](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/Publications/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR0301-RR0400/RR0334.pdf)
- **Grass pickerel [MI]**: rotenone recovery was "81.2 percent … young-of-the-year". At Pleasant Lake [WI], mean lengths were 8.4–8.7 in in 1964–65, and "only 10 percent exceeded 10 inches". — [Kleinert & Mraz 1966](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/JPQYSFG3QCFOM87/E/file-789f9.pdf)
- **Golden shiner [NE]**: a unimodal length-frequency, mode 80 mm, maximum 115 mm (Moe Pond). — [McCoy et al. 2001](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1000834)
- **Brown bullhead [NE]**: 48% annual survival over ages 2–5, so each year class halves. — [McCoy et al. 2001](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1000834)
- **Banded killifish (seine catches, Presque Isle Bay)**:
  - summer: age 1 57%, age 2 39%, age 3 4%;
  - fall: age 0+ 36% (32 mm), 1+ 48% (52 mm), 2+ 17% (63 mm). — [Phillips et al. 2007](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-14-2/18-Phillips.shtml)
- **Oneida perch model [NE]**: 15,325–60,594 18-mm juveniles/ha led to 205–221 age-2 recruits/ha (0.4–1.6% survival). — [Rose et al. 2001](https://aquaticecology.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2012/07/Rose_etal_FishFisheries.pdf)
- **Chain pickerel [ME]**: YOY reach 4–5 in (10–13 cm) by September–October. — [MDIFW pickerel](https://www1.maine.gov/ifw/docs/strategic-management-plans/pickerel.pdf)

### Inferences
- With a 7.5 cm individual threshold, perch, pumpkinseed, shiner and white perch YOY stay mostly below it through their first summer (perch 6.6–8.1 cm by fall in productive lakes). Chain pickerel YOY (10–13 cm by fall) and fallfish cross it in their first year.
- A realistic late-summer census should therefore show far more school/fry mass than individuals. The individual counts should be dominated by ages 1–3.

### Gaps
- No summer length-frequencies were found for white perch, white sucker or smallmouth in a small northeastern lake.

## Q4. Seasonal habitat in shallow, unstratified lakes (brief)

### Takeaway
In a shallow unstratified lake, most fish live along the weedy margin in summer. In Lake Opinicon (Ontario) 80–90% of fish biomass is at ≤ 2.5 m. Under ice, fish move toward deeper water and later toward O₂. White perch and white sucker are the main users of open, mud-bottomed water. This repo's `research_notes/Seasonal fish habitat use/` notes cover these species in detail.

### Cited Findings
- **Lake Opinicon, Ontario [ON]** (unstratified; strip counts): **80–90% of fish biomass along the margins at ≤ 2.5 m**. — [Keast & Harker 1977](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/fish-distribution-and-benthic-invertebrate-biomass-relative-to-depth-in-an-ontario-lake)
- **White perch**: schools over level, fine-sediment bottoms. They sit at about 4–9 m by day and come inshore at about 1 m at night, and gather in the deepest basin by midwinter. — [Stanley & Danie 1983, FWS/OBS-82/11.7](https://njscuba.net/wp-content/uploads/biology/pdf/usfws_white_perch_0199.pdf) (via the repo's seasonal notes)
- **Chain pickerel and smallmouth, Milipsigate Lake, Nova Scotia [NS]** (telemetry):
  - half of pickerel detections were at 0.76–1.82 m;
  - by December–January every tagged smallmouth bass was at one of four wintering sites. — [Sergio 2026 thesis](https://dal.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/0d151e69-14e6-46e0-ab65-3fac80168436/download) (via the repo's seasonal notes)
- **White sucker in a small boreal lake** (telemetry): mean depth 3.8 m in spring, 4.9 m in summer, 5.0 m in fall and 6.1 m in winter, within 1 m of the bottom 84–88% of the time. — [EBF 2024, s10641-024-01581-8](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10641-024-01581-8) (via the repo's seasonal notes)
- **Under-ice O₂**: consumption and growth fall at or below 2 mg/L — [Roberts et al. 2011](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70118788) — and the repo's notes find fish avoiding water below about 2 mg/L. Black bullheads survived winter anoxia that killed sunfish and white sucker. — [Shroyer 2007](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2007/other/070599.pdf) (both via the repo's seasonal notes)
- **Redbreast sunfish**: aggregate in deeper water below 5 °C, and move into 15–46 cm shallows above 16.7 °C. — [COSEWIC redbreast, ch. 6](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/redbreast-sunfish/chapter-6.html)
- **Banded killifish**: not catchable in fyke nets below 12 °C. They probably leave the littoral for deeper water in autumn, an inference from a congener. — [COSEWIC 2014](https://wildlife-species.canada.ca/species-risk-registry/virtual_sara/files/cosewic/sr_Banded%20Killifish_2014_e.pdf)

### Inferences
- In Hot Brook's 4–5.5 m saucers there is no deep refuge from warmth. The summer split is therefore weed edge versus open mud rather than shallow versus deep.

### Gaps
- No telemetry was found for any species in an unstratified, tea-coloured Maine lake.

## Q5. Life histories of redbreast sunfish, fallfish, banded killifish and blacknose dace

### Takeaway
All four are native to Maine and are small-to-mid-sized littoral or stream fishes.
- **Redbreast** is a nest-guarding *Lepomis* that grows slowly at the northern edge of its range: 88 mm at age 2+ and 141 mm at 5+ in New Brunswick. It spawns at about 20 °C in late June to early July.
- **Fallfish** is a large (to 45–54 cm), mound-building stream cyprinid. It matures at age 3–4, spawns at about 14–19 °C, and turns partly piscivorous above about 10 cm.
- **Banded killifish** is a weedy-shallows topminnow. It matures at age 1 (≥ 64 mm), spawns repeatedly at 19–23 °C in July, lives 3–4 years, and has broad thermal and O₂ tolerance (incipient upper lethal 34.5 °C).
- **Blacknose dace** is a 6–8 cm riffle minnow. It matures at age 2, spawns over gravel at 15.5–22 °C, lives 3–4 years, and is the least heat-tolerant of the four (incipient upper lethal 28.8–29.9 °C).

### Cited Findings

**Redbreast sunfish (*Lepomis auritus*)**
- **Length–weight** (W in g, TL in cm): Alabama W = 0.0209 TL^3.01 (n = 3,937); Central Appalachia W = 0.0127 TL^2.59 (n = 30, 4.5–18.8 cm). — [FishBase L–W list](https://www.fishbase.se/popdyn/LWRelationshipList.php?ID=3370&GenusName=Lepomis&SpeciesName=auritus). The Alabama equation gives 43 g at 126 mm, which matches Yoho Lake's measured mean of 126 mm / 42 g (derived check).
- **Growth, New Brunswick [NB]** (Yoho Lake, back-calculated TL at the end of each growing season): **0+ 36, 1+ 64, 2+ 88, 3+ 111, 4+ 127, 5+ 141 mm**.
  - Length-frequency modes: 91 mm (2+) and 117 mm (3+).
  - Mean size 126 mm / 42 g in Yoho and 143 mm / 81 g in Oromocto.
  - Guarding males averaged 15.7–16.6 cm FL.
  - North Carolina fish were larger: 2+ 139–142 mm up to 6+ 229–235 mm. — [COSEWIC redbreast sunfish, ch. 6](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/redbreast-sunfish/chapter-6.html)
- **Georgia [S]** (Satilla River): back-calculated lengths at annuli I–VII of 59, 90, 125, 153, 181, 205 and 222 mm. — [Sandow et al., SEAFWA](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/SANDOW-279.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Maturity**: "about 23 g or in the second year" (Carlander 1977). No Canadian data. Fish to age 8 are recorded, and generation time is about 4 years. — [COSEWIC ch. 6](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/redbreast-sunfish/chapter-6.html)
- **Fecundity**: 963–8,250 eggs at 139–235 mm TL (North Carolina, Davis 1972); 1,396–4,138 at 100–175 mm (Florida, Bass & Hitt 1974). — [COSEWIC ch. 6](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/redbreast-sunfish/chapter-6.html). Satilla River: 322–9,206, mean 3,302; sex ratio 1 male : 1.49 females. — [Sandow et al.](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/SANDOW-279.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Spawning and nests**:
  - Peak spawning at 16.7–27.8 °C across the range. In New Brunswick lakes it ran from **late June to early July as water approached 20 °C**.
  - Nests averaged **50.9 cm across, 7.1 cm deep, in 43 cm of water**. 58% were more than 5 m from the next nest; in Oromocto 85% were within 1 m of a rock or log.
  - Substrate was coarse sand to fine gravel, excavated through vegetation.
  - Eggs are about 2 mm and adhesive. The male guards them; larvae are 4.9 mm at hatch and stay 1–2 weeks in the nest. — [COSEWIC ch. 6](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/redbreast-sunfish/chapter-6.html)
- **Diet**:
  - Sub-yearlings eat mainly cladocerans and copepods.
  - Adults eat aquatic insects (chironomids, caddisflies, mayflies, odonates), molluscs and small fish. In Florida, 32–36% of diet volume was vegetation and debris. In Yoho Lake caddisflies dominated, and cannibalism was recorded. — [COSEWIC ch. 6](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/redbreast-sunfish/chapter-6.html)
- **Habitat and behaviour**:
  - Slow river reaches and vegetated lake margins.
  - "Darts under rocks or bottom cover when disturbed".
  - Strong site fidelity: two-thirds of tagged fish moved less than 100 m.
  - Overwinters deeper below 5 °C. — [COSEWIC ch. 6](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/redbreast-sunfish/chapter-6.html)
- **Distribution**: found in **14%** of 203 northeastern lakes. — [Whittier et al. 1999](https://nysm.nysed.gov/bibcite/reference/21118) (via this repo's Maine report)

**Fallfish (*Semotilus corporalis*)**
- **Length–weight**:
  - Ontario: log10 W = −5.6336 + 3.3039 log10 TL. Units are unstated; it is consistent with W in g and TL in mm, giving 40 g at 155 mm and 680 g at 365 mm, which matches the listed adult range of 0.04–0.68 kg at 15.5–36.5 cm (derived check). — [Ontario Freshwater Fishes Life History Database](https://www.ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=74)
  - Quebec: W = 0.0432 FL^2.698 (cm, g). — [FishBase L–W list](https://www.fishbase.se/popdyn/LWRelationshipList.php?ID=2953&GenusName=Semotilus&SpeciesName=corporalis)
- **Size and age**:
  - Ontario lists adults 15.5–36.5 cm TL, a maximum of 54.0 cm and 1.7 kg, and a lifespan of 6–10 years. — [Ontario database](https://www.ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=74)
  - Massachusetts: largest recorded 480 mm, typical adults about 300 mm; "the largest and longest-lived minnow" native to eastern North America. — [MassWildlife](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/fallfish)
  - FishBase gives a maximum reported age of 6 and a common length of 17.1 cm. — [FishBase](https://www.fishbase.se/summary/2953) (via search summary)
- **Maturity**:
  - Massachusetts study of eight populations: males mature at age III+, females at IV+. — [SciSpace topic digest](https://scispace.com/topics/fallfish-3o5slshq) (via search summary; the digest quotes the Massachusetts paper, whose authors and year were not seen, so the attribution is uncertain)
  - Ontario lists 2–3 years for males and 3–4 for females. — [Ontario database](https://www.ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=74)
- **Fecundity**: 2,000–12,321 eggs. — [Ontario database](https://www.ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=74). The Massachusetts study gives F = 159.1 + 26.4 W (units of W not given). Eggs are 2.16 mm unfertilized and 2.70 mm water-hardened, hatch in 138–144 h at 17 °C, and give 6.82 mm larvae. — [SciSpace digest](https://scispace.com/topics/fallfish-3o5slshq) (via search summary)
- **Spawning**:
  - Late April–June when water reaches 14.4 °C (Massachusetts) — [SciSpace digest](https://scispace.com/topics/fallfish-3o5slshq) (via search summary).
  - May–June at **14–19 °C**; a nonguarding brood hider over gravel (lithophil). — [Ontario database](https://www.ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=74)
  - "Temperatures below 15°C inhibit spawning"; males grow head tubercles for territorial defence. — [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Semotilus_corporalis)
  - Its large size "allows it to build pyramidal mounds that it and other species use to spawn". — [MassWildlife](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/fallfish) (via search summary)
- **Diet**:
  - Juveniles eat chironomids and zooplankton. Above about 100 mm the diet shifts to small fish (including their own young), fish eggs and terrestrial insects. — [Wikipedia mirror, secondary](https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Fallfish)
  - Massachusetts stomachs showed an omnivore: algae, fish, aquatic and terrestrial insects, crayfish. — [SciSpace digest](https://scispace.com/topics/fallfish-3o5slshq) (via search summary)
  - Ontario classes it as an invertivore/carnivore. — [Ontario database](https://www.ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=74)
- **Habitat and thermal**:
  - "Gravel- and cobble-bottomed pools and runs of small to medium rivers, and lake margins"; a coolwater species; **preferred temperature 22.3 °C**. — [Ontario database](https://www.ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=74)
  - An otolith study of three New York stream populations concluded that "the size of the stream and the density of conspecifics" mainly set growth. — [SciSpace topic digest](https://scispace.com/topics/fallfish-3o5slshq) (via search summary). This is probably Victor & Brothers 1982, *Can. J. Zool.* 60:2543–2550 (otolith-validated age and growth), but that was not confirmed.
- **Maine**: present in **461 lakes**. — [MDIFW Non-Sport Plan](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/docs/strategic-management-plans/nonsportcommercial.pdf) (via this repo's Maine report). On the Hot Brook species list. — [MDIFW lake map](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/docs/lake-survey-maps/washington/hot_brook_lake_upper.pdf)

**Banded killifish (*Fundulus diaphanus*)**
- **Length–weight**, Presque Isle Bay, Lake Erie (W in g, L in mm; checked: an 80-mm female gives 5.4 g, inside the 2.96–8.20 g range):
  - summer females ln W = −11.638 + 3.040 ln L (r² 0.895);
  - males ln W = −10.089 + 2.671 ln L;
  - fall fish of all ages ln W = −11.032 + 2.919 ln L. — [Phillips et al. 2007](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-14-2/18-Phillips.shtml)
  - FishBase also lists W = 0.01268 SL^3 (cm), and a Lake Jesse, Nova Scotia fit of 0.00132 FL^3.25. The second gives implausibly light fish (≈ 0.7 g at 7 cm) and is probably mis-entered. — [FishBase L–W list](https://www.fishbase.se/popdyn/LWRelationshipList.php?ID=3190&GenusName=Fundulus&SpeciesName=diaphanus)
- **Growth**:
  - Lake Erie, summer: age 1 **67 mm**, age 2 **79 mm**, age 3 **93 mm**. Fall: 0+ 32, 1+ 52, 2+ 63 mm. Summer fish weighed 1.99–8.20 g. — [Phillips et al. 2007](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-14-2/18-Phillips.shtml)
  - Lake Opinicon, Ontario: YOY reach **49.5 mm by mid-October**, then stop growing until spring. — [COSEWIC 2014](https://wildlife-species.canada.ca/species-risk-registry/virtual_sara/files/cosewic/sr_Banded%20Killifish_2014_e.pdf)
- **Size and age**:
  - The eastern subspecies reaches about 110 mm; the largest Newfoundland fish was 128 mm.

- **Diet**:
  - Younger fish rely on cladocerans; older fish on chironomids and amphipods. — [Phillips et al. 2007](https://eaglehill.us/NENAonline/articles/NENA-14-2/18-Phillips.shtml)

- **Habitat and physiology**:
  - Shallow, clear, low-current water over sand or mud with submerged plants. Some move at night into deeper rocky habitat; weak swimmers.
  - Preferred temperature 21.0 °C (Nova Scotia fish) versus 28.6 °C (Pennsylvania fish). **Upper incipient lethal 34.5 °C**, and fish have been observed at 38.3 °C.

- **Maine**: present in 363 lakes and on the Hot Brook list. — [MDIFW Non-Sport Plan](https://www.maine.gov/ifw/docs/strategic-management-plans/nonsportcommercial.pdf) (via this repo's Maine report)

**Blacknose dace (*Rhinichthys atratulus*, the eastern species; the western form is now *R. obtusus*)**
- **Length–weight** (W in g, TL in cm): Central Appalachia W = 0.0091 TL^3.07 (n = 72, 4.2–6.7 cm), which gives 3.6 g at 7 cm (derived). — [FishBase L–W list](https://www.fishbase.se/popdyn/LWRelationshipList.php?ID=2943&GenusName=Rhinichthys&SpeciesName=atratulus)
- **Size and age**:
  - Adults 5.6–8.3 cm TL and 1–5 g; maximum 9.9 cm TL; lifespan 3–4 years; matures at age 2. — [Ontario database](https://ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=69)
  - Maximum reported age 3. — [FishBase](https://fishbase.org/summary/2943) (via search summary)
  - Typically about 7.5 cm, up to 13 cm, living 3–4 years. — [MassWildlife](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/blacknose-dace) (via search summary)
- **Growth (western blacknose dace, Iowa [S/Midwest])**:
  - Age 0 average 24–27 mm in the first year; age I reaches 49–50 mm.
  - Maturity at age II, and few reach age III.
  - Fastest growth in May–July; spawning early May–July over gravel; Diptera larvae were the only consistently used food.
  - Fish overwintered in pools and concentrated in cool headwaters in summer. — [Noble 1965, *Proc. Iowa Acad. Sci.* 72:282–293](https://scholarworks.uni.edu/pias/vol72/iss1/42)
- **Reproduction**: fecundity **166–1,116**; spawning May–June at **15.5–22 °C**. A nonguarding open-substratum litho-pelagophil. — [Ontario database](https://ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=69)
- **Habitat**:
  - "Runs and pools of clear, cool, swiftly-flowing creeks and small rivers with gravelly substrate"; benthic; preferred temperature range 19–25 °C. — [Ontario database](https://ontariofishes.ca/fish_detail.php?FID=69)
  - One of the most common stream fishes of cool and coldwater streams in Massachusetts. — [MassWildlife](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/blacknose-dace) (via search summary)
- **Thermal**: incipient upper lethal **29.9 °C (16:8 photoperiod) and 28.8 °C (8:16) at 20 °C acclimation**. — [Terpin, Spotila & Koons 1976, *Comp. Biochem. Physiol.* 53A:241–244](https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/7227758)

### Inferences
- **Redbreast sunfish.** Maturity at about 23 g is ≈ 10.3 cm on the Alabama L–W (derived). On Yoho growth that is **age 3** in Maine-like conditions, a year later than Carlander's "second year", which comes from southern fish.
- **Fallfish.** In Hot Brook it belongs mainly in the stream and at the inlet/outlet margins; its spawning mounds belong in the riffles. With a preferred temperature of 22.3 °C and an upper limit unknown, the 24 °C lake peak is near its comfort ceiling. That argues for summer use of the stream pools and the lake margin at the inflow.
- **Banded killifish.** The four species' thermal limits rank: killifish (UILT 34.5 °C) > redbreast (assumed *Lepomis*-like, around 35 °C, unmeasured) > fallfish (unknown) > blacknose dace (≈ 29 °C). Hot Brook's 24 °C peak stresses none of them, but a stream pool at 26–28 °C would push dace to their limit.
- **Blacknose dace.** In a lake model it should be confined to the stream (riffles and runs), with only accidental lake use.

### Gaps
- No thermal maximum or hypoxia tolerance was found for redbreast sunfish or fallfish. Cherry et al. (1977) and Stauffer (1981) were checked and do not include them.
- No dissolved-oxygen tolerance was found for blacknose dace.
- No primary fallfish length-at-age table was retrieved (the Massachusetts eight-population study and Victor & Brothers 1982 were seen only as citations).
- No Maine-specific growth data were found for any of the four.

## Q6. Which Fish Bioenergetics 4.0 parameter set to use for each new species

### Takeaway
None of the four has its own FB4 set. Recommended surrogates:
- **redbreast → bluegill** (Kitchell et al. 1974), a congener;
- **banded killifish → California killifish** (Madon et al. 2001), a congener whose CTM 34 °C matches the banded UILT of 34.5 °C;
- **blacknose dace → "Dace" (*Phoxinus*; He 1986; Trudel & Boisclair 1994)**, whose CTM 29 °C matches the blacknose UILT of about 29 °C;
- **fallfish → no good match**: dace for juveniles and northern pikeminnow for adults, with the temperature curve adjusted.

### Inferences
- **Redbreast → bluegill.** Same genus, same body size and the same invertivorous diet. A northern redbreast that spawns at about 20 °C may justify lowering CTO from 27 to about 24–25 °C. That is a judgement, not a measurement.
- **Banded killifish → California killifish.** Same genus, a similar 5–10 cm body, and a CTM of 34 °C that matches the measured banded UILT. The plains killifish set has extreme allometry (CA 3.6, CB −1.16) and prairie-stream temperatures (CTM 43 °C), so avoid it.
- **Blacknose dace → *Phoxinus* "dace".** A small cyprinid measured in Quebec lakes (Trudel & Boisclair). Its CTM of 29 °C and RTM of 32 °C bracket the blacknose UILT of 28.8–29.9 °C. Note its high egestion fraction (FA 0.4).
- **Fallfish (no clean surrogate).**
  - Use the *Phoxinus* dace set below about 10 cm, where the diet is insects and zooplankton.
  - For adults, the northern pikeminnow set is the only large, partly piscivorous, coolwater cyprinid. Its CTO of 20.1 °C and CTL of 27 °C are colder than the fallfish preference of 22.3 °C. Its CQ of 0 and CK1/CK4 of 0.001/0.01 look unusual and should be checked against Petersen & Ward (1999) before use.
  - A pragmatic option is pikeminnow CA/CB with a CEQ-2 temperature curve of CTO ≈ 22–24 °C and CTM ≈ 29–30 °C, interpolated between dace and humpback chub. This is my construction and unvalidated.

### Gaps
- No published bioenergetics model exists, as far as this search found, for redbreast sunfish, fallfish, banded killifish or blacknose dace.
- Petersen & Ward (1999) was not opened to confirm the pikeminnow CEQ-3 values (CQ 0, CK1 0.001, CK4 0.01) as transcribed from the CSV.
