## Q1. White sucker in northern lakes: numbers, biomass, age structure, and abundance relative to perch

### Takeaway
- Direct lake estimates of white sucker numbers are few. Adult (spawner-sized) densities fall in the **~20–110 adults/ha** range, at **~15–35 kg/ha**:
  - **23 adults/ha** at 17.5 kg/ha in South Bay, Lake Huron.
  - **68–109 adults/ha** in sucker-dominated Big Bear Lake, MI, where suckers were 78% of netted fish biomass.
  - **14.7 kg/ha** in soft-water Nebish Lake, WI.
- Survival of large adults is about **70–75%/yr**, so the adult stock spans many year classes.
- Juveniles are rarely caught, so true age-1+ totals are unknown.
- In soft, unproductive lakes, suckers can rival or exceed perch biomass. Removing them raised perch recruitment many-fold.

### Cited Findings
- **Big Bear Lake, Otsego Co., Michigan [MI]** — [Schneider & Crowe 1980, Fisheries Research Report 1887](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1887.pdf) (primary; text layer read)
  - **The lake and the method.**
    - 142 ha (350 acres), max depth 11 m, mean 4.9 m.
    - Alkalinity 45–98 ppm (moderately hard), Secchi ~4 m, seepage lake.
    - Spring trap-net Schnabel mark–recapture, 1940–56. The pots had 64-mm stretch mesh, so the gear "captured only the larger fish, referred to in this report as 'adults'".
    - The authors call the estimates "relative indices of adult fish density" because of gear selectivity.
  - **Sucker numbers and biomass.**
    - "Between 9,700 and 15,400 adult suckers were estimated to be present in 1940-42", = **68–109 adults/ha** (derived).
    - The Schnabel table gives 9,699 (1940), 15,423 (summer 1941) and 10,480 (1942). In spring 1943 a DeLury estimate gave 5,600, and then **5,778 (>90%) were removed**.
    - Suckers were **78% by weight** of netted fish in 1940–41.
    - "The standing crop of all nettable fish (exclusive of yellow perch and the subadults of all species) was estimated at 35-42 kg per hectare". That puts suckers at **≈ 27–33 kg/ha** (derived: 78% of 35–42).
    - In the 1955 re-removal, 10,732 adults were taken, printed as "about 36 kg per acre". That is ≈ 76 fish/ha (derived). If "per acre" is correct it is ≈ 89 kg/ha, about 1.2 kg per fish; the unit may be a misprint for per hectare.
    - Recovery took 8 years after the first removal and 3 years after the second.
  - **Growth and density.** Age-III suckers averaged **342 mm** at high density (1940–43) and **418 mm** after thinning (1944–52). The 1940 stock was "dense, slow-growing".
  - **Effect on perch.** After removal, the angler catch of yellow perch rose "from about 500 per year to over 12,000", and rock bass rose about 4-fold. The authors judged "the increases in perch and rock bass stocks were achieved more by improved recruitment than by improved growth".
  - **When removal works.** The authors list the conditions favouring sucker removal, beginning with "relatively unproductive lakes where suckers comprise more than 50% of the fish biomass".
- **Nebish Lake, Vilas Co., Wisconsin [WI]** (soft water; full rotenone census, October 1966): white sucker **13.1 lb/acre = 14.7 kg/ha** (derived), **6.2% of all fish biomass**. The number column is unreadable (OCR prints 0.9/acre, which would imply impossible 15-lb suckers). The estimate itself rests on an assumed 21% recovery rate. — [Kempinger & Christenson 1978, Wisconsin DNR Research Report 96](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/EIQCHSPXF35AK8O/E/file-19978.pdf) (primary, OCR)
- **South Bay, Lake Huron [GL/ON]** — [Coble 1967, J. Fish. Res. Board Can. 24 (via cdnsciencepub record)](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/f67-173) (via search summary)
  - In 1956 the adult population was "about 160,000 adult white suckers, or 23 fish per hectare or 17.5 kg/hectare, for the part of the bay less than 36 m deep". Mean adult weight ≈ 0.76 kg (derived).
  - "Annual survival rate of suckers larger than approximately 38 cm appeared to be about 70–75%", decreasing with size.
- **23 Ontario lakes [ON]** — [Chen & Harvey 1995, TAFS 124:262–271](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/growth-abundance-and-food-supply-of-white-sucker/) (abstract)
  - Population sizes came from Petersen mark–recapture or CPUE. Suckers in lakes with abundant littoral chironomids and **low sucker density** grew faster.
  - Population size was **negatively correlated with benthos abundance**: "the first evidence that white sucker growth may be constrained by both population density and food supply".
  - The abstract gives no per-ha values.
- **ELA Lake 223 [ON]** (acidified, pH 6.8 → 5.0): white sucker **recruitment failed in 1981–85**. — [Bagley et al., ESA 2016 abstract](https://eco.confex.com/eco/2016/webprogram/Paper62024.html). The population numbers are in [Chalanchuk 1997, Can. Tech. Rep. 2138](https://publications.gc.ca/site/eng/422311/publication.html), which was not read.
- **Cross-reference** (prior note): Marsh Lake, MI (soft water, ~6 ppm) held **47 lb/acre (≈ 53 kg/ha) of sucker** alongside 5 lb/acre of smallmouth.

### Inferences
- **Adult density.** For a soft, unproductive lake where suckers are common but not dominant, a defensible adult density is **25–70 adults/ha at 0.5–1.0 kg each (≈ 15–35 kg/ha)**. Big Bear's 68–109/ha is the sucker-dominated upper end.
- **Juveniles.** Fish of 7.5–30 cm (ages 1–4) add an uncertain amount. The adult survival of 70–75% and recruitment failures that run several years imply a stock spread over 10+ age classes. A juvenile:adult ratio of about 0.5–1.5 by number (≈ 15–70 juveniles/ha) is my guess. Nets and the trap-net studies under-sample juveniles.
- **Current census.** The design's **30/ha (≥7.5 cm) is low** against every estimate here. A more defensible value is **≈ 50–100/ha ≥7.5 cm (≈ 30,000–65,000 fish over 643 ha), at 15–30 kg/ha**. That also matches the EMAP adult share of ~7%.
- **Suckers versus perch.**
  - By number, perch (yellow plus white) outnumber suckers 10–30:1 in these lakes.
  - By biomass, suckers can match or exceed perch in soft, unproductive lakes: Big Bear 78%, Marsh Lake 90%, and Nebish 14.7 kg/ha of sucker against 5.6 kg/ha of age-1+ yellow perch.
  - Sucker density should feed back on perch recruitment through the shared benthos (Big Bear and Wilson Lake).

### Gaps
- Chen & Harvey (1995, 1999) give the per-lake densities only in tables I could not read; the alr-journal.org abstract page for the 1999 paper returned **HTTP 403**. Trippel & Harvey (1987) was not found online.
- The ELA Lake 223 sucker counts (DFO technical reports) and the Quebec Laurentian-lake sucker data (Magnan's brook-trout lakes) were not retrieved.
- No Maine, New Brunswick or Nova Scotia sucker density was found.
- No source gives sucker age-1 to age-3 numbers per ha.

## Q2. White perch in northern lakes: density, biomass, age and size structure, sizes at age in Maine

### Takeaway
- **No mark–recapture, rotenone or hydroacoustic per-ha estimate was found for white perch in any northern inland lake.** The only northern per-ha numbers are open-water trawl densities from western Lake Erie [GL]:
  - age-0 white perch about **1,600–3,000/ha** in fall;
  - "forage-sized" fish about **390/ha**.
- Size and age data from northern lakes show:
  - Lake Winnipesaukee (NH) fish reach 200 mm in ~3 years.
  - Maine fish are ~20–25 cm at age 4.
  - Maximum ages are 7 years (Hudson) to 17 (New Brunswick), with most fish living about 6.
- The per-ha range used in the design (≈ 400–1,500/ha) therefore still rests on southern-reservoir analogues plus the Maine/EMAP evidence that white perch are the most numerous adult fish.

### Cited Findings
- **Western Lake Erie [GL]**, bottom-trawl forage survey, 2019 — [Lake Erie Forage Task Group report, March 2020](https://www.glfc.org/pubs/lake_committees/erie/FTG_docs/annual_reports/FTG_report_2020.pdf) (via search summary)
  - Age-0 white perch **1,573/ha**, half the ten-year mean of **2,961/ha**.
  - "Yellow Perch (1,291/ha) and White Perch (389.1/ha) were the most abundant forage sized fish".
- **Lake Winnipesaukee, New Hampshire [NE]**, 2016 assessment — [NH Fish & Game](https://www.wildlife.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt746/files/inline-documents/sonh/assessment-white-perch-winnipesaukee-2016.pdf) (via search summary; the PDF returned **HTTP 403** to the fetch tool)
  - White perch averaged **2.93 years to reach 200 mm**.
  - PSD = 100, "an unbalanced population with an extreme number of large fish".
  - The agency "currently has limited data on White Perch populations".
- **Maine [ME]** (prior note, MDIFW species page): an 8–10 in (20–25 cm) fish weighs ~0.45 lb (~200 g) and is about 4 years old. A 6-year-old can be 6–12 in. Lifespan is ~5–7 years.
- **New Brunswick [NB]**: white perch "can live to be 17 years old, though most live only six"; mainly in southwestern NB. — [NB government species sheet](https://www.mynbpropertyassessment.ca/content/dam/gnb/Departments/nr-rn/pdf/en/Fish/WhitePerch.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Hudson River, New York [NE]**: maximum age 7 for both sexes; "most of the growth occurred in the first 3 years"; mature by the second year. — [Bath & O'Connor 1982, Fishery Bulletin 80(3)](https://fisherybulletin.nmfs.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-content/1982/803/bath.pdf) (via search summary)
- **Mortality (estuarine, [S])**: in the York River, Virginia, males age III+ died at about **59%/yr**; older James River males at about **70%/yr**. — [W&M thesis 1971](https://scholarworks.wm.edu/items/66b2bc88-5bac-4f4e-b62d-f4a3d762d5b8) (via search summary)
- **Western Lake Erie invasion**: YOY abundance varied "nearly 200 fold", with increasingly strong year classes in 1980–84. — [Schaeffer & Margraf 1986, J. Great Lakes Res.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0380133086717073) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Mean weight and numbers.** With Maine growth (≈ 12 cm at age 2, 16–18 cm at age 3, 20–25 cm at age 4) and survival of 30–45%/yr, a population dominated by ages 2–4 has a mean weight of ≈ 40–80 g for fish ≥7.5 cm. At the design's 20–30 kg/ha that is **≈ 300–700 fish/ha**. The design's **800/ha** implies a stunted mean of ~30 g, which fits the "large numbers of undersized fish" description of crowded Maine lakes. Both are defensible; this note cannot narrow them further.
- **Upper bound from yellow perch.** Lake Erie's ~390 forage-sized white perch/ha sits alongside ~1,290 yellow perch/ha in the same trawls. Inland Maine nets instead show adult white perch outnumbering adult yellow perch by ~7:1 (EMAP medians 42% vs 6%). Applied to the soft-water yellow perch densities (230–240/ha ≥75 mm; Q7), that ratio gives **~1,600–1,700 white perch/ha**. Treat it as an upper bound: Crystal's "adults" include every perch ≥75 mm, whereas EMAP "adults" are larger fish. It is a crude cross-check, not a measurement.
- **Size classes for the sim** (age 1+ in late summer; my synthesis):
  - ~35% at 7.5–15 cm (ages 1–2);
  - ~45% at 15–22 cm (ages 3–4);
  - ~20% above 22 cm (ages 5+).
  - Winnipesaukee-type fast growth shifts this toward larger fish; crowding shifts it toward 10–18 cm.

### Gaps
- No inland northern per-ha white perch estimate was found. Searched: Lake Champlain, Oneida, Bay of Quinte, Hamilton Harbour, Quebec, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and Maine.
- The NH Winnipesaukee report (HTTP 403) and the MDIFW white perch plan (403 in the prior pass) were not readable.
- University of Maine Digital Commons has been retired ("sunset June 30, 2026"), so Maine theses were not reachable.

## Q3. Chain pickerel: population estimates and numbers by size class

### Takeaway
- **No mark–recapture or rotenone density for chain pickerel was found anywhere in the Northeast or Atlantic Canada.**
- The one per-ha value found is a southern blackwater impoundment [S]: Robinson Impoundment, South Carolina, cove rotenone, **≈ 94 chain pickerel/ha and ≈ 12.8 kg/ha** (all sizes; OCR). Pickerel were "major contributors to the total biomass".
- Northern growth and longevity data allow a size split: New Jersey fish reach 157 mm at age 1, 372 mm at 3 and 513 mm at 5, with a maximum age of 9.
- Together with the grass-pickerel proxies (22–275/ha; prior note) and Maine's harvest objective, a Hot Brook value of **≈ 10–40 pickerel ≥7.5 cm per ha** remains a reasoned estimate, not a measurement.

### Cited Findings
- **Robinson Impoundment, South Carolina [S]** (911 ha, coastal-plain cooling impoundment; 1/4-in block nets; rotenone at 2 ppm in three coves, 1974–75; mean of 6 samples) — [Carolina Power & Light report in NRC docket ML022890248, §4.3](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0228/ML022890248.pdf) (primary; table OCR)
  - "Standing crop estimates ranged from 29.3 kg/ha in the upper impoundment cove during 1975 to a high of 139.8 kg/ha in the lower impoundment cove". "During both years of sampling, chain pickerel were major contributors to the total biomass."
  - Table 4.3.2 reads (OCR) **chain pickerel 94/ha and 12,805 g/ha**, a mean of ≈ 136 g including young (derived), and **redfin pickerel 30/ha and 833 g/ha**.
  - The table compares the impoundment with North Carolina blackwater lakes (Singletary, Waccamaw, Alligator) where "bluegill, warmouth, chain pickerel, and largemouth bass [were] frequently occurring as dominant species".
  - Cove rotenone over-represents littoral species relative to the whole lake.
- **New Jersey [NE-adjacent, Mid-Atlantic]**, summary of NJ lake inventories 1990–95 — [NJ Division of Fish & Wildlife chain pickerel profile](https://dep.nj.gov/njfw/wp-content/uploads/njfw/Chain-Pickerel.pdf) (primary)
  - Length at age: **I 157, II 256, III 372, IV 423, V 513, VI 638 mm**.
  - "Adults typically 350-500 mm up to a maximum 990 mm. Maximum longevity is 9 years of age."
  - Males and females mature at 3–5 years. Spawning runs February–May at 8–11 °C in shallows to 3 m over vegetation. Females "appear to grow faster and live longer".
- **Maine [ME]** (prior note, MDIFW Chain Pickerel Assessment):
  - Maturity at age 3–4 and 13–16 in. YOY are 4–5 in by fall.
  - Harvest objective ~0.35 lb/acre/yr; angled fish average 16.2 in and 1.1 lb.
  - "moderate to high density populations" in shallow weedy water.
- **Grass pickerel proxies [WI/MI]** (prior note): 22–97/ha (Pleasant Lake, WI), 250–275/ha in two Michigan ponds, and 62/ha by rotenone in a Michigan lake, **81% of them YOY**.

### Inferences
- **Size split.** Maine growth is slower than New Jersey's: YOY are 10–13 cm by fall, and fish mature at 33–41 cm at age 3–4. Using a Maine-like split and survival of ~40–55%/yr (an assumption; no measured value found):

  | Size class (age) | Fish/ha |
  |---|---|
  | 7.5–25 cm (age 0+ in fall, age 1) | ≈ 8–20 |
  | 25–38 cm (ages 2–3) | ≈ 4–10 |
  | ≥38 cm (ages 4–9) | ≈ 2–6 |

- **Biomass check.** That totals **≈ 15–35/ha and ≈ 4–10 kg/ha**, close to the design's 20/ha. Over 643 ha it is ≈ 10,000–22,000 pickerel ≥7.5 cm, of which ≈ 1,300–3,900 are ≥38 cm (derived).
- **Southern ceiling.** Robinson's 94/ha (12.8 kg/ha) is a pickerel-rich southern blackwater system sampled in littoral coves. Treat it as a ceiling, not a target.

### Gaps
- No pickerel density or annual-mortality estimate was found for New England, New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey or Nova Scotia.
- The Pennsylvania Fish & Boat chain pickerel plan link redirected to a dead pa.gov page.
- The older New York and Connecticut life-history studies (Raney 1942; Foote & Blake 1945; Underhill 1949) were seen only as citations.

## Q4. Smallmouth bass in small northern lakes: adults and age-1+ per ha, numbers by age, catch-and-release

### Takeaway
- Two soft-water northern Wisconsin lakes give the best numbers so far.
- **Pallette Lake** (71 ha, alkalinity 6 ppm, clear; liberal harvest 1958–84), cohort analysis:
  - age I ≈ **12.8/ha**;
  - ages II–V ≈ **11.5/ha**;
  - ages III–V ≈ **4.3/ha**.
- **Nebish Lake** (38 ha, alkalinity 15–19):
  - age I+ ≈ **40/ha** at ≈ 3.4 kg/ha in the 1966 rotenone census;
  - ages III–V averaged **33/ha** by mark–recapture later (Serns 1984).
- **Big Bear, MI**: adults 2–9/ha.
- **Mortality.** Under heavy harvest total annual mortality averaged **71%**, with natural mortality M = 0.58 (≈ 56% natural survival). Under catch-and-release (Pallette, later) adult survival was **80–83%**.
- So an unharvested Hot Brook population should hold **more and older adults per recruit** than these harvested benchmarks.

### Cited Findings
- **Pallette Lake, Vilas Co., Wisconsin [WI]** — [Hoff & Serns 1990, Wisconsin DNR Technical Bulletin 171](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/XHJ2UPT7X2J368V/E/file-266d1.pdf) (primary; text layer read)
  - **The lake.** "Pallette Lake is a 176-acre infertile, clear-water, seepage lake with a total alkalinity of 6 ppm, Secchi disc transparency of approximately 16 ft". There were no season, bag or size limits on bass in 1958–84.
  - **Harvest.** Annual harvest averaged **234 fish (1.3/acre) and 82 lb (0.5 lb/acre)**, with a mean harvested length of 9.0 in.
  - **Mortality.**
    - "Total instantaneous and annual mortality rates for adult smallmouth bass averaged 1.332 and 0.709". Catch-curve A ranged 0.38–0.91 by year class.
    - The cohort analysis used **M = 0.581 and F = 0.751**.
  - **Spring numbers at age** (cohort analysis, 1958–79 cohorts):

    | Age | Mean number in lake | Per ha (derived) |
    |---|---|---|
    | I | ≈ 912 (range 179–2,161; my mean of the 22 tabled values) | ≈ 12.8 (2.5–30) |
    | II | 515 ± 325 | 7.2 |
    | III | 229 ± 175 | 3.2 |
    | IV | 62 ± 58 | 0.9 |
    | V | 15 | 0.2 |

  - **Totals.** "Population size of age groups II-V and III-V from 1963-79 was 820 (4.7/acre) and 305 (1.7/acre)" = **11.6/ha and 4.2/ha**.
  - **Harvest share.** About 23% of each cohort alive at age I was harvested by age V.
  - **Recruitment.** There was no stock–recruit relationship for ages III–V versus cohort size.
- **Nebish Lake, Vilas Co., Wisconsin [WI]** (94 acres = 38 ha, max 15 m, seepage, alkalinity 15–19 ppm, TP 0.02 ppm in 1960; liberal fishing and spearfishing 1946–66) — [Kempinger & Christenson 1978, WDNR Research Report 96](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/EIQCHSPXF35AK8O/E/file-19978.pdf) (primary, OCR)
  - Rotenone on 3 October 1966, after fin-clip marking.
  - Smallmouth by size: "4.3-7.2, 7.3-8.9 inches, and 9.0 inches and over, approximating Age Groups I, II, and III and over, respectively, were 1,938, 255, and 250, or 10.9, 2.7, and 2.6 fish/acre" = **27, 6.7 and 6.4/ha**.
  - Smallmouth standing crop **6.1 lb/acre**, including YOY (≈ 3.1 lb); age I+ ≈ 3.0 lb/acre ≈ 3.4 kg/ha (derived).
- **Nebish after restocking [WI]**: the density of age III–V smallmouth "determined by mark-recapture population estimates, averaged 13.5/acre (Serns 1984)" = **33/ha**. Hoff & Serns attribute Nebish's slower growth to that higher density. — [Hoff & Serns 1990](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/XHJ2UPT7X2J368V/E/file-266d1.pdf) (primary). A WDNR summary also gives "16.2 ± 1.8 smallmouth bass/acre since 1972" (≈ 40/ha). — [UW digital collections, Nebish reports](https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/A7CNPPHSMHYQUB8V) (via search summary; OCR, unverified)
- **Big Bear Lake, Michigan [MI]** (142 ha; trap-net Schnabel, adult-sized only): smallmouth **1,333 (1940), 304 (1941), 720 (1942), 294 (1943)** = **2.1–9.4/ha** (derived). Largemouth were 584–2,987 (4–21/ha). Neither bass benefited from sucker removal. — [Schneider & Crowe 1980](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1887.pdf) (primary; table OCR)
- **Lake Opeongo, Ontario [ON]** (58-year creel and mark–recapture reconstruction): adults were defined as "all fish aged five years and over", and age at 50% maturity was ≤ 5 years. Growth of ages 1–4 is "higher when summers are warmer and when population abundance is reduced". The absolute abundances appear only in a figure, not in the text. — [Shuter & Ridgway 2002, AFS Symp. 31](https://www.harkness.ca/PDFs/Contributions%202000s/Shuter%20Ridgway%202002%20Bass%20in%20Time%20and%20Space.pdf) (primary)
- **Catch-and-release (prior note)**: at Pallette Lake in 2015–25 (Shaw et al., NAJFM), annual survival was **80% (males) and 83% (females)**, and each bass was caught 3–7 times a year.
- **Little Moose Lake, Adirondacks [NE]** (prior note): 47,682 bass removed over 6 years from 271 ha. The removal *raised* total numbers by releasing immature fish. — [Zipkin et al. 2008, CJFAS (USGS record)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70030237) (USGS page returned HTTP 500)

### Inferences
- **Projection without harvest** (derived, my construction): take Pallette's age-I recruitment (~13/ha), natural survival ~0.56 from age I to III, and the catch-and-release adult survival ~0.80 thereafter. That gives roughly:

  | Age | Fish/ha |
  |---|---|
  | I | 13 |
  | II | 7 |
  | III | 4 |
  | IV | 3.3 |
  | V | 2.6 |
  | VI | 2.1 |
  | VII+ | ~8 (summed geometric tail) |

  That totals **≈ 40 age-1+ fish/ha, of which ≈ 16/ha are age 4+ (≈ ≥ 28–30 cm in Maine)**. Nebish's 33–40/ha at higher density corroborates the order of magnitude.
- **Design census.** The current **40/ha (≥7.5 cm)** fits. Whole-lake: ≈ 26,000 bass ≥7.5 cm over 643 ha, of which **≈ 6,000–10,000 are adults** (derived).
- **Catch-and-release.** It should mainly lengthen the age tail (more fish ≥ 40 cm). It should not much raise age-1 to age-3 numbers, which are set by first-winter survival and density-dependent growth (Opeongo; Little Moose overcompensation).

### Gaps
- No Maine, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia or Quebec smallmouth density was found.
- The Opeongo absolute numbers are only in a figure. Zipkin et al. (2008) was not readable (USGS 500).
- Nebish's later (1970s) per-age tables were not extracted.

## Q5. Pumpkinseed and brown bullhead in soft-water northern lakes

### Takeaway
- Soft-water estimates are scarce and low:
  - **Pumpkinseed** were only **~12–22/ha (0.3 lb/acre)** in soft Nebish Lake. Netted "adults" were **7–28/ha** in Big Bear (moderately hard). The prior Escanaba range (alkalinity 20) was 27–437/ha at 7–33 kg/ha.
  - **No soft-water brown bullhead density was found.** Nebish's black bullheads had disappeared by 1951. The only per-ha bullhead values remain Moe Pond (260/ha) and Cassidy (48/ha) from the prior note.

### Cited Findings
- **Nebish Lake, WI [WI]** (soft; October rotenone census) — [Kempinger & Christenson 1978](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/EIQCHSPXF35AK8O/E/file-19978.pdf) (primary, OCR)
  - Pumpkinseed ≈ 5–9/acre (≈ 12–22/ha; the OCR digit is unclear, bounded by the table's column sums) at **0.3 lb/acre (0.34 kg/ha)**.
  - Bluegill 50.2/acre (124/ha) at 2.5 lb/acre; green sunfish 11.5/acre at 0.6 lb; rock bass 56.5/acre (140/ha) at 5.9 lb/acre.
  - Black bullhead: "The last black bullheads and black crappies were recorded in 1951: thus in all probability, these 2 species had disappeared prior to 1966."
  - Nebish is deep (15 m) with "a narrow littoral zone", so littoral sunfish were scarce.
- **Big Bear Lake, MI [MI]** (trap-net Schnabel, adult sizes) — [Schneider & Crowe 1980](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1887.pdf) (primary; table OCR)
  - Pumpkinseed 1,049–3,926 adults (**7.4–27.6/ha**, derived), with 1,325 (1940), 3,926 (1941), 2,486 (1942), 2,326 (1955) and 2,415 (1956).
  - Bullheads were present but not estimated. Angler bullhead catches rose after sucker removal.
- **Montana pond population [W]** (introduced, flagged): Petersen estimate of **142.8 lb/acre (160 kg/ha) of pumpkinseed >2.7 in**, and annual survival of age III–VIII pumpkinseed of **51%**. — [Montana State thesis](https://scholarworks.montana.edu/bitstreams/df43480f-3cfa-4bcf-899b-e88b05714181/download) (via search summary)
- **Ontario [ON]**: Fox studied 27 pumpkinseed populations (funnel traps, growth versus density), including small beaver-pond populations beside Lake Opinicon. — [QUBS project page](https://research.qubs.ca/projects/growth-density-and-interspecific-influences-on-pumpkinseed-sunfish-life-histories). No per-ha values are given.

### Inferences
- **Pumpkinseed.** Hot Brook is shallow and weedy, unlike Nebish, but soft and coloured. A pumpkinseed density between Nebish and Escanaba, **≈ 50–150/ha ≥7.5 cm (≈ 2–6 kg/ha)**, is better supported than the design's 215/ha. It also matches the EMAP adult share of 5%.
- **Brown bullhead.** Keep the design's 140/ha only if Hot Brook's soft mud and weed beds are judged Moe Pond-like. **≈ 50–150/ha (≈ 5–14 kg/ha)** spans the two measured lakes.
- **Age structure.** Bullhead survival of ~48%/yr (Moe Pond) puts about half the age-1+ fish at age 1–2 (≈ 10–20 cm).

### Gaps
- No pumpkinseed or bullhead per-ha estimate was found from the Ontario Shield, Adirondacks, Maine or Nova Scotia.
- The Fox (1994) and Keast Lake Opinicon papers' density values were not reachable.

## Q6. Fallfish in lakes and near inlets: density or relative abundance with numbers

### Takeaway
- **No per-area fallfish density was found for any lake or stream.**
- The only numeric abundance found here is from big Maine rivers. Boat electrofishing counted **>50–100 fallfish/km** (Kennebec), **>200/km** (Androscoggin, mostly YOY) and **150/km** (a Sebasticook impoundment). Fallfish were among the three or four most numerous species in the Kennebec–Androscoggin system.
- Lake shares remain the EMAP values (0–23% of adults; prior note).

### Cited Findings
- **Kennebec, Androscoggin and Sebasticook rivers, Maine/NH [ME]** (2002–03; 1.0-km boat-electrofishing sites) — [Midwest Biodiversity Institute 2006, Tech. Rep. MBI/12-05-1](http://cybrary.friendsofmerrymeetingbay.org/FishWildlife/MAINE.RIVERS.REPORT_FINAL.09.01.2006.pdf) (primary; text layer read)
  - "Smallmouth bass … was the numerically most abundant species followed by American eel …, fallfish …, redbreast sunfish …, and white sucker. White sucker predominated in terms of biomass."
  - Kennebec: fallfish "most numerous between Solon and Waterville occurring mostly as young-of-year and juveniles … Peak abundance (>50-100 individuals/km)".
  - Androscoggin: "Peak abundances of >200 individuals/km were attributable to a high proportion of young-of-year".
  - Sebasticook: "the greatest number (150 individuals/km) occurring in the Benton Falls impoundment".
  - "Young-of-year and juvenile life stages comprised their numbers in modified habitats while largest adults occurred in areas of poor water quality."
  - "Smallmouth bass and fallfish [predominated by biomass] in the impounded segments" of the upper mainstem.
- **Salmon River, New York [NE]**: fallfish are "the most abundant native species in the Salmon River". — [USGS publication 70035960](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70035960) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Brook and mouths.** Fallfish are a dominant species where flowing water and gravel exist, and YOY dominate counts. In Hot Brook the connecting brook and the two inlet/outlet mouths should hold the bulk of them.
- **Lake-wide.** Spread over the lakes, **≈ 25–100/ha ≥7.5 cm** remains my estimate (prior note: 3–10% of the census). That is ≈ 16,000–64,000 over 643 ha, concentrated near flowing water.
- **Brook density.** The river counts (50–200/km of bank) cannot be turned into a per-ha brook density without the sampled width, so they are not used numerically.

### Gaps
- No fallfish mark–recapture or depletion density was found (per m², per 100 m, per ha) for any stream or lake.
- The Victor & Brothers (1982) and Massachusetts eight-population studies were not retrieved. The HSI full text was unreachable.

## Q7. Yellow perch in soft-water and coloured northern lakes: numbers by age and size, share ≥15 cm

### Takeaway
- Two soft-water Wisconsin censuses agree closely on adult density:
  - **Crystal Lake** (46 ha, conductivity 13 µS/cm): **≈ 240 adults/ha (≥75 mm; mean 157 mm, 40 g; ≈ 9.6 kg/ha)** by spring mark–recapture.
  - **Nebish Lake**: **≈ 230 age-1+/ha (mean ≈ 24 g; 5.6 kg/ha)** plus **≈ 7,500 YOY/ha (≈ 3.6 g; 27 kg/ha)** in early October.
- In clear, oligotrophic water, summer hydroacoustics *under*-estimated adult perch 4–8.5-fold because most adults stay littoral.
- With the prior note's Escanaba range (≥15 cm: 49–573/ha), a soft-water lake's age-1+ perch plausibly run **≈ 200–600/ha**, with **≈ 20–50% ≥15 cm** where growth is not stunted.

### Cited Findings
- **Crystal Lake, Vilas Co., Wisconsin [WI]** — [Mrnak et al. 2021, NAJFM, doi 10.1002/nafm.10605](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10316443) (primary; text layer read)
  - "An oligotrophic, 46-ha seepage lake … maximum depth of 20.5 m", Secchi 6 to >8 m, "low water conductivity (13 µS)". The fish assemblage is "dominated by invasive Rainbow Smelt … and Yellow Perch".
  - Spring 2020 mini-fyke Chapman mark–recapture: "Mean PE … was 11,051 adult Yellow Perch" = **≈ 240/ha** (derived).
  - Adults were "75 mm TL since all individuals of that length expressed gametes". Spring fish averaged **156.8 ± 35.9 mm and 39.9 ± 29.9 g** (≈ 9.6 kg/ha, derived).
  - Summer hydroacoustic estimates were 4.0–8.5 times lower; "approximately 22% of adult Yellow Perch selected for pelagic habitats postspawn".
- **Nebish Lake, WI [WI]** (October 1966 rotenone + Petersen) — [Kempinger & Christenson 1978](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/EIQCHSPXF35AK8O/E/file-19978.pdf) (primary, OCR)
  - "The population included 8,469 and 394 fish of Age Groups I and II and over, respectively, which collectively represented 93.4 fish/acre" = **230/ha**. Age II+ was only **4% of age-1+ fish** (derived: 394 / 8,863).
  - Age I+ biomass 5.0 lb/acre; YOY **3,034.5/acre (≈ 7,500/ha) at 24.0 lb/acre**; total perch 29.0 lb/acre (13.8% of all fish).
  - The YOY estimate rests on 16 recaptures of 2,250 marked fish.
  - Through 1946–66 perch were **57% of the angler catch**.
- **Big Bear Lake, MI [MI]**: perch were not sampled by the nets. Angler catch rose from ~500 to 12,000–15,000 a year after sucker removal, by recruitment rather than growth. — [Schneider & Crowe 1980](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1887.pdf) (primary)
- **Nebish restocked population** (1967–76): "yellow perch became the more abundant of the 2 species by number and by biomass … the 1969 year class was the largest of the decade, with overcrowding apparently causing severe mortality". The pre-treatment estimate was 855 perch in 1964, "dominated by one year class". — [WDNR Report 119 (Kempinger, Forbes, Serns & Snow 1982), UW digital collections](https://search.library.wisc.edu/digital/A7CNPPHSMHYQUB8V) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **Design census.** Soft, clear lakes (Crystal, Nebish) hold ~230–240 age-1+ perch/ha. Hot Brook is shallower and weedier, and the EMAP nets show juveniles there far outnumbering adults. A Hot Brook value of **≈ 300–700/ha ≥7.5 cm** is better supported than the design's **860/ha**, which would need Cassidy-type (hard-water, stunted) densities.
- **Size split** (late summer, for the census):
  - 55–70% at 7.5–12 cm (age 1);
  - 20–30% at 12–15 cm (age 2);
  - 10–20% ≥15 cm (age 3+).
  - The ≥15 cm share is lower than Crystal's mean of 157 mm suggests, because Hot Brook perch are reported small (owner) and EMAP adult yellow perch are scarce.
- **Young of year.** Early-autumn YOY can outnumber age-1+ perch 30:1 (Nebish 7,500 vs 230/ha). That supports holding YOY as `world.fry` schools rather than individuals.

### Gaps
- No yellow perch per-ha estimate was found for a *coloured* (humic) northern lake. ELA, Sudbury and Rouyn-Noranda studies were sought but not reached.
- Escanaba age-specific numbers are in the prior note only as ≥15 cm totals.

## Q8. Whole-community totals in soft-water northern lakes and scaling with TP or alkalinity

### Takeaway
- **Nebish Lake** (soft, oligotrophic, TP ≈ 20 µg/L), the most complete northern census found, held:
  - **236 kg/ha of fish of all sizes**, of which 152 kg/ha were small minnows (an uncertain estimate);
  - **≈ 84 kg/ha of all other fish**;
  - only **≈ 640 age-1+ non-minnow fish/ha at ≈ 53 kg/ha**.
- **Big Bear's** nettable adults were 35–42 kg/ha.
- **Across lakes**, fish biomass scales with TP:
  - TP is the best single predictor of biomass (r² 0.75) and yield (r² 0.84) in Hanson & Leggett 1982.
  - Lake community production averages **82 kg/ha/yr** over 22 lakes (Randall et al. 1995).
  - In Florida, TP explains only ~25% of the variance around a mean of 131 kg/ha.
- For Hot Brook (TP 9–10 µg/L, alkalinity 5, but shallow), **≈ 50–90 kg/ha of fish ≥7.5 cm** is better supported than the design's ~100 kg/ha.

### Cited Findings
- **Nebish Lake, Wisconsin [WI]** (94 acres; seepage; alkalinity 15–19 ppm; TP 0.02 ppm, single May 1960 sample; conductance 33–54 µmhos) — [Kempinger & Christenson 1978](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/EIQCHSPXF35AK8O/E/file-19978.pdf) (primary, OCR)
  - **Method.** Rotenone 3 October 1966 + Petersen estimates from pre-marked fish. "Nebish Lake was not a 'problem lake', a type from which most of Wisconsin's standing crop data have originated."
  - **Total.** "The standing crop of fish was 210 lb/acre. Minnows alone made up 135 lb/acre". That is **236 and 152 kg/ha** (derived). The minnows were bluntnose minnow and mimic shiner: ≈ 33,800/acre ≈ 83,500/ha, estimated from an 11:1 minnow-to-perch-fingerling seine ratio. The authors flag this estimate as "open to question".
  - **Other fish** (lb/acre; fish/acre):

    | Species | lb/acre | Fish/acre |
    |---|---|---|
    | Yellow perch | 29.0 (incl. YOY 24.0) | — |
    | White sucker | 13.1 | — |
    | Walleye | 10.8 | 16.0 |
    | Northern pike | 6.5 | 6.8 |
    | Smallmouth | 6.1 | — |
    | Rock bass | 5.9 | 56.5 |
    | Bluegill | 2.5 | 50.2 |
    | Green sunfish | 0.6 | — |
    | Largemouth | 0.4 | — |
    | Pumpkinseed | 0.3 | — |
    | **Sum** | **75.2 (≈ 84 kg/ha)** | |

  - **Age-1+ non-minnow fish** (derived from the table): **≈ 260/acre (≈ 640/ha) and ≈ 47 lb/acre (≈ 53 kg/ha)**. The OCR sub-total of "3,933.3" fish/acre is inconsistent with the grand total of 37,334.3 and must be 3,533.3.
- **Big Bear Lake, Michigan [MI]**: "standing crop of all nettable fish (exclusive of yellow perch and the subadults of all species) was estimated at 35-42 kg per hectare". — [Schneider & Crowe 1980](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1801-RR1900/RR1887.pdf) (primary)
- **Michigan small lakes [MI]**: "lakes whose waters ranged from very soft to very hard … ranged from 10 to 194.5 pounds per acre" (≈ 11–218 kg/ha, derived). "The hard-water lakes were, in general, more productive than the soft-water lakes." — [Michigan DNR Research Report 1087](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/PDFS/DNRFishLibrary/ResearchReports/RR1001-RR1100/RR1087.pdf) (via search summary; the summary's own kg/ha conversion was wrong and is corrected here)
- **Hanson & Leggett 1982 [mixed]**, CJFAS 39:257–263 — [doi 10.1139/f82-036](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/f82-036) (abstract via search summary; the page returned **HTTP 403**)
  - "Total phosphorus concentration and macrobenthos biomass/mean depth were the best univariate predictors of fish yield (r² = 0.84 and r² = 0.48, respectively) and biomass (r² = 0.75 and r² = 0.83)", outperforming the MEI, TDS and mean depth.
  - A secondary review reproduces a multiple regression "Y = 0.066 TP + 0.141 z + 0.013 TDS − 1.513 (n = 21, r = 0.98)" with units not stated. **Do not use it until it is checked.**
- **Randall, Minns & Kelso 1995**, CJFAS 52:631–643 — [OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/40EMMM3l) (abstract via search summary)
  - Mean community production was **82 kg·ha⁻¹·yr⁻¹ at 22 lakes** versus 273 at 55 river sites.
  - Rivers had ~14× the density and ~2× the biomass, with fish 7× smaller.
  - "Fish production was positively correlated with phosphorus in both lakes and rivers."
- **Downing & Plante 1993** (100 populations, 38 lakes): production rose with temperature, TP, chlorophyll, primary production and pH; "the morphoedaphic index is not a good predictor". — [UMN record](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/production-of-fish-populations-in-lakes/) (via search summary)
- **65 Florida lakes [S]**: "The standing crop of fish … averaged 131 kg·ha⁻¹ (range 7–683)". Correlations with TP, TN, chlorophyll and Secchi had r² of only 0.24–0.28. Kautz (1980) found **sport-fish** standing crops of **52, 89 and 65 kg/ha** in oligotrophic, meso-eutrophic and hypereutrophic Florida lakes. — [Bachmann et al. 1996, CJFAS 53:842–855](https://lake.wateratlas.usf.edu/upload/documents/f95-236.pdf) (primary)
- **Turkey Lakes, Algoma, Ontario [ON]** (soft Shield lakes; 8–11 species per lake):
  - "annual variation in total fish abundance is driven mainly by small species, especially northern redbelly dace", while biomass changes are driven by large species, "which remain relatively stable". — [ECCC Turkey Lakes fish page](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/turkey-lakes-watershed-study/site/fish-and-aquatic-biota.html) (primary; no numbers on the page)
  - Kelso & Johnson (1991, CJFAS 48:2523–2532) estimated community biomass and production in 19 small lakes. Lakes averaged 4.5 species, with ">75% of each lake's population" from fewer than 3 species. — [Turkey Lakes 1991 publication list](https://www.canada.ca/fr/environnement-changement-climatique/services/etude-bassin-lacs-turkey/publications/liste-documents-etude-1982-a-ce-jour/an-1991.html) (via search summary)
- **Cross-reference** (prior note):
  - Michigan "normal" lakes averaged ≈ 99 kg/ha.
  - Escanaba (alkalinity 20) sport fish were 21–131 kg/ha, or 600–1,080 sport fish/ha.
  - Cassidy (hard) held 5,470 fish ≥3 in per ha at 163 kg/ha.

### Inferences
- **Two soft-water anchors.** Age-1+ non-minnow fish run **≈ 640/ha and 53 kg/ha** (Nebish, deep, narrow littoral, TP ~20). Adults alone run 35–42 kg/ha (Big Bear, harder water).
- **Hot Brook total.** Hot Brook has half Nebish's TP but is far shallower; shallow lakes yield more fish per area. A total of **≈ 1,500–2,500 fish ≥7.5 cm per ha and ≈ 50–90 kg/ha** is my best synthesis. That is ≈ 1.0–1.6 million fish and ≈ 32–58 t over 643 ha (derived). The design's ~2,500/ha and ~100 kg/ha sit at the top of this range.
- **Small fish.** Nebish shows that small cyprinids and YOY can carry *more* biomass than all larger fish together. Hot Brook's minnow guild (killifish, dace, juvenile fallfish) plus YOY perch should carry a large fry/school biomass in late summer, held outside the ≥7.5 cm census.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved:
  - Hanson & Leggett's TP-only equation (cdnsciencepub 403);
  - Kelso & Johnson's per-lake kg/ha;
  - the ELA whole-lake community biomasses;
  - Adirondack ALSC numbers (CPUE only);
  - Rupp & DeRoche 1965 (Maine), which exceeds the fetch limit (prior note).
- No Maine, New Brunswick or Nova Scotia whole-community standing crop was found online.
