# Esocid first-year survival (helper report to pickerel_population_dynamics.md, 2026-10-18)

A research helper's findings, condensed. It found **no published first-year survival or young-of-year density for chain pickerel**; every
number below is northern pike (a few grass/mud pickerel and muskellunge). Tags: [primary] full text read, [secondary] cited in a document
read, [abstract], [snippet]. Z = −ln S; daily S = e^(−Z/d).

## Egg → swim-up
- Pike eggs on vegetation, Minnesota: 60–95 % survival (Franklin & Smith 1963, via Dahlberg 1979, Mar. Fish. Rev. 41(3)) [secondary];
  hatching parameter 0.735 in Arlinghaus et al. 2010 (Biol. Conserv. 143, Table 1) [primary].
- Forney 1968 (Oneida Lake marsh, via Dahlberg): eggs S 0.18 over 13 d (Z 0.128/d); yolk-sac S 0.572 over 8 d (0.072/d) [secondary].
- Michigan synthesis (Williams & Jacob 1971, MI DNR R&D Rep. 242): natural marsh hatch ~64 % [primary].
- Wright & Shoesmith 1988 (UK gravel pit): egg loss 9–10 %/day; stage-1 egg → hatched fry 3–6 % [abstract].
- Inskip 1982 (USFWS HSI, northern pike): incubation 26 d at 6 °C … 5 d at 16–20 °C [primary].
- Chain pickerel: hatch in 11–12 d at 8–11 °C (NJ DFW sheet; Maine pickerel plan) — no survival figures.

## Swim-up → first fall
- Carbine 1944 (Houghton Lake marsh, complete weir counts): egg → emigrant 0.07–0.44 % over ~95 d (~0.067/d) [primary].
- Ortonville rearing pond: 160,000 fry (5 May) → 362 pike (14 Oct): S 0.23 % over 162 d (0.038/d) [primary].
- Williams & Jacob 1971: fry → 1-in fingerling in a natural marsh 0.26 %; managed marsh fry → ~3 in 7 % over 50 d (0.053/d) [primary].
- Beyerle & Williams 1972 (Long Lake, MI): marked 2.5–3.6-in fingerlings, May → fall 6–63 % (mean 28 %; ~0.0085/d) [primary].
- Wright & Giles 1987 (ponds): density-dependent from July; May → December 0.06–11 % (Z ~0.010–0.035/d) [abstract].
- **Lake Hestholm, Denmark** (253 ha shallow, Falck-Rasmussen 2005): 0+ May → June S 0.66 (0.012/d), June → Sept S 0.27 (0.015/d),
  May → Sept 0.175 (0.014/d) [primary].
- Grimm (EIFAC, Netherlands): ~45 % of 0+ pike (12–22 cm) survived the same growing season [primary].

## First fall → age 1
- Lake Hestholm index: ~46 % December → March [primary].
- Tiger muskellunge overwinter (Koupal 1999): 0 % with < 1 % vegetation, 13–20 % with 20–35 % — herons the main cause [secondary].
- Managers' assumptions: 50 %/yr after the first fall (Beyerle & Williams); 65 %/yr (Inskip).

## Young-of-year density, fall
- Long Lake, MI: 7.0 natural 0+/ha lake-wide (18.3 with stocked) [primary]. UK pits: 6.6/ha sparse vegetation, 23/ha dense [snippet].
- Grimm, 4 Dutch waters: 0+ 1.2–52.5 kg per ha of VEGETATED area, ~44–1,110 per ha of vegetation [primary].
- Lake Hestholm: ~21/ha lake-wide in September (~117 per ha of vegetated littoral) [primary].

## Vegetation
- Juveniles and adults need 35–70 % macrophyte cover (Casselman & Lewis 1996, via Craig 2008) [secondary]; pike under ~14 cm stay in dense
  vegetation larger pike can't reach (Grimm 1994) [secondary]; 0+ biomass negatively correlated with larger-pike biomass, r −0.75 to −0.89
  (Grimm) [primary]; abundant vegetation 3.5× the 0+ density and 1.8× the survival (Wright 1990) [snippet].
- Maine: largemouth bass "serious predators" on young pickerel, confined to weed patches (MDIFW pickerel plan) [primary].

## Cannibalism
- Tanks: 1–4 % of fry turn cannibal at ~5 weeks (predator:prey ~2:1), causing 54–96 % of daily mortality (Giles et al. 1986) [snippet].
- Unconfined fry: only 1–2 % cannibals (Franklin & Smith 1963) [snippet]. Lake Hestholm: 0+ pike 52 % of fish prey in 1+ pike [primary].
- Grass pickerel: cannibalism rare — 9 of 387 stomachs (Crossman 1962); "infrequent" (COSEWIC) [snippet/primary].
- No quantified share of age-0/yearling mortality from cannibalism in a natural lake was found.

## Stock–recruitment
- Windermere (Edeline et al. 2008, PNAS 105:19792): Ricker, ln(R/SSB) slope −1.5 to −2.0 ×10⁻⁴ per spawner; + temperature [primary].
- Windermere IPM (Vindenes et al. 2016): egg → age 1 survival mean 0.00028 (0.0001–0.0007 in 50 of 53 years); adult survival ~0.67 [primary].
- Strong compensation: recruitment varied 16–27-fold with no stock–recruitment relation (Paxton et al. 2009) [abstract]; stocking fry
  into self-reproducing lakes didn't raise year classes (Hühn et al. 2014, 2023) [abstract].

## Takeaway (pike scale)
Egg → swim-up ~3–20 %; swim-up → fall ~0.1–30 % (~0.01–0.05/day) by stage, cannibals and cover; egg → age 1 ~1–7 ×10⁻⁴; fall 0+ ~7–40 per
ha lake-wide, capped ~20–50 kg per ha of vegetation with strong, overcompensating density dependence.
