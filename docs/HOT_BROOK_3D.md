# The Hot Brook lakes in 3-D — the whole-lake sim (design, in progress)

The 2-D sim (`src/`) became a slice through the real Upper and Lower Hot Brook Lake in stage A (`docs/HOT_BROOK_SIM.md`).
On 2026-10-15 the human asked for **both whole lakes and the brook in 3-D**, chose a **1/10 horizontal scale** and a
**1-hour year**. The 3-D sim is built alongside the 2-D one in `src/w3/` with its own gate (`test/w3.mjs`). The 2-D sim
keeps running, and its gate stays green, until the 3-D sim reaches parity and the app switches over.

## 1. The decisions and why they hang together

The sim compresses PHYSIOLOGY about 17,500× (a fish's day of growth in ~5 s; now ~10 s with the 1-hour year), but fish swim
at REAL speed (what the eye wants when the camera follows one). In the 2-D slice the lakes were squeezed ~90× along the
slice, which by accident gave a cruising fish a realistic distance per physiological day. In 3-D, three knobs trade off:
the area scale, the calendar, and how many real fish one brain stands for. The options shown to the human:

| | 1/5 length | **1/10 length (chosen)** | 1/25 length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | 26 ha | **6.5 ha** (Upper ~196 × 344 m) | 1 ha |
| Fish ≥ 7.5 cm at real density | ~69,000 | **~17,000** | ~2,800 |
| Brains (~3,000 budget) | everything in big groups | **big fish 1 each; sunfish/bullhead ~4; perch/shiner ~10** | every fish |
| Long trips | ~10× real speed | **~2.5–5× (1-h year)** | ~2× |

Depths, densities, plants and water are real in every option. Measured costs that set the budget (2-D engine, 2026-10-15):
the brain's forward pass is ~3 % of a step; senses and neighbour queries are ~45 % and the agent update ~25 %; ~40–60 µs
per fish per step today — the 3-D engine must reach ~8 µs (data-oriented arrays, senses at a lower rate, grid lookups).
The environment costs ~0.5 µs per wet cell per update.

## 2. The world (built: `src/w3/geometry.js`, data `src/w3/hotbrook3d.js`)

Generated by `tools/hotbrook/build_world3d.py` from the landscape model (`tools/hotbrook/data/hotbrook_model.json`: the
1949 MDIFW soundings fitted to today's shore, USGS 3DEP elevation, NLCD land cover, OSM outlines and brooks).
- Both lakes at 1/10 of their real horizontal size, TRUE depths: one 15-m model cell = one 1.5-m world cell, so nothing is
  resampled. Upper 3.75 ha, mean 3.05 m, max 5.52 m (its 18-ft hole in the north basin by the outlet), 44 % ≤ 3 m; Lower
  2.79 ha, mean 2.67 m, max 3.94 m, 57 % ≤ 3 m — the measured beds' own statistics.
- The connecting brook at 1/2 (77 m; Lower moves out along the brook's line to make room), 4 m wide, its profile from the
  angler: the deeper pool at its upper mouth (1.1 m), riffles (~0.35 m), a shallower run, the water falling 0.6 m.
- A 30-m band of real land around each lake (elevation, land cover): each land cell reads the real terrain under the nearer
  lake's placement, blended across the seam.
- Coordinates: x east, y north, metres, origin at the brook's upper mouth; z = depth below the local water surface. Water
  runs south → north: the inflow enters Upper's south end, the outlet leaves Lower's north end.
- Queries: `bodyAt`, `depthAt` (bilinear, continuous through the shore: negative on land = the ground's height),
  `shoreDist`, `inWater`, `downhill`, `levelAt`, `elevAt`, `coverAt`; `BODIES` (area, volume, mean, max, deepest point).
- The 1/10 scale steepens every real slope 10× (a 1° littoral slope becomes ~10°); the land's relief is kept true for
  now — the renderer may soften it.
- Open: the cove between Upper's west points (the angler's sunfish spot) is 2–3 m in the model, interpolated where the
  1949 soundings are sparse; the angler calls it shallow. A depth from their fishing app would settle it.

## 4. Plan (stages; each leaves both gates green)

1. **Geometry** — done (this section 2; `test/w3.mjs`).
2. **Design** — the research notes in `docs/research_notes/Whole lake 3D fish simulation/` (super-individual IBMs,
   shallow-lake 3-D physics, fish movement and groups, browser 3-D rendering) → the environment grid, the group rules, the
   movement model, the renderer; a four-season walk-through; shown to the human before building.
3. **Environment in 3-D** — columns × layers over the real beds; the 2-D sim's physics and chemistry per column, horizontal
   exchange, the brook's through-flow; closed budgets; ice.
4. **Fish in 3-D** — agents with a group size; 3-D movement, senses and brains; bioenergetics and feeding reused.
5. **Reproduction, young-of-year, recruitment into groups; immigration.**
6. **Plants, invertebrates, wildlife** (loon, frogs, turtles, dragonflies; the shore and the air — stage D's mechanisms).
7. **The renderer** (three.js): terrain, water above and below, fish, plants, sky, weather, seasons; the UI.
8. **Saves, fast-forward, performance, parity → the app switches to 3-D.**
